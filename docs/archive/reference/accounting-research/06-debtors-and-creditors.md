# 06 — Persons: debtors and creditors

**Questions (from the outline):** One running account per person vs separate receivable and payable. Advances paid to suppliers and received from customers. Retention money. Bad debts, bad debts recovered, provision for doubtful debts.
**Settles (tracing only):** F-05, F-10; GAPS D2, D3, D7; D19

Source key: **[N9]** NCERT Class 11 Accountancy, Financial Accounting Part II, Ch. 9 "Financial Statements – II" (keac202.pdf, 2026-27 reprint), §9.8–9.10. **[N4]** Part I Ch. 4 "Recording of Transactions – II" (keac104.pdf). **[N6]** Part I Ch. 6 (keac106.pdf; rectification illustrations). **[A9]** ICAI AS 9 *Revenue Recognition*, text read at indasaccess.icai.org. URLs in §5.

## 1. Findings

### 1.1 A customer or supplier has one personal account that runs on both sides
The textbook ledger keeps a personal account for each customer. Sales to the customer are debited; returns and cash received are credited; the balance is what remains due. The same account takes every kind of entry for that person.
**Source:** [N4] §4 worked ledger, "Ramneek's Account" (sales Dr; sales return and cash Cr; Dr side total ₹4,140 = Cr side total) — fetched
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Ramesh A/c ₹4,140 · Cr Sales A/c ₹4,140`; later `Dr Cash A/c ₹3,700 · Cr Ramesh A/c ₹3,700`; return `Dr Sales Return A/c ₹440 · Cr Ramesh A/c ₹440`. Ramesh's account is closed by a balance. Plain words: "Sold ₹4,140 of goods to Ramesh on credit; he returned ₹440 and paid ₹3,700."

### 1.2 A debt that cannot be realised is a loss: Bad debts A/c Dr, Debtor Cr
Bad debts are amounts the firm has not been able to realise from its debtors. They are a loss, entered `Bad debts A/c Dr · To Debtors A/c`. The loss is already in the trial balance if recorded during the year.
**Source:** [N9] §9.8 — fetched
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Bad Debts A/c ₹2,500 · Cr Mohan A/c ₹2,500`. Plain words: "Mohan has gone bankrupt; the ₹2,500 he owed will never come. Written off."

### 1.3 Further bad debts at year end reduce debtors and raise bad debts
A debtor found irrecoverable only at year end ("further bad debts") is written off by the same entry. Debtors in the balance sheet fall by that amount, bad debts in the profit and loss account rise by it.
**Source:** [N9] §9.8 (₹15,500 debtors, ₹4,500 bad debts + ₹2,500 further = ₹7,000; debtors ₹13,000) — fetched
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Bad Debts A/c ₹2,500 · Cr Debtors A/c ₹2,500` (debtors ₹15,500 → ₹13,000). Plain words: "At year end I learnt one more customer will never pay ₹2,500."

### 1.4 Provision for doubtful debts: an estimate on the remaining good debtors
Because debtors still outstanding may not all pay, a reasonable estimate is charged to the profit and loss account: `Profit and Loss A/c Dr · To Provision for doubtful debts A/c`. The provision is shown as a deduction from debtors on the asset side. It is estimated on debtors *after* further bad debts.
**Source:** [N9] §9.9 (5% of ₹13,000 = ₹650) — fetched
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Profit and Loss A/c ₹650 · Cr Provision for Doubtful Debts A/c ₹650`. Plain words: "I expect about 5% of the ₹13,000 owed to me may not pay, so I set aside ₹650."

### 1.5 An existing provision absorbs the year's bad debts; only the difference is charged
The provision carried from last year (old provision) is used to meet the bad debts of the current year. At year end, bad debts (including further bad debts) are transferred against the provision, and the new provision is set at the required percentage. Profit and loss bears (bad debts + new provision − old provision).
**Source:** [N9] §9.9 worked case (debtors ₹32,000; bad debts ₹2,000; old provision ₹3,500; further bad debts ₹1,000; 5%: entries ₹1,000 / ₹3,000 / ₹1,050; new provision ₹1,550; net debtors ₹29,450) — fetched
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Bad Debts A/c ₹1,000 · Cr Debtors A/c ₹1,000`; `Dr Provision for Doubtful Debts A/c ₹3,000 · Cr Bad Debts A/c ₹3,000`; `Dr Profit and Loss A/c ₹1,050 · Cr Provision for Doubtful Debts A/c ₹1,050`. Plain words: "One more customer will not pay ₹1,000; I had set aside ₹3,500 last year, so the year's ₹3,000 of bad debts come out of that; top up the set-aside to ₹1,550."

### 1.6 Bad debts recovered are credited to their own account, not to the debtor
When an amount previously written off is received, the credit goes to *Bad debts recovered A/c*, not to the debtor's personal account. NCERT's rectification illustrations show a recovery wrongly credited to a debtor and corrected: `Debtor Dr · To Bad debts recovered A/c`. The text I extracted does not state in a sentence whether Bad debts recovered is income, but it names it as the account credited. Chapter 3 exercises also pose a recovery (₹1,200 of a debt written off last year) and a part-payment on insolvency (60 paise in the rupee) as journal questions, without answers in the text.
**Source:** [N6] rectification illustrations (Khanna ₹800, Khalil ₹700) — fetched; NCERT Part I Ch. 3 exercise 14–15 (questions only) — fetched
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Cash A/c ₹1,200 · Cr Bad Debts Recovered A/c ₹1,200`. Plain words: "Received ₹1,200 from Mohan, whose debt I had written off last year."

### 1.7 When collectability doubt arises after the sale: provide, do not reverse the sale
Where doubt about collection arises after the sale, it is more appropriate to make a separate provision for the uncertainty than to adjust the revenue originally recorded. Where collection is uncertain at the time of the claim, recognition of revenue is postponed to the extent of the uncertainty.
**Source:** [A9] ¶9.2 and ¶9.3 — fetched (text read at indasaccess.icai.org)
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Profit and Loss A/c ₹5,000 · Cr Provision for Doubtful Debts A/c ₹5,000` (sale stays at its original amount). Plain words: "Sold to Kapoor Traders ₹40,000; he is now struggling. I keep the sale as it was and put ₹5,000 aside."

### 1.8 Provision for discount on debtors is computed on the good debtors
Discount likely to be allowed to customers may be provided for, on debtors after deducting doubtful debts (₹13,000 − ₹650 = ₹12,350 in the text).
**Source:** [N9] §9.10 — fetched
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Profit and Loss A/c ₹227 · Cr Provision for Discount on Debtors A/c ₹227`. Plain words: "I may allow some prompt-payment discount next year; I set aside ₹227."

### 1.9 Advances paid to suppliers, received from customers, and retention money
The sources I read give no rule for a supplier paid ahead (a debit balance in a creditor's account), a customer who pays ahead (a credit balance in a debtor's account), or an amount held back by a customer as retention. The nearest sourced rule is for *expenses and income*, not goods: an expense paid in advance is a prepaid asset at year end (§9.4) and income received in advance is a liability (§9.6).
**Source:** [N9] §9.4 and §9.6 — fetched; no source found for advances to suppliers or retention
**Example.** Professional (§9.6, rent): `Dr Rent Received A/c ₹3,000 · Cr Rent Received in Advance A/c ₹3,000`. Plain words: "Part of the rent I took belongs to next year, so ₹3,000 is shown as owed back."

### 1.10 An advance paid to a supplier is an asset; an advance received from a customer is a liability, moved to Sales on delivery
Cash paid before the expense or goods is booked creates an asset, called a *trade advance*; cash received before the revenue is booked creates a liability. In the BoS model balance sheet "Advances" (for example *Advances to Suppliers*, shown among current assets) sit on the assets side, and "Advances Taken" among current liabilities. For a customer's advance the BoS keeps an *Advance from Customers* account: cash received is credited to it, and the part that relates to the year's deliveries is transferred to Sales (`Advance from Customers A/c Dr · To Sales A/c`). The BoS gives the supplier-side clearing entry by no worked example; it is the mirror image.
**Source:** ICAI BoS, Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 1 Unit 2, accrual concept, pp. 1.38–1.39; Ch. 7 Unit 1 §1.6 *Balance Sheet*, p. 7.39 and Illustration 6, pp. 7.40–7.41 (*Advances to Suppliers* ₹1,00,000 as an asset); Ch. 7 Unit 1 exercise 4 and its solution (*Advance from Customers A/c*), pp. 7.66, 7.73 — fetched (ICAI BoS PDF) https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74600bos60479-fnd-cp1-u2.pdf; https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74616bos60479-fnd-cp7-u1.pdf.
**Example.** Professional (supplier): `Dr Advance to Kapoor Traders A/c ₹20,000 · Cr Bank A/c ₹20,000`; when the goods arrive, `Dr Purchases A/c ₹20,000 · Cr Advance to Kapoor Traders A/c ₹20,000` (clearing entry: own derivation, the mirror of the BoS customer entry). Customer: `Dr Bank A/c ₹30,000 · Cr Advance from Ramesh A/c ₹30,000`; on delivery `Dr Advance from Ramesh A/c ₹30,000 · Cr Sales A/c ₹30,000`. Plain words: "Paid Kapoor Traders ₹20,000 ahead for goods not yet received." / "Ramesh paid ₹30,000 ahead; the goods went out today, so it now counts as a sale."

### 1.11 A bad debt recovered later is a gain: credit Bad Debts Recovered, not the debtor
If an amount written off as bad is recovered, "it should be treated as a gain. It should not be credited to the party paying it. It should be credited to Bad Debts Recovered Account", which is entered on the credit side of the Profit and Loss Account. The chapter's True/False key marks "recovered debts are credited to Debtors Account" false: they are credited to Bad Debts Recovered Account "and becomes an income". The rectification chapter corrects a recovery wrongly posted to the debtor in the same way.
**Source:** ICAI BoS, Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 7 Unit 1 §1.4 item (v) *Bad debts*, pp. 7.20–7.21; True/False 13 and its answer, pp. 7.61, 7.67; Ch. 2 Unit 6 §6.5.1, p. 2.138 — fetched (ICAI BoS PDF) https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74616bos60479-fnd-cp7-u1.pdf; https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74611bos60479-fnd-cp2-u6.pdf.
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Cash A/c ₹1,200 · Cr Bad Debts Recovered A/c ₹1,200`; at the year end `Dr Bad Debts Recovered A/c ₹1,200 · Cr Profit and Loss A/c ₹1,200`. Plain words: "Mohan, whose ₹1,200 I wrote off last year, has now paid it; it is a gain this year, not a credit on his page."

### 1.12 Bad debts are met first from an existing provision; the provision is then raised to the required figure
Where a provision for doubtful debts exists, bad debts written off are debited to it; otherwise they go to the Profit and Loss Account. When a write-off and a new provision are both needed, the write-off is taken against the existing provision first and the remainder is raised to the required figure; the amount charged to the Profit and Loss Account is what is needed to do that. The provision is worked on trade receivables *after* the write-offs, and a provision for discount on debtors is worked on the receivables after deducting the provision for bad debts, "because Provision for discount is created only on good debtors". On the balance sheet the provision is deducted from the receivables. The BoS illustration: opening provision ₹40,000, bad debts ₹1,00,000, receivables ₹15,00,000, required provision 5% of ₹14,00,000 = ₹70,000, so ₹1,30,000 is charged to the Profit and Loss Account.
**Source:** ICAI BoS, Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 7 Unit 1 §1.4 adjustments (6) and (7) and Illustration 4, pp. 7.26–7.27; summary of adjustments, items 6–8, p. 7.57 — fetched (ICAI BoS PDF) https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74616bos60479-fnd-cp7-u1.pdf. This agrees with §1.4–§1.5 (NCERT).
**Example.** Professional (own figures): opening provision ₹2,000; bad debt ₹1,000 (receivables then ₹40,000); required 5% = ₹2,000. `Dr Bad Debts A/c ₹1,000 · Cr Mohan A/c ₹1,000`; `Dr Provision for Doubtful Debts A/c ₹1,000 · Cr Bad Debts A/c ₹1,000`; top up: `Dr Profit and Loss A/c ₹1,000 · Cr Provision for Doubtful Debts A/c ₹1,000` (provision is again ₹2,000 = 5% of ₹40,000). Plain words: "Mohan will never pay ₹1,000; I had ₹2,000 set aside, so ₹1,000 of it is used up; I add ₹1,000 to bring the set-aside back to 5% of the ₹40,000 still owed to me."

## 2. Where the sources disagree, or are silent
- **Person who is both debtor and creditor; set-off; advances to suppliers; advances from customers; retention money:** NCERT Part I/II (extracted text) is silent. AS 9 deals only with revenue recognition and collectability, not with presentation of advances. ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1 Ch. 1, 2 and 7 were read on 20 Oct 2026 (§1.10–§1.12) and are silent on the dual-role person, set-off and retention money; Intermediate chapters on contractors were **not fetched**. A search for ICAI retention-money coverage returned nothing relevant.
- **Bad debts recovered as income:** NCERT shows the account name in rectification illustrations but I did not see a sentence describing it as income. The ICAI BoS states it plainly (§1.11): a gain, credited to Bad Debts Recovered A/c and the Profit and Loss Account. No higher source conflicts.
- **Provision for doubtful debts and AS 29:** not fetched; whether AS 29 governs it was not checked.
- **Small books:** NCERT's rule 1.4–1.5 is for an entity that uses the mercantile basis; whether a cash-basis book keeps any provision is topic 01.
- **BoS Foundation Ch. 1, 2 and 7 read (20 Oct 2026).** The BoS now answers advances (§1.10), bad debts recovered (§1.11) and the order of write-off and provision (§1.12). It is silent on a person who is both customer and supplier, on set-off between the two, on retention money (no hit for "retention" in any Foundation chapter read) and on ageing of debtors (no hit). Its model balance sheet shows trade receivables and "Provision for Bad debts" (Ch. 7 Unit 1 pp. 7.39–7.40) but no sub-ledger rule for a person with both balances.

## 4. Answers to the GAPS items this topic settles
- **D2 (builder's 5% retention held back in the party balance):** not settled, see §3 (retention).
- **D3 (supplier paid in advance crosses to Dr and back):** the personal account runs on both sides (§1.1); the BoS says an advance paid is an asset (*Advances to Suppliers*) and an advance received a liability (§1.10) but not whether it sits in the same personal account as other dealings; see §3.
- **D7 (tenant who paid ahead sits Cr):** nearest rule: rent received in advance is a liability [N9 §9.6]; not settled for a tenant's personal account.
- **F-05, F-10, D19:** open findings/decisions; the underlying questions are in §3.
