# 18 — Landlord

**Questions (from the outline):** Rent charged vs rent received. Advance rent. Tenants' security deposits. Repairs vs improvements.
**Settles (tracing only):** GAPS D6, D7

House-property bookkeeping only; no tax. No source read is written for a landlord. The findings are the textbook rules for income and expense adjustments and for subsequent expenditure on property, applied to rent. Where that application is mine rather than the source's, §1 says so and §3 carries the question.

## 1. Findings

### 1.1 Rent earned but not yet received is income of the year (accrued income), shown as an asset
Rent that is earned in the year but not received by year end is added to the rent income of that year, and the amount due appears on the balance sheet as a current asset. Adjusting entry: *Accrued income A/c Dr · To the concerned income A/c*. The same is taught for commission, interest and rent.
**Source:** NCERT Accountancy Class 11 Part II, Ch. 9 *Financial Statements – II*, §9.5 *Accrued Income* — fetched (https://ncert.nic.in/textbook/pdf/keac202.pdf). The worked example is commission, not rent; the chapter names rent in the opening sentence of §9.5.
**Example.** Professional: Rent of ₹8,000 for March is due from tenant Sharma and not received on 31 March. `Dr Accrued Rent A/c ₹8,000 · Cr Rent Income A/c ₹8,000`. Plain words: "March rent ₹8,000 from Sharma is still due; counted as this year's income."

### 1.2 Rent charged to several tenants in one entry (compound journal entry)
A journal entry may have more than one account on either side ("compound journal entry"). The textbook's own example has one debit and two credits; nothing in the chapter restricts the number of accounts. How a landlord posts a month's rent to each tenant's own account is **not** taught: the textbook's personal-account treatment is shown for suppliers and customers (for example `Purchases A/c Dr · To Govind Traders A/c`).
**Source:** NCERT Accountancy Class 11 Part I, Ch. 3 *Recording of Transactions – I*, section on the journal (simple and compound entries, "Office Furniture … Cash … Modern Furniture"), p. 62–63 — fetched (https://ncert.nic.in/textbook/pdf/keac103.pdf).
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Sharma (Tenant) A/c ₹6,000 · Dr Verma (Tenant) A/c ₹7,000 · Cr Rent Income A/c ₹13,000`. Plain words: "Rent for April: Sharma ₹6,000, Verma ₹7,000, total ₹13,000." (The multi-debit shape is the form the textbook permits; the tenant accounts are my application, see §3 row 1.)

### 1.3 Rent received in advance is a liability, not income of the year
A part of an income that belongs to the next period is "income received in advance" (unearned income). It is not credited to income; it is a liability on the balance sheet. NCERT's example is a sub-let building: rent for April, May and June received on 31 March at ₹1,000 a month, ₹3,000 in all. The amount had been credited to income; the adjusting entry is *Rent received A/c Dr 3,000 · To Rent received in advance A/c 3,000*.
**Source:** NCERT Accountancy Class 11 Part II, Ch. 9, §9.6 *Income Received in Advance* — fetched.
**Example.** Professional: Tenant Verma pays ₹9,000 on 20 March for April–June. On receipt: `Dr Cash A/c ₹9,000 · Cr Rent Received in Advance A/c ₹9,000`. In April, ₹3,000 for the month: `Dr Rent Received in Advance A/c ₹3,000 · Cr Rent Income A/c ₹3,000`. If it was first credited to income: `Dr Rent Income A/c ₹9,000 · Cr Rent Received in Advance A/c ₹9,000` (NCERT §9.6 pattern). Plain words: "Verma paid three months ahead, ₹9,000; kept as 'rent received in advance' until each month comes."

### 1.4 A tenant who has paid ahead is owed, not a debtor
Following §1.3, the tenant's prepayment is something the landlord owes (a liability). The ICAI Foundation material shows the same pattern for refundable deposits (§1.5). It does not show a tenant's personal account in credit; that follows from the liability rule only by my reading. See §3 row 2.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Part II Ch. 9 §9.6 (liability for rent received in advance) — fetched.
**Example.** Professional: Verma has paid ₹9,000 for three months; in April's rent ₹3,000 is due. If kept on a tenant's account: `Dr Verma (Tenant) A/c ₹3,000 · Cr Rent Income A/c ₹3,000`; Verma's account then stands at Cr ₹6,000 until the next two months. Plain words: "Verma is ₹6,000 ahead with us."

### 1.5 A tenant's security deposit is a liability while held
No ICAI or NCERT text read speaks of a tenant's deposit. Two close analogies: (a) the ICAI Foundation not-for-profit chapter lists "Laboratory and library deposit" collected from students as an item that "remain[s] with the institution till the student finally leaves", and its worked balance sheet shows "Security Deposit – Students ₹1,50,000" among **current liabilities**; (b) a municipal accounts manual (search extract only) says a deposit collected is a liability when received and is settled on refund, and becomes income only when a forfeited deposit's refund right has expired.
**Source:** ICAI BoS Foundation, Ch. 8, §5.2.3 and Illustration 12 balance sheet — fetched (https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74619bos60479-fnd-cp8.pdf, pp. 8.31 and 8.36). Municipal manual — search (https://cpgfm.icai.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/AP-Accounts-Manual.pdf); not read in full, not an ICAI pronouncement.
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Cash A/c ₹50,000 · Cr Security Deposit – Sharma A/c ₹50,000` (received). On vacating: `Dr Security Deposit – Sharma A/c ₹50,000 · Cr Cash A/c ₹50,000`. Plain words: "Sharma gave ₹50,000 as deposit." / "Returned Sharma's ₹50,000 deposit when he left."

### 1.6 Repairs go to expense; improvements are added to the cost of the building
Day-to-day servicing (labour, consumables, small parts; "Repairs and Maintenance") is not added to the asset: it is charged to the profit and loss account as incurred. Expenditure that increases the life or efficiency of the asset beyond what was first assessed, and replacement of a major part, is capitalised; the carrying amount of any replaced part is derecognised. The table "Subsequent Expenditure" sets these side by side.
**Source:** ICAI BoS, Intermediate Paper 1, Ch. 5 Unit 2 (AS 10 Property, Plant and Equipment), §2.10 *Treatment of Subsequent Costs*, pp. 5.38–5.40 — fetched (https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74696bos60485-inter-p1-cp5-u2.pdf). Study material that explains AS 10, not the pronouncement itself.
**Example.** Professional: whitewash of the rented house ₹8,000: `Dr Repairs and Maintenance A/c ₹8,000 · Cr Cash A/c ₹8,000`. Building an extra room that lets the landlord rent more: `Dr House (Building) A/c ₹2,00,000 · Cr Cash A/c ₹2,00,000`. Plain words: "Paid ₹8,000 for whitewash." / "Paid ₹2,00,000 to add a room."

### 1.7 The BoS: rent and premiums on leases accrue; rent received in advance is a liability on a personal (representative) account
The BoS lists "Rents and Premiums on leases" among *income accrued and accruing*: the amount earned but not yet received is added to the year's income and carried as an asset, by `Accruing account Dr · To Appropriate Income Account`; income received in advance is deducted from the year's income and carried as a liability (*Income received in advance*). Its classification table calls "Rent received in advance" a *personal* account and a liability, and "representative personal accounts" are described as those not in the name of a person (outstanding and prepaid accounts, capital, drawings). The BoS profit and loss format lists "Rent of property let-out" on the credit side. The BoS has no landlord's tenant ledger and no rule for a rent register.
**Source:** ICAI BoS, Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 7 Unit 1 §1.4 adjustments (2) and (3), pp. 7.24–7.25; profit and loss format, p. 7.16; Ch. 2 Unit 1 §1.7.1, p. 2.16 and §1.8 table, p. 2.29(r) — fetched (ICAI BoS PDF) https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74616bos60479-fnd-cp7-u1.pdf; https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74606bos60479-fnd-cp2-u1.pdf. Study material, not a pronouncement.
**Example.** Professional (own figures): March rent ₹12,000 is due but unpaid at 31 March: `Dr Accrued Rent A/c ₹12,000 · Cr Rent Income A/c ₹12,000`; a tenant has paid ₹6,000 for next year: `Dr Rent Income A/c ₹6,000 · Cr Rent Received in Advance A/c ₹6,000`. Plain words: "Sharma has not yet paid March's ₹12,000; it is this year's income and he owes it." / "Verma paid ₹6,000 for next year; it is not this year's income, it is owed back to him as service."

## 2. Where the sources disagree, or are silent
- **Silent:** every source read is silent on a landlord's own book, a rent register, and the tenant's personal account.
- **Silent:** tenant deposits (only analogies, §1.5): forfeiture, set-off against dues, interest on a deposit.
- **Silent:** whether a small landlord must capitalise improvements and depreciate (topic 9 asks it; AS 10 applies to enterprises).
- **Silent:** rent written off as unrecoverable: belongs to bad debts (topic 6), not read here.
- **Not read:** ICAI Guidance Note on Non-Corporate Entities, ICAI AS 9 text (revenue recognition) — not fetched; Grewal, Goel — not fetched.
- The AS 10 chapter used is the **Revised** presentation in BoS study material; the older "fixed assets" wording was not compared.
- **BoS Foundation read (20 Oct 2026).** Ch. 1 Unit 3 §3.2(c)–(d), p. 1.63 says expenditure on improvement of an asset is charged to profit and loss where expected benefits do not change and is added to the asset where benefits increase, and that materiality is one of the considerations (§3.2(e)); no exemption from depreciation is stated. The BoS is silent on forfeiture or set-off of a deposit and on a tenant's own ledger account (no hit for "tenant" outside exercises).

## 4. Answers to the GAPS items this topic settles
- **D6** (rent charged to several tenants in one entry): a journal entry with several accounts on one side is a compound entry (NCERT Class 11 Ch. 3); the textbook's example has several credits, and a landlord's multi-tenant shape is my application, not a quoted rule. See §3 row 1.
- **D7** (a tenant who paid ahead sits Cr): rent received in advance is a liability (NCERT Class 11 Part II Ch. 9 §9.6), consistent with a Cr position. Whether it sits on the tenant's own account is not settled, §3 row 2.
