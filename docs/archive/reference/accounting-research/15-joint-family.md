# 15 — Joint family

**Questions (from the outline):** The karta (who manages the family) and the sub-families. How a joint family keeps its own account and its sub-families' accounts. Businesses owned by the family. Money a family business sends home: income or return of capital. Settling or carrying forward at year end.
**Settles (tracing only):** GAPS A11(1)(3), C4

**Headline.** The formal sources read for this note are silent on the joint family. None of them mentions a joint family, a karta, a Hindu undivided family or a sub-family account. Rule 6 of the outline excludes Hindu law as a source. Rule 1 says a rule with no source is not written. So §1 holds only what the textbooks say about the nearest thing they do teach (an owner and the business he owns), clearly labelled as an analogy. Everything specific to the family goes to §3. One web search for a bookkeeping or textbook treatment of joint-family accounts returned only tax and bank-account guides (not read, not used).

## 1. Findings

### 1.1 The business is a separate entity from its owner; the owner's personal dealings stay out of the business's book
For accounting, the business and its owner are two separate entities. When the owner brings money in, it is the business's liability to the owner (capital). When he takes money out for personal use (drawings), that reduces his capital. The book is kept from the business's point of view. The owner's personal assets, liabilities and transactions are not recorded in it, unless they move business funds in or out.
**Source:** NCERT Accountancy Class 11 (Financial Accounting Part I), Ch. 2 *Theory Base of Accounting*, §2.2.1 *Business Entity Concept*; and Ch. 1, §1.5.16 *Drawings* (definition: withdrawal of money or goods by the owner for personal use; drawings reduce the investment) — fetched (ncert.nic.in/textbook/pdf/keac102.pdf, keac101.pdf; text read, reprint 2026-27).
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Cash A/c ₹50,000 · Cr Ramesh Capital A/c ₹50,000` (money put in); `Dr Ramesh Drawings A/c ₹5,000 · Cr Cash A/c ₹5,000` (money taken for the house). Plain words: "Ramesh put ₹50,000 into the shop." "Ramesh took ₹5,000 from the shop for home."
**Use of this finding.** It is taught for a **sole proprietor**. Money a business sends to its owner is **drawings** there, a reduction of capital and not income of the owner. The textbooks do not say whether the same holds when the owner is a joint family, or whether the family's own book should treat the money received as income. That is §3 row 1.

### 1.2 The only formal "account with another book" the textbooks teach is the head-office / branch pair
Two books joined by converse accounts (each book holds an account named after the other; the two balances are equal and opposite) is taught only for head office and branch. See note 16 §1.1. It is **not** taught for a family and its members. Whether a family's account for a sub-family can be handled the same way is not answered by any source read (§3 row 2).
**Source:** ICAI BoS, Intermediate Paper 1 *Advanced Accounting*, Ch. 15 *Accounting for Branches including Foreign Branches*, §6 — fetched (see note 16).
**Example.** See note 16 §1.1 (not repeated here, because it is not a family rule).

## 2. Where the sources disagree, or are silent
- **Silent:** NCERT Class 11 Part I (Ch. 1–5), Part II Ch. 9 (as far as read); NCERT Class 12 Part I Ch. 1–4 (partnership); ICAI BoS Foundation *Principles and Practice of Accounting* Ch. 8 (not-for-profit; the module introduction was also read); ICAI BoS Intermediate *Accounting* Ch. 5 Unit 2 and Ch. 15; ICAI Technical Guide on NPOs. A text search of the files read for "joint family", "karta" and "undivided" found no hit.
- **Not read:** ICAI Guidance Note on Financial Statements of Non-Corporate Entities — not fetched; it may say something about a family-owned business, and this note cannot say it does not.
- **The ICAI Framework is silent on families.** Read in full (ICAI PDF (ASB, issued July 2000; owner-supplied copy `89089asb-aps2918-framework.pdf`, checked 20 Oct 2026)): ¶67 names sole proprietorships, partnerships and trusts as enterprises to which its definition of equity applies; no paragraph mentions a family, a household or a joint family.
- **Not read:** Grewal and Goel (paid) — not fetched, not cited.
- **Traditional practice** (rokad bahi, khata bahi and the jama/naam convention for family accounts) was not researched in this note; it is the tier where a family's account with its members is most likely to be described, and it is a gap in this research.
- **The Guidance Note's coverage (FAQ, 20 Oct 2026).** ICAI's FAQ on the Guidance Note on Financial Statements of Non-Corporate Entities lists, among the non-corporate *business or professional* entities the Note covers, a joint family carrying on business (Q2(b), under its legal name). The Note prescribes presentation formats (Q4); it says nothing, as far as the FAQ shows, about how a family keeps its household accounts or its sub-families' accounts. The Note itself is still not read.
- **ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1 chapters read (20 Oct 2026).** Chapters 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 9 and 10 were searched for "joint family", "karta", "HUF", "undivided", "household" and "Hindu". The only family mention is the HUF in Ch. 10 Unit 1 §1.2(i), p. 10.3, as an example of a relation that arises from *status* (not contract) as against a partnership; it carries no accounting rule. The BoS drawings list for an owner's book ("household expenses met from business cash", Ch. 9 §4.4, p. 9.22) is for a sole proprietor and partners, not a family.

## 4. Answers to the GAPS items this topic settles
- **A11(1)** (joint family and sub-family running account; settle or carry forward): not settled, see §3 rows 1, 2, 5.
- **A11(3)** (money a family business sends home is the family's income): not settled, see §3 rows 3, 4. The only teaching found (drawings, a reduction of capital) is for a sole proprietor, and it points to *return of capital*, not income, if it applied.
- **C4** (a household's account with the joint family sits under its own money): not settled. Nearest source: a branch's balance with head office is a net-assets figure, not a debtor or creditor in the ordinary sense (ICAI BoS Intermediate Ch. 15 §6, "the branch will be looked upon either as a debtor or creditor"). That is about branches only.
