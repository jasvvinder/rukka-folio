# 04 — Opening a set of books

**Questions (from the outline):** The opening statement of affairs. Capital as the balancing figure. Opening balances of persons, banks and assets.
**Settles (tracing only):** GAPS B4

How the sources were read. NCERT and ICAI BoS PDFs were downloaded and the full text read (`fetched`). The current NCERT Class 11 books (reprint 2026-27) no longer contain a chapter on incomplete records or statement of affairs; that material is taken from the ICAI BoS chapter, which is study material, not a pronouncement.

## 1. Findings

### 1.1 Starting a new business: the first entry is the owner's capital
A new business has no earlier balances. Its first entries record what the owner brings in: cash introduced is debited to Cash, money put straight into the bank is debited to Bank, and the credit is to the owner's Capital account. In a cash book started for a new business the amount is written "To Capital Account" in the cash or bank column.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Financial Accounting I, ch. 3 §3.2–3.3.1, example "Rohit started business with a capital of ₹5,00,000" (p. 51, 55) — fetched. ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1, Module 1 ch. 2 unit 5 (Cash Book), "writing up the three-column cash book" point 1 — fetched.
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Cash A/c ₹5,00,000 · Cr Rohit's Capital A/c ₹5,00,000`. Plain words: "Rohit started the business with ₹5,00,000 cash."

### 1.2 Starting books for an existing business: the opening statement of affairs
When a business that has been running without proper books is brought into books, or when its position at a date is needed, a statement of affairs is drawn up: a list of assets and liabilities at that date, laid out like a balance sheet, with capital as the balancing figure. ICAI BoS: Capital = Assets − Liabilities. Sources for the figures: cash book for cash; bank pass-book for the bank; the personal ledger or list for debtors and creditors; inventory by actual count; fixed assets from a list, with original cost and date of purchase, less a reasonable depreciation to give a written-down value.
**Source:** ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Module II, Aug 2026 ed.), ch. 9 §3.1 (printed pp. 9.6–9.8; the statement-of-affairs design is on p. 9.8) — fetched. (Label and page range corrected 20 Oct 2026: it is Paper 1, and §3.1 begins on p. 9.6.)
**Example.** Professional (BoS illustration, statement of affairs of Mr X at 31 March 2021): assets Building ₹1,00,000, Furniture ₹50,000, Inventory ₹1,20,000, Debtors ₹40,000, Bank ₹70,000, Cash ₹1,200 = ₹3,81,200; liabilities Loans ₹1,00,000, Creditors ₹40,000 = ₹1,40,000; Capital (balancing figure) ₹2,41,200. Plain words: "On 31 March 2021 the business owned ₹3,81,200 and owed ₹1,40,000, so Mr X's share was ₹2,41,200."

### 1.3 Capital is the balancing figure, not an entered amount
In a statement of affairs capital is "merely a balancing figure being excess of assets over liabilities", so assets need not equal liabilities until capital is added. In a balance sheet drawn from the ledger, capital comes from the capital account and the two sides always agree. ICAI BoS says a statement of affairs is built partly from records and partly from memory and estimate, and valuation is "arbitrary"; its purpose is to find capital at the beginning and end of a period. The same device serves not-for-profit bodies: the opening Accumulated or Capital Fund is the excess of the total assets over the liabilities at the start of the period, found from an opening balance sheet.
**Source:** ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1, ch. 9 §3.2 table (printed pp. 9.9–9.10) — fetched. (Label corrected 20 Oct 2026: Paper 1, not Paper 2.) ICAI BoS Module 2 ch. 8, the section on preparing the Income and Expenditure Account and Balance Sheet from the Receipts and Payments Account, step (i) — fetched.
**Example.** Professional (BoS Illustration 2): Rakesh holds cash ₹3,200, inventory ₹34,800, Ajay owes ₹31,000, machine ₹85,000 (total ₹1,54,000); owes Vinod ₹12,000 and a bank loan ₹10,000 (total ₹22,000); capital = ₹1,32,000. Plain words: "Everything he has is worth ₹1,54,000, he owes ₹22,000; what is his own is ₹1,32,000."

### 1.4 The opening entry in the journal proper
When books are started or a new year begins, the opening balances of assets and liabilities are journalised. This is the first of the journal-proper entries: each asset is debited, each liability and the capital credited. The balance sheet of one period becomes the opening trial balance of the next, and an opening entry in the journal opens the accounts it contains. In the opening entries of the sources read, the credit goes to the owner's Capital account; no account called "Opening Balance" appears.
**Source:** NCERT ch. 4 §4.6 item 1 (p. 139) — fetched. NCERT Class 11 Financial Accounting II, ch. 8 §8.6 and §8.7 "Opening Entry" (p. 300, 308) — fetched. ICAI BoS Module 1 ch. 2 unit 4, §4.7 journal proper list item (i) "Opening entries" (p. 2.96) — fetched.
**Example.** Professional (NCERT §8.7): `Dr Furniture A/c ₹15,000 · Dr Debtors A/c ₹15,500 · Dr Bank A/c ₹5,000 · Dr Cash A/c ₹1,000 · Cr Capital A/c ₹16,500 · Cr 10% Long-term Loan A/c ₹5,000 · Cr Creditors A/c ₹15,000` (debits ₹36,500 = credits ₹36,500). Plain words: "Opening day: furniture ₹15,000, debtors ₹15,500, bank ₹5,000 and cash ₹1,000; owed ₹5,000 loan and ₹15,000 to creditors; the owner's capital is ₹16,500."

### 1.5 Opening balances of persons (debtors and creditors)
After the opening entry, each person's account starts with a balance brought down on the side it belongs: a debit balance (a person who owes the business) as "To Balance b/d" on the debit side; a credit balance (a person the business owes) as "By Balance b/d" on the credit side. This is the same b/d that follows any period's balancing; the opening entry and the brought-down balance are the same amount.
**Source:** ICAI BoS Module 1 ch. 2 unit 2 §2.4 (p. 2.54) — fetched. NCERT ch. 4 §4.7 (p. 141) — fetched.
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Kapoor Traders A/c ₹15,500 · Cr Capital A/c ₹15,500` (as part of the opening entry): Kapoor Traders' page opens "To Balance b/d ₹15,500" on the debit side. Plain words: "On opening day Kapoor Traders owed us ₹15,500."

### 1.6 Opening balances of cash and bank
The cash book starts with the cash and bank balances at the beginning of the period. When a new cash book is begun for an existing business the opening balances are written "To Balance b/d". A debit opening balance in the bank column is a favourable balance; a credit opening balance in the bank column is an overdraft, which is a liability. No separate cash or bank account is opened in the ledger when a cash book is kept.
**Source:** NCERT ch. 4 §4.1 (p. 100) — fetched. ICAI BoS Module 1 ch. 2 unit 5, three-column cash book point 1 (p. 2.108) and the true/false items on overdraft (pp. 2.119, 2.121) — fetched.
**Example.** Professional: opening bank overdraft ₹500, cash ₹2,000: cash book `Dr Cash ₹2,000` (To Balance b/d, cash column) and the bank column shows ₹500 on the credit side (By Balance b/d). Plain words: "Opening day: ₹2,000 cash in hand; the bank account is overdrawn by ₹500."

### 1.7 Opening balances of assets
Fixed assets enter the opening statement at original cost less depreciation to date (written-down value). In a statement of affairs the owner supplies cost and date and an accountant allows a reasonable depreciation. In the balance sheet proper fixed assets are shown at original cost less depreciation to date and the method of valuation is disclosed. Inventory is entered by physical count and valuation.
**Source:** ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1, Module II ch. 9 §3.1 (printed p. 9.8) and §3.2 table, "Basis of valuation" row (p. 9.10) — fetched.
**Example.** Professional: furniture bought for ₹50,000 two years ago, depreciated ₹5,000 a year, opened as `Dr Furniture A/c ₹40,000` against Capital (illustration built from the rule). Plain words: "Furniture that cost ₹50,000 is counted at ₹40,000 after two years' wear."

### 1.8 Partnership: the partners' capital accounts are the opening capital
In a firm every dealing between the partners and the firm is recorded through the partners' capital accounts. Under the fixed capital method each partner has a capital account that stays fixed unless capital is added or withdrawn, plus a current account for profit share, drawings and interest; under the fluctuating capital method there is one account per partner. At the firm's opening the firm's capital is the sum of what the partners bring in. ICAI BoS ch. 9 does not give a separate partnership statement of affairs; it treats a proprietorship or partnership alike for capital comparison.
**Source:** NCERT Class 12 Accountancy Part I, ch. 1 §1.4 — fetched. ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1, Module II ch. 9 §1 and §3.1 (pp. 9.3–9.4, 9.6; profit by capital comparison includes "fresh investments" by partners) — fetched.
**Example.** Professional: Ramesh brings ₹3,00,000 cash and Sunita brings furniture ₹1,00,000: `Dr Cash A/c ₹3,00,000 · Dr Furniture A/c ₹1,00,000 · Cr Ramesh's Capital A/c ₹3,00,000 · Cr Sunita's Capital A/c ₹1,00,000` (illustration built from the rule). Plain words: "Ramesh put in ₹3,00,000 cash, Sunita put in furniture worth ₹1,00,000."

### 1.9 Checking the opening: debits equal credits, and the opening entry is checked first when a trial balance fails
The total of the debit opening balances (assets) must equal the total of the credit opening balances (liabilities and capital); capital is the figure that makes it so. When a trial balance fails to agree, "the posting of all the entries including the opening entry should be checked".
**Source:** NCERT ch. 3 §3.2 (p. 50–51) — fetched. ICAI BoS Module 1 ch. 2 unit 6 (Rectification of Errors), §6.4 list item (viii) on locating a trial balance difference, p. 2.131, just before §6.5 — fetched.
**Example.** Professional: debits ₹36,500 (Furniture ₹15,000, Debtors ₹15,500, Bank ₹5,000, Cash ₹1,000) equal credits ₹36,500 (Capital ₹16,500, Loan ₹5,000, Creditors ₹15,000). Plain words: "What the business owns, ₹36,500, equals what it owes plus the owner's share."

### 1.10 The BoS opening entry: assets debited; each liability to its own account; capital the balancing credit
The first journal entry of a new year records the closing balances of the previous year, taken from the balance sheet: "the assets shown in the balance sheet are debited and the liabilities and the capital account credited". A creditor is credited in his own account, not in a general account; capital is credited as the balance. Posting it opens each asset account with "To Balance b/d" on the debit side and each liability account with "By Balance b/d" on the credit side. The BoS example makes no use of an account called "Opening Balance".
**Source:** ICAI BoS, Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 7 Unit 1 §1.8 *Opening entry*, Illustration 8, pp. 7.43–7.44 — fetched (ICAI BoS PDF) https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74616bos60479-fnd-cp7-u1.pdf. Study material, not a pronouncement.
**Example.** Professional (the BoS figures): `Dr Cash A/c ₹43,000 · Dr Bank A/c ₹2,67,500 · Dr Trade Receivables ₹7,49,500 · Dr Inventory A/c ₹9,00,000 · Dr Machinery and Equipment A/c ₹6,00,000 · Cr Mahendra & Sons A/c ₹5,60,000 · Cr Capital A/c ₹20,00,000` (₹25,60,000 each side). Plain words: "On 1 April I write down what the shop has and owes as the balance sheet left it: cash, bank, ₹7,49,500 owed to me, stock and machines; ₹5,60,000 owed to Mahendra & Sons; the rest, ₹20,00,000, is my capital."

## 2. Where the sources disagree, or are silent
- **Capital as an account vs a figure.** NCERT and BoS credit a Capital account in the opening entry; in a statement of affairs the same capital is only a balancing figure. They agree once the entry is posted; the order (figure first, entry second) is the BoS method.
- **No "Opening Balance" account.** None of the sources read uses an account named "Opening Balance" in an opening entry. Absence of use is not a prohibition; no source says whether one may be used.
- **Household and joint family.** No source read describes an opening statement for a household or a joint family, or what stands in place of capital for a family.
- **Negative capital.** The ICAI BoS ch. 9 text read does not say how a statement of affairs shows liabilities exceeding assets. A search-result extract from a secondary guide (Oswal Publishers, `search`) said the shortfall appears as capital deficiency on the assets side; not confirmed in a primary source.
- **Partnership opening.** NCERT Class 12 ch. 1 does not give an opening entry; the example in 1.8 is built from §1.4. ICAI BoS has no partnership statement of affairs.
- **NCERT currency.** The current NCERT Class 11 books do not teach statement of affairs; ICAI BoS is the only source read for it.
- **BoS Foundation Ch. 7 and Ch. 9 re-read (20 Oct 2026).** The BoS opening entry (§1.10) credits Capital and each person's own account; it does not use an "Opening Balance" account and does not say one is forbidden. Its statement-of-affairs text (Ch. 9 §3.2, pp. 9.9–9.10) calls capital "merely a balancing figure" and still does not say how a deficit is shown. It is silent on a household or joint family opening, and on a mid-year start (no hit).

## 4. Answers to the GAPS items this topic settles
- **B4 (partnership openings are each partner's agreed figures; the firm's assets balance them; confirm that no Opening Balance / Capital line appears when the partners' figures balance the firm's).** Practice in the sources read: the firm's capital is the partners' capital accounts, credited directly in the opening entry (NCERT Class 12 ch. 1 §1.4; NCERT Class 11 Part II §8.7 for the form of opening entry); capital is the excess of assets over liabilities (ICAI BoS ch. 9 §3.1). No source uses an account named "Opening Balance". Whether such a line may appear when the partners' figures already balance the firm's is not addressed; see section 3.
