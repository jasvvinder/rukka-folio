# 13 — Sole proprietor and household

**Questions (from the outline):** Capital and drawings, including drawings in kind. The owner's personal book vs the business's book: how each records the other. What a household's own account shows. Why personal money is not "profit".
**Settles (tracing only):** GAPS C2, C3; D13; A11(2)

Citation key. "NCERT 11-I" = NCERT Accountancy Class 11, *Financial Accounting – I* (files keac101…keac107, Reprint 2026-27); "NCERT 11-II" = *Financial Accounting – II* (keac201, keac202). "File p." is the page number of the PDF file, not the printed folio. "ICAI ED-NCE" = ICAI *Exposure Draft, Guidance Note on Financial Statements of Non-Corporate Entities* (comments by 8 March 2023), the only version of that Guidance Note I could read; the final Guidance Note was not fetched.

## 1. Findings

### 1.1 The owner and the business are two separate parties
For accounting, the business and its owner are treated as two separate entities. Money the owner brings in is recorded as an amount the business owes the owner; money the owner takes out for personal expenses reduces that amount. The books are written from the business's point of view. The owner's personal assets and liabilities are not recorded in the business books, and the owner's personal transactions are not recorded either, "unless it involves inflow or outflow of business funds".
**Source:** NCERT 11-I, ch. 2 *Theory Base of Accounting*, §2.2.1 *Business Entity Concept* (file keac102, p.3-4) — fetched
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Cash A/c ₹5,00,000 · Cr Capital A/c ₹5,00,000`. Plain words: "Ramesh started his shop with ₹5,00,000 of his own cash." (The textbook's own opening entry is the same shape: NCERT 11-I ch. 3, Illustration 1 journal, "Business started with cash", file keac103 p.18.)

### 1.2 Capital introduced: debit the asset received, credit Capital
Fresh capital brought in during the year is recorded the same way as the opening capital: the asset received is debited and the owner's Capital account is credited. NCERT also classes additional capital brought in by the owner as a *capital receipt* (it creates an obligation to the owner), not income.
**Source:** NCERT 11-I, ch. 3, Illustration 1 journal (keac103 p.18); NCERT 11-II, ch. 8 §8.2.2 *Receipts* (keac201 p.5) — fetched
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Bank A/c ₹1,00,000 · Cr Capital A/c ₹1,00,000`. Plain words: "Ramesh put another ₹1,00,000 from his own pocket into the business bank account."

### 1.3 Drawings of cash: debit Drawings, credit Cash (or Bank)
Money the proprietor takes out for personal use is debited to a Drawings account. The Drawings account is not an expense: it has the effect of reducing the owner's capital. At the end of the year its balance is transferred to the Capital account (a "transfer entry", also called a closing entry). In the balance sheet it is shown as a deduction from capital.
**Source:** NCERT 11-I, ch. 3, Illustration 2 journal entry of 29 April, "Withdrawal of cash from the business for personal use of the proprietor" (keac103 p.20); ch. 4 §4.6 *Journal Proper*, item 4 *Transfer entries* "Drawing account is transferred to capital account at the end of the accounting year" (keac104 p.42); NCERT 11-II ch. 8 §8.6.2 item (8) *Drawings* (keac201 p.26) — fetched
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Drawings A/c ₹5,000 · Cr Cash A/c ₹5,000`; at year end `Dr Capital A/c ₹60,000 · Cr Drawings A/c ₹60,000` (the year-end transfer is the textbook's rule; the textbook text names it but this particular closing journal is written by me from that rule). Plain words: "Ramesh took ₹5,000 from the till for home expenses." Year end: "His ₹60,000 of withdrawals for the year are taken off his capital."

### 1.4 Drawings in kind (goods taken for own use)
Goods the owner takes from the stock for personal use are recorded in the journal proper. The textbook's correcting entry for goods taken and not recorded is `Drawings A/c Dr · To Purchases A/c`. In the final accounts of a business that follows this route the goods are deducted from purchases in the trading account and from capital in the balance sheet. The textbook's examples state the goods at cost ("Goods costing ₹1,000 were taken away by the proprietor"); a selling-price valuation is not discussed in what I read.
**Source:** NCERT 11-I ch. 4 §4.6 item 5(iii) "Goods withdrawn by the owner for personal use" (keac104 p.42); ch. 6 Illustration 7(i), "Drawings A/c Dr 500 · To Purchases A/c 500" (keac106 p.34); NCERT 11-II ch. 9 *Financial Statements – II*, adjustment 7 and its solution: "Less Goods withdrawn (1,000)" in the trading account and in the balance sheet under capital (keac202 file pp.44-45) — fetched
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Drawings A/c ₹500 · Cr Purchases A/c ₹500`. Plain words: "Ramesh took ₹500 worth of goods (at what they cost him) from the shop for his house."

### 1.5 Net profit goes to the proprietor's capital
In a sole proprietorship the profit or loss ascertained by the profit and loss account is transferred to the proprietor's capital account. The capital shown on the balance sheet is the opening capital, plus profit (or minus loss), plus capital introduced, minus drawings.
**Source:** NCERT 11-II ch. 8 §8.6.2 item (7) *Capital*, "the amount originally contributed … as increased by profits … and decreased by losses, drawings" (keac201 p.26); ch. 9 solution, "Net profit (transferred to capital account)" and balance sheet "Capital 60,000, Add Net profit 20,194, Less Drawings (6,000)" (keac202 file pp.44-45); NCERT 12-I ch. 1 §1.5 (keac101 p.8) states the same for "sole partnership" — fetched
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Profit and Loss A/c ₹80,000 · Cr Capital A/c ₹80,000`. Plain words: "The year's profit of ₹80,000 is added to Ramesh's capital." (The profit-transfer entry is the textbook rule written as a journal by me.)

### 1.6 Interest on the owner's own capital is optional, and it nets to nothing
A proprietor who wants to know the profit after allowing himself interest on capital may charge it: interest is worked on the opening capital (and on any addition from the date it came in) and treated as an expense of the business. It is credited to Capital, so the lower profit and the higher capital cancel out.
**Source:** NCERT 11-II ch. 9 §9.12 *Interest on Capital* (keac202 file pp.21-22) — fetched
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Interest on Capital A/c ₹600 · Cr Capital A/c ₹600` (the textbook's own figures: 5% on ₹12,000). Plain words: "Ramesh gave his business 5% interest on his ₹12,000 capital, ₹600; profit is ₹600 lower, capital is ₹600 higher."

### 1.7 Why money from the owner's pocket or money taken out is not profit
Profit is the excess of the period's revenues (income) over its related expenses. Capital brought in is a capital receipt (an obligation to the owner), and drawings reduce capital; neither is a revenue or an expense, so neither enters the profit calculation. A "gain", by contrast, is incidental profit from events such as the sale of a fixed asset.
**Source:** NCERT 11-I ch. 1 §1.3.2 (keac101 p.12-13) and §1.5.10 *Profit* "The excess of revenues of a period over its related expenses… Profit increases the investment of the owners" (keac101; section headings 1.5.10 and 1.5.11 *Gain*, page not recorded); NCERT 11-II ch. 8 §8.2.2 (keac201 p.5) — fetched
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Cash A/c ₹20,000 · Cr Capital A/c ₹20,000` (not `Cr Sales`/`Cr Income`). Plain words: "Ramesh put ₹20,000 of his own savings into the shop. That is his money going in, not what the shop earned." (If the money were a loan from the brother it would be a liability, a different rule, outside this topic.)

### 1.8 Owner's current account alongside capital, and what the owner's note must show
The ICAI exposure draft lays out the balance sheet with an *Owners' Fund* section that has two lines: *Owners'/Partners' Capital Account* and *Owners'/Partners' Current Account*. It then requires a note for each owner's capital account and each current account showing separately: opening balance, capital introduced during the year, remuneration for the year, interest for the year, withdrawals during the year, share of profit or loss (percentage and amount), closing balance. The note's rows are named "Partner / Proprietor / Owner", so the format covers a sole proprietor as well as a partner.
**Source:** ICAI ED-NCE, Part I Balance Sheet format (Owners' Fund line), General Instructions for the Balance Sheet, note 6A *Owners Funds* (PDF p.21); Notes 3a and 3b (PDF pp.52-53) — fetched (exposure draft, 7 Feb 2023; final Guidance Note not fetched)
**Example.** Professional: for a proprietor the note reads opening `₹4,00,000`, introduced `₹1,00,000`, remuneration `nil`, interest `₹0`, withdrawals `(₹60,000)`, profit `₹80,000`, closing `₹4,20,000 + ₹1,00,000 − ₹60,000 + ₹80,000 = ₹5,20,000` (arithmetic mine on the draft's columns; the draft does not give a worked proprietor example). Plain words: "At 31 March Ramesh's account shows what he started the year with, what he added, what he took out, and what the year earned."

### 1.9 The owner's personal book and the business book
The sources I could read describe only the business's side: capital in, drawings out, profit added (1.1 to 1.6). They do not describe a personal book of the owner at all, so how the owner's own book records "money put into / taken from my business" is not taught in these sources. NCERT states only the negative: the owner's personal transactions are not recorded in the business books unless business funds move.
**Source:** NCERT 11-I ch. 2 §2.2.1 (keac102 p.3-4) — fetched. Nothing found on a personal book in NCERT 11-I/II or the ICAI exposure draft — see §3.

### 1.10 A household's own account
None of the sources I read (NCERT Class 11 Parts I and II, NCERT Class 12 Part I chapters 1-2, the ICAI exposure draft) teaches accounts for a household or a personal budget. The Class 11 chapters treat the owner's personal use only as capital and drawings of a business. What a household's statement shows and how it names its result (saving versus spending more than came in) is therefore not settled by these sources.
**Source:** not found in the sources fetched; Grewal / Goel not fetched; ICAI BoS Foundation chapters 1, 2, 7, 9 read 20 Oct 2026 — no household account (see §2) — see §3.

### 1.11 The BoS on drawings: a reduction of capital, what counts as drawings, and goods taken for own use
Drawings "are not expenses for the firm but reduction of capital" and are debited to the proprietor's capital account (through a Drawings account), not to the profit and loss account; if the proprietor enjoys a benefit personally, such as the use of the firm's car, a suitable amount is treated as drawings and the charge to profit and loss is reduced. Premium on the proprietor's own life policy is a personal expense debited to capital. Goods taken for personal use: `Drawings A/c Dr · To Purchases A/c`. The balance sheet shows capital plus net profit less drawings. For an owner's mixed book the BoS lists what counts as drawings: household expenses from business cash; rent of premises used for both residence and business; common electricity and telephone bills; life insurance premiums of the proprietor or partners from business cash; private loans to friends and relatives out of business cash; personal gifts; goods or services taken for personal consumption; cash withdrawals for family expenses; and debtors' collections used for personal expenses. An expense that benefits both the owner and the business is split on an equitable basis (for example rent by the area he lives in). Receipts that belong to the owner (interest on a private investment, a legacy, money he contributes) are credited to his capital. All personal income and expense must be kept apart from the business's. The BoS states no value for goods taken (the credit to Purchases implies cost, but it is not said).
**Source:** ICAI BoS, Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 7 Unit 1 §1.2(a), p. 7.4; §1.4 item (i) *Drawings*, p. 7.19; §1.5 item 3, p. 7.31; Illustration 6, p. 7.41; answers 15 and 19, pp. 7.67–7.68; Ch. 9 §4.1 items (1) and (4), p. 9.15; §4.4, p. 9.22 — fetched (ICAI BoS PDF) https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74616bos60479-fnd-cp7-u1.pdf; https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74620bos60479-fnd-cp9.pdf. Study material, not a pronouncement.
**Example.** Professional: cash taken for the house `Dr Drawings A/c ₹6,000 · Cr Cash A/c ₹6,000`; goods taken home `Dr Drawings A/c ₹800 · Cr Purchases A/c ₹800`; at the year end `Dr Capital A/c ₹6,800 · Cr Drawings A/c ₹6,800`. Plain words: "Took ₹6,000 from the till for the house, and ₹800 of goods; at year end both come off my capital."

### 1.12 Interest on the proprietor's capital and on his drawings: the BoS shows where each goes
The BoS lists "interest on capital, i.e., the interest on proprietor's investment in the business" among the adjusting entries. In the summary of adjustments, interest on capital is a debit in the profit and loss account and is *added to capital* on the balance sheet; interest on drawings is a credit in the profit and loss account and is *deducted from capital*. Items to be debited or credited to the proprietor, such as interest on drawings, interest allowed on capital and charges for services rendered to him personally, are shown apart from other items. The BoS says nothing of a salary to a sole proprietor.
**Source:** ICAI BoS, Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 2 Unit 4 §4.7(v)(c), p. 2.97; Ch. 7 Unit 1 §1.4, p. 7.19; summary of adjustments, items 10–11, p. 7.57 — fetched (ICAI BoS PDF) https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74616bos60479-fnd-cp7-u1.pdf; https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74609bos60479-fnd-cp2-u4.pdf. Study material, not a pronouncement.
**Example.** Professional (own figures): 6% on capital of ₹1,00,000 allowed to the proprietor: `Dr Interest on Capital A/c ₹6,000 · Cr Capital A/c ₹6,000`. Plain words: "I allow myself 6% on the ₹1,00,000 I have put in: ₹6,000 is charged to the shop's profit and added to my capital."

## 2. Where the sources disagree, or are silent
- **Drawings account vs direct debit to Capital:** NCERT 11-I teaches a separate Drawings account closed to Capital at year end (ch. 4 §4.6 item 4); NCERT 12-I (partnership, fluctuating capital) debits drawings in the partner's capital account (Fig. 1.2); the ICAI exposure draft's Notes 3a/3b list "withdrawals during the year" as a movement column and do not require a Drawings account. The sources agree on the effect (capital reduced) and differ only on whether a separate account is kept.
- **Proprietor's salary or remuneration:** NCERT 11-I/II do not discuss any salary to a sole proprietor. The ICAI exposure draft has a "remuneration for the year" column and a P&L line *Partners' remuneration* ("wherever applicable") but gives no rule for a proprietor.
- **Drawings in kind at cost or at selling price:** the NCERT examples value the goods at cost; no source I read states a rule.
- **Personal book of the owner:** silent in all sources read.
- **Household accounts:** silent in all sources read.
- **Interest on proprietor's capital:** NCERT 11-II §9.12 treats it as an expense that is credited back to capital; the ICAI draft lists "interest on partners'/members' capital" as a finance cost (General Instructions for P&L, item 4) and names no proprietor interest.
- **BoS Foundation Ch. 7 and Ch. 9 read (20 Oct 2026).** The BoS keeps drawings in a separate Drawings account closed to capital (Ch. 7 Unit 1 p. 7.19; balance sheet p. 7.41), which agrees with NCERT 11-I and differs from the "withdrawals" column of the ICAI draft; for a *partner* it allows either a debit to capital or a separate drawings/current account (Ch. 10 Unit 1 §1.7, p. 10.14). It still describes only the business's side: nothing on a sole proprietor's personal book or on a household's own account (no hit for "household" outside the drawings list), and nothing on a salary to a proprietor.

## 4. Answers to the GAPS items this topic settles
- **C2 (one-owner business with one owner's account, no Drawings account):** NCERT 11 teaches Capital plus a Drawings account closed to Capital at year end (ch. 4 §4.6(4)); the ICAI exposure draft shows Capital plus a Current Account with a withdrawals column (Notes 3a/3b). A single owner account with withdrawals debited directly is the fluctuating-capital shape NCERT 12-I teaches for partners (Fig 1.2) but is not taught for a sole proprietor in what I read; not settled, see §3.
- **C3 (household statements, result "Saving", money from the family or the owner's business is not income):** not settled, see §3. The part that is supported: capital introduced and drawings are not revenue or expense (1.7; NCERT 11-I §1.5.10, 11-II §8.2.2).
- **A11(2) (owner's book and the business it owns):** the business side is settled (the business records the owner's money as Capital and withdrawals as Drawings, 1.1 to 1.4); the owner's side is not taught, see §3.
- **D13:** its text was not read (tracing only); the findings above answer the topic's questions.
