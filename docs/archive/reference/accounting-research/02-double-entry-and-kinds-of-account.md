# 02 — Double entry and kinds of account

**Questions (from the outline):** Personal, real and nominal accounts, with their golden rules. The modern five types: asset, liability, capital, income, expense. How the two classifications map onto each other. The natural balance of each type.
**Settles (tracing only):** catalogue (v3 §1.7)

How the sources were read. NCERT and ICAI BoS PDFs were downloaded and the full text read (`fetched`). Page numbers are those printed in the book where they could be seen; otherwise only the section number is given.

## 1. Findings

### 1.1 Double entry: every transaction has two effects, and debits equal credits
Every transaction has two effects, receiving a benefit and giving a benefit. Each transaction therefore touches at least two accounts, and the total debited equals the total credited. A debit is an entry on the left side of an account, a credit on the right (T-account). Double entry is called a complete system because both aspects are recorded; the trial balance checks arithmetic; it "can be implemented by big as well as small organisations".
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Financial Accounting I, ch. 2 §2.3 (p. 36) and ch. 3 §3.3 (p. 53–54) — fetched. ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1, Module 1 ch. 2 unit 1 §1.1 and §1.2 — fetched.
**Example.** Professional: Rohit starts business with ₹5,00,000 cash: `Dr Cash A/c ₹5,00,000 · Cr Rohit's Capital A/c ₹5,00,000`. Plain words: "Rohit put ₹5,00,000 cash into the business."

### 1.2 The accounting equation
Assets equal liabilities plus capital: A = L + C, so A − L = C and A − C = L. It is also called the balance sheet equation. Resources of the business equal the claims of those who financed them; the owner's claim is capital, outsiders' claims are liabilities. Every transaction leaves the equation in balance. ICAI BoS states it holds "at all points of time and for any number of transactions … except when there are errors".
**Source:** NCERT ch. 3 §3.2 (p. 50–52) — fetched. ICAI BoS Module 1 ch. 2 unit 1 §1.6 — fetched.
**Example.** Professional: furniture bought for ₹60,000 by cheque: `Dr Furniture A/c ₹60,000 · Cr Bank A/c ₹60,000` (one asset up, another down; A unchanged). Plain words: "Bought furniture ₹60,000, paid by cheque."

### 1.3 The traditional classification: personal, real and nominal
ICAI BoS divides accounts into personal and impersonal. Personal accounts relate to persons: natural (Ram, Rita), artificial or legal (government, companies, clubs, co-operative societies), and representative (not in a person's name but standing for one, e.g. outstanding liability, prepaid, capital, drawings). Impersonal accounts are real (assets of the firm "but not debt": land, building, investment, fixed deposit, cash in hand, cash at bank) and nominal (expenses, losses, gains, revenue: salary, interest paid, commission received). ICAI BoS says the capital account is the proprietor's and so personal, but profit and loss adjustments are made in it. Nominal accounts are temporary: their net result goes to profit and loss, and then to capital.
**Source:** ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1, Module 1 ch. 2 unit 1 §1.7.1 — fetched.
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Rent A/c ₹8,000 · Cr Cash A/c ₹8,000` — rent is nominal, cash is real. `Dr Ramesh A/c ₹2,000 · Cr Cash A/c ₹2,000` — Ramesh is personal, cash real. Plain words: "Paid ₹8,000 rent in cash." / "Paid ₹2,000 to Ramesh in cash."

### 1.4 The golden rules of the traditional classification
Personal account: debit the receiver, credit the giver. Real account: debit what comes in, credit what goes out. Nominal account: debit expenses and losses, credit income and gains.
**Source:** ICAI BoS Module 1 ch. 2 unit 1 §1.7.2 (printed p. 2.18) — fetched. NCERT Class 11 teaches debit and credit by the five-type rules (1.5) and does not state these three rules in the chapters read.
**Example.** Professional: purchase of goods on credit from Sumit Traders ₹55,000, and a ₹10,000 advance in cash to Ramesh for machinery: `Dr Purchases A/c ₹55,000 · Cr Sumit Traders A/c ₹55,000` (nominal debited as an expense; personal credited as giver). `Dr Ramesh A/c ₹10,000 · Cr Cash A/c ₹10,000` (receiver debited; what goes out credited). Plain words: "Bought goods ₹55,000 on credit from Sumit Traders." / "Paid Ramesh ₹10,000 cash in advance."

### 1.5 The modern classification: five types and their rules
All accounts fall into five categories: asset, liability, capital, expense or loss, revenue or gain. Increase in an asset is debited and decrease credited; increase in an expense or loss is debited and decrease credited. Increase in a liability, in capital, and in revenue or gain is credited, and decrease is debited. ICAI BoS gives the same table with a "normal balance" column and adds a sixth line, a withdrawals (drawings) account, normal balance debit, debited when it increases.
**Source:** NCERT ch. 3 §3.3.1 (p. 54) — fetched. ICAI BoS Module 1 ch. 2 unit 1 §1.8 — fetched.
**Example.** Professional, one entry per type: `Dr Cash A/c ₹5,000 · Cr Sales A/c ₹5,000` (asset up, revenue up); `Dr Wages A/c ₹1,000 · Cr Cash A/c ₹1,000` (expense up, asset down); `Dr Cash A/c ₹20,000 · Cr Loan from Kapoor Traders A/c ₹20,000` (asset up, liability up); `Dr Drawings A/c ₹3,000 · Cr Cash A/c ₹3,000` (capital reduced by owner's withdrawal). Plain words: "Sold goods ₹5,000 for cash." / "Paid wages ₹1,000." / "Borrowed ₹20,000 from Kapoor Traders." / "Owner took ₹3,000 for home."

### 1.6 How the two classifications map onto each other
Neither NCERT nor ICAI BoS prints a mapping table. Putting the ICAI BoS definitions and its worked example side by side:

| Traditional | Modern type | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Personal: a debtor | Asset | BoS §1.7.1 (trade receivable); NCERT ch. 3 illustration (Rajani Enterprises "as debtors" under assets) |
| Personal: a creditor, a lender | Liability | BoS §1.7.1; NCERT ch. 3 (Ramjee Lal, Sumit Traders as creditors, liabilities) |
| Personal: capital | Capital | BoS §1.7.1 and §1.8 |
| Personal (representative): outstanding expense, prepaid | Liability (outstanding), asset (prepaid) | BoS §1.7.1(c) lists both as representative personal; BoS ch. 1 unit 2 timing table gives payable and advance |
| Personal (representative): drawings | Drawings (reduces capital) | BoS §1.7.1(c) and §1.8 |
| Real | Asset | BoS §1.7.1(ii)(a) |
| Nominal: expense or loss | Expense | BoS §1.7.1(b) and §1.8 |
| Nominal: gain or revenue | Revenue (income) | BoS §1.7.1(b) and §1.8 |

This table is our reading of the two classifications; it is not printed in either source. Section 2 lists where the sources themselves do not agree.
**Source:** ICAI BoS Module 1 ch. 2 unit 1 §1.7.1, §1.8; ch. 1 unit 2; NCERT ch. 3 §3.2–3.3 — fetched (mapping is ours).
**Example.** Professional: the same entry both ways. `Dr Bank A/c ₹50,00,000 · Cr Capital A/c ₹50,00,000` — traditional: bank (receiver), capital (giver); modern: asset up (debit), capital up (credit). Plain words: "Started business with ₹50,00,000 in the bank." (BoS §1.7.2 worked example.)

### 1.7 Natural (normal) balance of each type
Asset: debit. Expense or loss: debit. Drawings: debit. Liability: credit. Capital: credit. Revenue: credit. The permanent accounts (assets, liabilities, capital) are balanced and carried forward and appear in the balance sheet; the temporary accounts (revenue and expense) are closed into trading and profit and loss at year end. "Nominal accounts are not balanced; the balance in the end are transferred to the profit and loss account."
**Source:** ICAI BoS Module 1 ch. 2 unit 1 §1.8 and unit 2 §2.4 — fetched. NCERT ch. 3 §3.5.1 (p. 75) — fetched.
**Example.** Professional: Sumit Traders supplies goods ₹55,000 on credit; the business pays ₹20,000: `Dr Purchases A/c ₹55,000 · Cr Sumit Traders A/c ₹55,000`; `Dr Sumit Traders A/c ₹20,000 · Cr Cash A/c ₹20,000`. Sumit Traders then shows a credit balance of ₹35,000. Plain words: "Bought ₹55,000 goods from Sumit Traders on credit; paid ₹20,000; still owe ₹35,000."

### 1.8 A compound entry
When more than two accounts are affected, the entry is compound, and total debits must equal total credits. NCERT's example: office furniture ₹25,000, ₹5,000 paid in cash, ₹20,000 payable.
**Source:** NCERT ch. 3 §3.4.1 (p. 62–63) — fetched. ICAI BoS Module 1 ch. 2 unit 1 §1.9.2 — fetched.
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Office Furniture A/c ₹25,000 · Cr Cash A/c ₹5,000 · Cr Modern Furniture A/c ₹20,000`. Plain words: "Bought office furniture ₹25,000 from Modern Furniture; paid ₹5,000 cash, owe ₹20,000."

### 1.9 What counts as an expense or an asset: goods bought for sale
NCERT and ICAI BoS debit goods bought for sale to a Purchases account, treated as an expense, not to a "Goods" asset account; Goods is divided into purchases, sales, purchase returns, sales returns and stock accounts. (How stock is valued is outside this note.)
**Source:** NCERT ch. 3 §3.4.1 (p. 62) and ch. 3 worked transaction 5 (p. 56) — fetched.
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Purchases A/c ₹30,000 · Cr Govind Traders A/c ₹30,000`. Plain words: "Bought goods ₹30,000 from Govind Traders on credit."

## 2. Where the sources disagree, or are silent
- **Capital: liability or its own type.** NCERT §3.3.1 lists capital as its own category. ICAI BoS §1.8 table also lists it separately, but BoS's own "modern approach" worked example labels Capital as "Liability". BoS §1.7.1 calls it personal.
- **Bank: personal or real.** BoS §1.7.1(ii)(a) says cash at bank is a real account; BoS's own traditional worked example (§1.7.2) labels Bank "Personal" (receiver/giver). The two cannot both hold.
- **Drawings.** NCERT's answer key to "Test Your Understanding I", item 4, places Drawings (item 9) under Expense; NCERT's own answer 2 says capital "decreases by drawings"; BoS §1.8 gives drawings a separate "withdraw account", debit. Three treatments of one account.
- **Golden rules for personal accounts.** BoS (traditional) defines them; NCERT Class 11 does not state the three traditional rules in the chapters read.
- **Mapping table.** Not printed in any source read (see 1.6).
- **Catalogue.** No source prints a full list of account heads; only the examples named in section 4 are shown.

## 4. Answers to the GAPS items this topic settles
- **Catalogue (v3 §1.7).** The sources give the types, not a fixed catalogue. Heads named in the worked examples read: Assets — Cash, Bank, Debtors, Stock, Furniture, Plant and Machinery, Land, Building, Investments, Fixed Deposits, Prepaid expenses (NCERT ch. 3; BoS §1.7.1, §1.6). Liabilities — Creditors, Loans (short and long term), Bank overdraft, Outstanding expenses (BoS §1.6; ch. 9 statement of affairs). Capital — Capital, Drawings (BoS §1.8). Income — Sales, Rent received, Interest received, Commission received (NCERT ch. 3; BoS §1.7.1). Expenses — Purchases, Wages, Salaries, Rent, Interest paid, Stationery (NCERT ch. 3; BoS §1.7.1). The choice of heads beyond these is not settled by the sources.
