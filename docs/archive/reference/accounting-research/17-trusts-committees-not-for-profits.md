# 17 — Trusts, committees, not-for-profits

**Questions (from the outline):** Receipts & Payments, Income & Expenditure, Balance Sheet. General fund vs corpus. Earmarked and restricted funds, and the investments made from them. Spending from a fund on an asset vs on an expense. Subscriptions. Donations in kind. The gollak (donation box).
**Settles (tracing only):** GAPS A4, E1–E6; F-08; D11

Main sources, both read in full text:
- **BoS** = ICAI Board of Studies, Foundation *Principles and Practice of Accounting*, Ch. 8 *Financial Statements of Not-for-Profit Organisations* (2022-23 edition; study material, not a pronouncement) — https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74619bos60479-fnd-cp8.pdf — fetched.
- **TG** = ICAI *Technical Guide on Accounting for Not-for-Profit Organisations (NPOs)*, third edition June 2023 (a copy hosted at https://caalley.com/icaipub/74188asb60123.pdf; ICAI-hosted copies at pdicai.org and kb.icai.org could not be opened) — fetched.

The **current NCERT Class 12 Part I (reprint 2026-27) has no not-for-profit chapter**: its chapters are partnership and dissolution (leac101–leac104 were read). The NPO chapter of the older NCERT edition (ciet.nic.in/moocspdf/Accountancy 03/leac_10101_e-text.pdf) could not be reached — not fetched. The outline's NCERT source for this topic is therefore not used. The two ICAI sources **disagree on how spending from a specific fund is posted** (§1.7); this is the most important point in the note.

## 1. Findings

### 1.1 Three statements, and what each one is
- **Receipts and Payments Account:** a summary of the cash book with no date column; receipts on the left, payments on the right; opens with the cash and bank balances and ends with them; includes receipts and payments of every period and of capital as well as revenue. It cannot show surplus or deficit. It is "usually not a part of the double entry system".
- **Income and Expenditure Account:** the counterpart of a profit and loss account; revenue items of the current period only, on the accrual principle; capital receipts and capital expenditure excluded; no opening balance; the balance is **surplus** (income over expenditure) or **deficit**, carried to the capital/accumulated fund.
- **Balance Sheet:** assets and liabilities at a date; the excess of assets over outside liabilities is the **Capital Fund** (or **Accumulated Fund** if members have not contributed capital). An Income and Expenditure Account "must always be accompanied by a Balance Sheet".
**Source:** BoS Ch. 8, §2, §2.1, §3, §3.2, §3.3, §4 (pp. 8.3–8.11) — fetched. TG paras 100–103 (income and expenditure account and balance sheet; integrated balance sheet, no set-off) — fetched.
**Example.** The R&P Account is a summary, not a journal entry. Professional (the entries that feed it): `Dr Cash A/c ₹3,000 · Cr Membership Fees A/c ₹3,000`. Plain words: "Received ₹3,000 membership fee from Kapoor." The year's surplus: `Dr Income and Expenditure A/c ₹57,000 · Cr Capital Fund A/c ₹57,000` ("Surplus for the year ₹57,000 added to the Capital Fund", BoS §3.3(iv); Illustration 4 shows an excess of income over expenditure of ₹57,000).

### 1.2 Basis: Receipts and Payments is cash; Income and Expenditure is accrual; the Guide recommends accrual for all
The cash basis records donations and grants when received and expenses when paid; its end product is a statement of receipts and payments, and a statement of assets and liabilities "may or may not" be prepared. The accrual basis relates income and expense to the period they belong to. The Guide recommends that **all** NPOs, corporate or not, keep accrual books; any other basis must be disclosed. BoS says the R&P Account "is not favoured except where the activities … are simple and modest, involve no carry over from one period to the next and it has no assets, apart from cash balance and no liabilities".
**Source:** TG paras 18–24 and 114; BoS Ch. 8 §2.1 — fetched.
**Example.** Professional (accrual): `Dr Subscription Outstanding A/c ₹500 · Cr Subscriptions A/c ₹500`. Plain words: "₹500 of this year's subscriptions is still to come in; counted as this year's income." On the cash basis the same ₹500 would appear only in the year it is received.

### 1.3 Capital Fund (general fund) and corpus
BoS: Capital Fund is what members contributed through legacies, special donations, entrance fees, and accumulated surplus; with no member contributions it is the Accumulated Fund. TG: **Corpus** is the non-reducible fund contributed by the **founders or promoters**, and may be increased only by further contribution by them; **General Fund** is all other unrestricted funds not designated and not corpus; surplus or deficit goes to the General Fund. The Guide's unrestricted funds are three: corpus, designated, general.
**Source:** BoS Ch. 8 §4 (p. 8.10–8.11); TG para 106(a)(i)–(iii) and 109 — fetched.
**Example.** Professional (opening): `Dr Cash A/c ₹10,00,000 · Cr Corpus A/c ₹10,00,000` (founders' gift). Plain words: "Founders gave ₹10,00,000 to start the trust."

### 1.4 Specific-purpose money is held in a fund of its own (fund accounting)
A donation raised for a stated purpose (the donor's declared intention) is credited to a **special/specific fund account**, not to income; without a stated purpose it goes to the Capital Fund (BoS §4.1.1). A capital receipt is credited to the capital fund or a special-purpose fund such as a Building Fund (BoS §3.1). TG: restricted funds are credited to a separate fund account when received and shown separately in the balance sheet. BoS adds a naming rule: "Fund" strictly applies when the money is invested (Scholarship Fund, Prize Fund); otherwise "Account" is more apt (Building Account, Tournament Account).
**Source:** BoS Ch. 8 §3.1 (p. 8.5–8.6), §4.1.1 (p. 8.11); TG paras 106(b), 110 — fetched.
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Cash A/c ₹1,00,000 · Cr Community Hall Fund A/c ₹1,00,000`. Plain words: "Received ₹1,00,000 from Sharma for the community hall."

### 1.5 Earmarked (designated) funds are different from restricted funds
TG: **designated (earmarked) funds** are unrestricted money the trustees set aside for a purpose or future commitment; the designation is self-imposed and can be lifted at will. **Restricted funds** carry conditions set by the contributor (or law); **endowment funds** are restricted funds whose capital must not be used, only the income. A designated fund is created by appropriating the year's surplus.
**Source:** TG paras 106(a)(ii), 106(b), 107 — fetched.
**Example.** Professional (appropriation to a designated fund): `Dr Income and Expenditure A/c ₹20,000 · Cr Hall Repair Reserve (Designated) A/c ₹20,000`. Plain words: "Trustees set aside ₹20,000 of this year's surplus for hall repairs." (The Guide describes the appropriation in words, not as a journal entry; the entry is written out from para 107.)

### 1.6 Specific-purpose donations in the Balance Sheet
BoS gives a second treatment of the same item in its "other concepts" list: a **specific donation** (for a building, library books) "should be capitalized and shown on the liabilities side of the balance sheet"; a **general donation** goes to the credit of the Income and Expenditure Account. TG: general-use income is unrestricted.
**Source:** BoS Ch. 8 "Other concepts" items 1(a), 1(b) (p. 8.13); Illustration 4 (donations ₹55,000 credited in the I&E account) — fetched.
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Cash A/c ₹55,000 · Cr Donations A/c ₹55,000` (general, to income). Plain words: "Received ₹55,000 in general donations."

### 1.7 How spending from a specific fund is posted — two ICAI sources, two methods
**Method A, charged to the fund (BoS).** Income from fund investments and donations collected for the purpose are credited to an account indicating the purpose, and "the expenditure incurred in carrying out the purpose of the fund is debited to this account. On no account any such expense is charged to the Income and Expenditure Account." The match-fund illustration shows the fund reduced by the spending and, where spending exceeds the fund, the excess ₹5,000 (₹1,10,000 − ₹1,05,000) goes to I&E. Endowment: "All the expenses should be deducted from that fund on the liabilities side."
**Method B, through the Income and Expenditure Account with a transfer (TG).** For a **restricted** fund meant for revenue spending, when the expense is incurred it is charged to the I&E Account (restricted-funds column) and "a corresponding amount is transferred from the concerned restricted fund account to the credit of the income and expenditure account" (same column). Result: the expense is visible in I&E, the fund falls by the same amount, and the surplus on that fund is nil. The same two steps apply to a **designated** fund (para 107). The Guide recommends an I&E account with three columns: unrestricted, restricted, total (para 101).
**Source:** BoS Ch. 8 §4.1.1 (p. 8.11), "Other concepts" item 5, Illustration 5 (p. 8.16); TG paras 101, 107, 110 — fetched.
**Example (spending ₹30,000 on hall repairs from the Hall Fund).**
Method A. Professional: `Dr Community Hall Fund A/c ₹30,000 · Cr Cash A/c ₹30,000`. Plain words: "Paid ₹30,000 for hall repairs, out of the Hall Fund."
Method B. Professional: (1) `Dr Hall Repairs (I&E, Restricted) ₹30,000 · Cr Cash A/c ₹30,000`; (2) `Dr Community Hall Fund A/c ₹30,000 · Cr Income and Expenditure A/c (Restricted) ₹30,000`. Plain words: "Paid ₹30,000 for hall repairs" and "Took ₹30,000 out of the Hall Fund to meet it."
**What BoS Illustration 12 shows (Republic College).** The solution charges research salaries and materials (₹1,20,000 + ₹1,50,000) to I&E and still shows Research Fund ₹8,00,000, unchanged, with a Research Fund Investment of ₹8,00,000. That fits neither method cleanly; read with Method A's "on no account" sentence it is an inconsistency inside the BoS chapter. It is recorded here as read, not resolved.

### 1.8 Fund investments and their income
BoS: investments bought from a special fund are disclosed separately; income from them is **credited to the fund** (4.1.1). TG: it **depends on the terms**: if the contributor says the income is for the same purpose, credit the fund; if general use, credit I&E as General Fund (para 111). For **endowment** funds the income is recognised in I&E only as far as the related expense is incurred, the rest stays in the fund (para 112). For **designated** funds the income goes to I&E, and an equal amount may be transferred to the fund as the NPO's policy decides (para 108). BoS Illustration 12 shows "Interest and Dividends on Investments ₹1,85,000" taken wholly to I&E, with fund investments listed separately; it does not split fund income from other income.
**Source:** BoS Ch. 8 §4.1.1 (p. 8.11), "Other concepts" item 5, Illustration 12; TG paras 108, 111, 112 — fetched.
**Example.** Professional: Hall Fund investment earns ₹6,000 interest; terms say use it for the hall. `Dr Bank A/c ₹6,000 · Cr Community Hall Fund A/c ₹6,000` (BoS 4.1.1; TG 111, same-purpose terms). If the terms allow general use: `Dr Bank A/c ₹6,000 · Cr Interest Income A/c ₹6,000`. Plain words: "Interest of ₹6,000 on the hall fixed deposit came in; added to the Hall Fund." / "…counted as ordinary income."

### 1.9 Buying an asset from a fund
Expenditure to acquire a fixed asset is capitalised; only the annual depreciation is revenue expenditure (BoS §3.1). Assets bought out of a special fund are "disclosed separately" (BoS §4.1.1). TG gives a full rule for a restricted fund meant for capital spending: the asset account is debited and depreciated (AS 10); the fund is treated as **deferred income** to the extent of the asset's cost and credited to I&E (restricted column) each year in proportion to the depreciation charged, so the unamortised part stays in the fund; any fund balance above the asset's cost may have to be refunded, or, if the donor does not require it, becomes income; for a **non-depreciable** asset (land) the fund moves to Capital Reserve when the asset is bought. For a designated fund meant for capital spending the asset is debited and the fund balance is transferred to I&E (para 107). The R&P illustration (Swaraj Club) keeps the pavilion's cost of ₹11,000 out of the I&E account, against the pavilion donation of ₹10,000.
**Source:** BoS Ch. 8 §3.1, §4.1.1, Illustration 1 (p. 8.3–8.4); TG paras 107, 110 — fetched.
**Example.** Professional: hall built for ₹5,00,000 from the Hall Fund. `Dr Hall Building A/c ₹5,00,000 · Cr Bank A/c ₹5,00,000`. Under TG each year with depreciation of ₹25,000: `Dr Depreciation (I&E, Restricted) ₹25,000 · Cr Hall Building A/c ₹25,000` and `Dr Community Hall Fund A/c ₹25,000 · Cr I&E (Restricted) ₹25,000`. Plain words: "Paid ₹5,00,000 to build the hall." Each year: "Wrote off ₹25,000 of the hall's value and moved ₹25,000 out of the Hall Fund to match."

### 1.10 Subscriptions: income of the period they belong to
A subscription account is set up to find the amount that belongs to the year: cash received, less amounts for earlier years (previously outstanding), less amounts received in advance for later years, plus amounts still due for this year. Outstanding subscription is an **asset**; subscription received in advance is a **liability**. Illustration 2 worked: cash received ₹42,000, of which ₹1,600 is for the previous year and ₹600 for the next, ₹3,000 still due → ₹42,800 to I&E.
**Source:** BoS Ch. 8 §4.1.3 (p. 8.11–8.12) with its journal entries; Illustration 2 (p. 8.8–8.9); Illustrations 6 and 7 (p. 8.17–8.18) — fetched.
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Cash A/c ₹42,000 · Cr Subscriptions A/c ₹42,000`; `Dr Subscription Outstanding A/c ₹3,000 · Cr Subscriptions A/c ₹3,000`; `Dr Subscriptions A/c ₹2,200 · Cr Outstanding Subscription (previous year) A/c ₹1,600 · Cr Subscriptions Received in Advance A/c ₹600`. Plain words: "Members paid ₹42,000. ₹3,000 more is due for this year. Of what came in, ₹1,600 was last year's dues and ₹600 was next year's paid early."

### 1.11 Entrance fees and life membership fees are capital by default
Entrance (admission) fees are normally capital receipts credited to the Capital Fund, unless small, or the rules treat them as income; "if the question is silent then always take it to be capital". Life membership fees are capital receipts added to the capital or general fund; three methods may spread them: (1) hold until the member dies, then to the Accumulated Fund; (2) transfer each year an amount equal to the normal annual subscription to I&E; (3) transfer an amount by age and average life.
**Source:** BoS Ch. 8 §4.1.2 (p. 8.11), §4.1.4 (p. 8.13), "Other concepts" items 2 and 4 (p. 8.13); Illustration 4 (p. 8.15; 50% of entrance fees capitalised) — fetched. (Page range corrected 20 Oct 2026: §4.1.2 is on p. 8.11, not 8.12–8.14.)
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Cash A/c ₹2,500 · Cr Entrance Fees (Capital Fund) A/c ₹2,500`. Plain words: "New member Verma paid ₹2,500 to join; kept as part of the capital."

### 1.12 Donations in kind
BoS: if a donor gives a security or other readily realisable asset instead of cash, "the value of asset on valuation, must be credited to the fund for which the amount has been donated" (4.1.1); in-kind donations to educational institutions are land, buildings, shares, utensils and furniture (§5.2.1). TG: the principles for grants apply to donations; a non-monetary asset received at a concessional rate is recorded at its acquisition cost; one received **free of cost is recorded at a nominal value**; assets received in kind and not previously recorded must be identified and brought in at cost "which may be at nominal values".
**Source:** BoS Ch. 8 §4.1.1, §5.2.1; TG paras 49(b), 49(h), 135 — fetched.
**Example.** Professional (BoS): Sharma gives a cupboard valued ₹40,000 for the office. `Dr Furniture A/c ₹40,000 · Cr Capital Fund A/c ₹40,000` (no stated purpose; BoS 4.1.1). Under TG the amount would be a nominal value that the Guide does not quantify. Plain words: "Sharma gave the society a cupboard worth about ₹40,000."

### 1.13 Legacies and general donations: the sources split
BoS "Other concepts" item 3: a legacy "should be capitalised … by adding to the Capital Fund"; its multiple-choice item 3 says "General donations and legacies are credited to … Capital Fund" (the printed answer was not shown); BoS "Other concepts" 1(b) says a general donation is credited to I&E; BoS §4.1.1 says donations without a stated purpose go to the Capital Fund. TG para 106(a): "All incomes (donations, legacies, investment income, fees, etc.) not subject to external restrictions are part of unrestricted funds."
**Source:** BoS Ch. 8 §4.1.1, "Other concepts" items 1 and 3, MCQ 3 (p. 8.13, p. 8.40); TG para 106(a) — fetched.
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Bank A/c ₹2,00,000 · Cr Capital Fund A/c ₹2,00,000`. Plain words: "A legacy of ₹2,00,000 came under the late Kapoor's will."

### 1.14 A cash donation box (gollak, hundi): the accounting texts are silent
No ICAI text read mentions a donation box, collection box, gollak or hundi. A news report on the Ram Mandir trust describes a custody procedure only: boxes sealed, two keys, trust official and bank representative together, cash counted, and the amount entered in the accounts only after the bank's digital receipt. No ledger entry is described. Whether the count is income (general donation), a fund credit or a clearing account until banked is not settled.
**Source:** news24online.com, "From donation box to bank" — search (https://news24online.com/india/from-donation-box-to-bank-heres-how-ram-mandir-monitors-every-rupee-donated-by-devotees/868043/); not an accounting source.
**Example.** Professional (by analogy to general donations, BoS Illustration 4): `Dr Cash A/c ₹14,200 · Cr Donations A/c ₹14,200`. Plain words: "Opened the donation box in front of two committee members; counted ₹14,200." This is not a rule from a source; see §3.

## 2. Where the sources disagree, or are silent
- **BoS vs TG on spending from a specific fund** (§1.7): BoS says debit the fund and "on no account" the I&E; TG says charge I&E and transfer from the fund. BoS Illustration 12 is not consistent with its own text.
- **TG's position is the same in its 2009 edition** (ICAI PDF, owner-supplied copy, foreword 26 Aug 2009 — read 20 Oct 2026): ¶114, a restricted fund is credited to a separate fund account when received; revenue spending from it "is charged to the income and expenditure account ('Restricted Funds' column); a corresponding amount is transferred from the concerned restricted fund account"; ¶111 the same for designated funds; ¶105 a three-column I&E (unrestricted, restricted, total); ¶107 an integrated balance sheet with each fund's balance shown distinctly. The 2023 third edition is still cited from the third-party copy; its paragraph numbers differ from the 2009 edition's.
- **BoS vs TG on income from fund investments** (§1.8): BoS credits the fund; TG depends on the contributor's terms; BoS Illustration 12 takes it all to I&E.
- **BoS vs TG on "corpus"** (§1.3): BoS's Capital Fund includes legacies, entrance fees and surplus; TG's corpus is the founders' contribution only.
- **BoS vs TG on donations in kind** (§1.12): valuation vs nominal value.
- **BoS internal split on general donations/legacies** (§1.6, §1.13): income vs Capital Fund.
- **Silent:** donation box (§1.14); how a committee that keeps only a cash book states its position (the R&P Account is presented, but no source gives a full worked cash-only book for a temple or gurdwara); refunds of a restricted fund to the donor beyond TG para 110.
- **The outline's NCERT source** (Class 12 Part I NPO chapter) is not in the current edition; the older edition was not reached.
- **Scope:** both ICAI sources note that NPOs registered as companies follow the Companies Act format; that is a statutory format and is not used here.
- **Which ICAI document governs a trust's or committee's statements (FAQ, 20 Oct 2026).** The Guidance Note on Financial Statements of Non-Corporate Entities does not apply where ICAI has given specific guidance, naming not-for-profit entities (Q2, exception (c)); for an NPO "the Technical Guide on Accounting for NPOs is relevant" and its formats apply (Q6). The FAQ links that guide as `https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74188asb60123.pdf` — the same ICAI document id as the third-party copy this note cites, so the TG text used here is ICAI's own 2023 guide.
- **Cash basis and Receipts & Payments (TG 2009 edition).** ¶26–27: the cash basis records transactions when cash moves, and its "end-product … is a statement of receipts and payments … A statement of assets and liabilities may or may not be prepared"; ¶32: accrual "is the scientific basis" and is recommended for all NPOs, including non-company NPOs. Bears on §3 (a committee that keeps only a cash book).
- **Foundation Ch. 8 re-checked against the printed pages (20 Oct 2026).** The page and section references to BoS Ch. 8 in §1.1–§1.13 were checked against the Aug 2026 edition (https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74619bos60479-fnd-cp8.pdf): §2 (p. 8.3), §2.1 (p. 8.4: R&P alone only where activities are "simple and modest", no carry-over, "no assets, apart from cash balance and no liabilities"), §3 (p. 8.5), §4 (p. 8.10), §4.1.1 (p. 8.11), "Other concepts" (p. 8.13), Illustrations 1–12 and §5.2.3 (p. 8.31) match, except the one page range corrected in §1.11. The chapter introduction (p. 8.2) says non-profit bodies prepare the Receipts and Payments Account, the Income and Expenditure Account and a Balance Sheet as their final accounts, and "Other concepts" item 8 (p. 8.14) treats an *honorarium* as a payment to a person who is not an employee. The BoS chapter is silent on a donation box (no hit for "box", "gollak", "hundi" or "offering").

## 4. Answers to the GAPS items this topic settles
- **A4** (money kept for a purpose, the earmarked fund): a fund account of its own, credited on receipt and shown in the balance sheet (BoS §4.1.1; TG 110); how spending is posted is **two-way** (§1.7, §3 row 1).
- **E1** (gollak counted and emptied only into cash box or bank): not settled, see §3 row 8.
- **E2** (construction as an expense head so the year is a deficit): the sources say expenditure on an asset is capitalised and only depreciation is expense (BoS §3.1; TG 110, Illus. 1); an expense head is the opposite of that. Not settled for a book that does not count assets, see §3 row 3.
- **E3** (dairy donation is income): general donations as income: BoS "Other concepts" 1(b), Illus. 4; contradicted by BoS §4.1.1 and MCQ 3, §3 row 4.
- **E4** (honorarium-type payments as expense heads paid in cash): ordinary revenue expenditure (BoS §3.1 examples; honorarium defined in "Other concepts" item 8).
- **E5** (material on a store's khata, bill on credit): outstanding expenses and creditors on the accrual basis (BoS §3.3(ii); Illus. 3 salaries unpaid/prepaid).
- **E6** (subscriptions written when received; member-khata alternative): BoS §4.1.3 teaches a subscription account with outstanding and advance balances; the member-by-member khata is not described, §3 row 10.
- **F-08, D11:** not traced (the meaning of these codes was not read, per the brief). Answered by the questions in §3.
