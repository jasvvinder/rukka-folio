# 19 — Year end

**Questions (from the outline):** Trial balance. Year-end adjustments: prepaid, outstanding, accrued. Closing entries vs a computed result. Final accounts for each entity type. Balances carried forward and nominal accounts restarting at zero. Whether a trial balance with opening, period and closing columns (a working trial balance) is expected, or only closing balances.
**Settles (tracing only):** GAPS F2, F3; D9, D10

Method note. NCERT PDFs (Reprint 2026-27) were downloaded from ncert.nic.in and their text read in full for the sections cited. ICAI's Technical Guide (June 2022) and AS 1 were read at source. The ICAI Guidance Note (Aug 2023) could **not** be read (see §5, item 6). Rupee figures marked "NCERT" are the textbook's own; the others are constructed by me to show the rule and are marked "constructed".

## 1. Findings

### 1.1 The trial balance is a list of ledger balances at a date, taken at year end (and at any other chosen date)
A trial balance is a statement of the balances (or totals) of all ledger accounts, to verify the arithmetical accuracy of posting. It shows the final position of every account and the financial statements are prepared from it. It is normally prepared at the end of the accounting year, but an organisation may prepare one at the end of any chosen period (monthly, quarterly, half-yearly or annual). The format heading is "Trial Balance of ______ as on ______" with columns Account Title, L.F., Debit balance, Credit balance. Debit balances are assets, expenses and receivables; credit balances are liabilities, revenues and payables. A zero-balance account may be listed with zero in its normal column. Sundry debtors and sundry creditors may be shown as one figure each instead of person by person.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Accountancy, Ch 6 "Trial Balance and Rectification of Errors", §6.1 and Fig. 6.1, §6.3.2; Ch 8 §8.3 (Ankit's trial balance "as on March 31, 2026") — fetched.
**Example (NCERT, Ch 8 §8.3).** Professional: trial balance of Ankit as on 31 March 2026 lists Cash Dr ₹1,000, Capital Cr ₹12,000, Sales Cr ₹1,25,000, Purchases Dr ₹75,000, and so on; both columns total ₹1,62,000. Plain words: "Listed every account's balance on 31 March; the debit side and the credit side both came to ₹1,62,000, so the posting is arithmetically right."

### 1.2 Which trial-balance forms the textbooks teach: balances only is used; no opening/period/closing form appears
NCERT describes three methods: (i) Totals method — total debits and total credits of each account; (ii) Balances method — closing balance of each account; (iii) Totals-cum-balances method — four columns (debit total, credit total, debit balance, credit balance). The balances method is "the most widely used method in practice". The totals method "is not widely used in practice, as it does not help in assuring accuracy of balances … and preparation of the financial statements". The totals-cum-balances method "is also not used in practice because it is time consuming and hardly serves any additional or special purpose." None of the sources read shows a trial balance with separate opening, period-movement and closing columns, and none calls such a form expected. The Technical Guide's capital note (1.12 below) does show opening, movements and closing for owners' capital, but that is a note to the balance sheet, not a trial balance.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11, Ch 6 §6.3.1–6.3.3 — fetched. ICAI Technical Guide, Appendix B Note 3 — fetched. (ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1 Ch. 2 Unit 3, read 20 Oct 2026: the BoS shows the same one-column "Total or Balance" form and no opening/period/closing form — §1.15.)
**Example (constructed).** Professional: Rent A/c in the ledger has opening Dr ₹0, period debits ₹96,000, and so closing Dr ₹96,000. Under the balances method the trial balance shows one line: `Rent A/c  Dr ₹96,000`. Plain words: "The trial balance has one number for rent, the year's ₹96,000; it does not show opening and movement beside it."
(See §3: the sources do not say whether a three-column working form is wanted for any entity.)

### 1.3 Adjustments are made because profit is not cash received less cash paid
Under the accrual concept the year's profit is not the revenue realised in cash less the expenses paid in cash. Some receipts and payments belong partly to the previous or next year, and some income and expenses of the year are not yet in the books. The final accounts "will not reflect the true and fair view" unless these are adjusted. The adjustments are given as "additional information" alongside the trial balance. The textbook list is: closing stock, outstanding expenses, prepaid (unexpired) expenses, accrued income, income received in advance, depreciation, bad debts, provision for doubtful debts, provision for discount on debtors, manager's commission, interest on capital. (Stock, depreciation, bad debts and provisions belong to other topics; this note covers the four timing adjustments.)
**Source:** NCERT Class 11, Ch 9 "Financial Statements – II", §9.1 — fetched.
**Example (NCERT, §9.1).** Professional: insurance premium ₹1,200 paid 1 July 2016 for twelve months; year ends 31 March 2017; three months (₹300) relate to 2017-18, so insurance charged to profit and loss is ₹900. Plain words: "Paid ₹1,200 insurance in July; only nine months of it belongs to this year, so ₹900 is this year's cost and ₹300 is paid for next year."

### 1.4 Outstanding expense: charge it to the year, show it as a liability
An expense of the year that is unpaid at year end is added to that expense for the profit and loss account and appears as a current liability. Entry: Concerned expense A/c Dr, To Outstanding expense A/c.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11, Ch 9 §9.3 — fetched.
**Example (NCERT, §9.3).** Professional: `Dr Wages A/c ₹500 · Cr Wages outstanding A/c ₹500`. Wages in the trial balance ₹8,000 become ₹8,500 in the profit and loss account; net profit falls to ₹19,000; the balance sheet shows "Outstanding wages ₹500" under current liabilities. Plain words: "Still owe the worker ₹500 for this year's work; count it as this year's wages and note that I owe it."

### 1.5 Prepaid expense: take it out of the year's expense, carry it as an asset
The part of a payment whose benefit falls in the next year is deducted from that expense and carried as a current asset. Entry: Prepaid expense A/c Dr, To Concerned expense A/c.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11, Ch 9 §9.4 — fetched.
**Example (NCERT, §9.4).** Professional: `Dr Prepaid salary A/c ₹5,000 · Cr Salary A/c ₹5,000`. Salary ₹25,000 becomes ₹20,000 in profit and loss; net profit rises to ₹24,000; the balance sheet shows "Prepaid salary ₹5,000" under current assets. Plain words: "Advanced ₹5,000 salary to a new employee; that is not this year's cost, so it sits as money due back in work next year."

### 1.6 Accrued income: add it to the year's income, carry it as an asset
Income earned in the year but not received is added to the related income and shown as a current asset. Entry: Accrued income A/c Dr, To Concerned income A/c.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11, Ch 9 §9.5 — fetched.
**Example (NCERT, §9.5).** Professional: `Dr Accrued commission A/c ₹1,500 · Cr Commission A/c ₹1,500`. Commission income ₹5,000 becomes ₹6,500; net profit rises by ₹1,500 to ₹25,500; balance sheet shows "Accrued commission ₹1,500" under current assets. Plain words: "Earned ₹1,500 commission this year but not yet paid; count it as this year's income and note that it is due to me."

### 1.7 Income received in advance: take it out of the year's income, carry it as a liability
The part of income received that belongs to the next period is unearned income and is shown as a current liability. Entry: Concerned income A/c Dr, To Income received in advance A/c.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11, Ch 9 §9.6 — fetched.
**Example (NCERT, §9.6).** Professional: `Dr Rent received A/c ₹3,000 · Cr Rent received in advance A/c ₹3,000` (₹1,000 a month for April, May, June, received before 31 March). Plain words: "Tenant paid ₹3,000 in March for the next three months; it is not this year's income, and I owe him three months' use."

### 1.8 Each adjustment appears at two places: the books, and the final accounts
"All adjustments are reflected in the final accounts at two places to complete the double entry": for example outstanding wages are added to the wages expense in the profit and loss account and appear again as a liability in the balance sheet. NCERT gives a journal entry for each adjustment (1.4–1.7) and in the same chapter shows the profit and loss account built by adding or deducting the amount against the trial-balance figure (for example "Wages 8,000 + Outstanding 500 = 8,500") without posting it to the ledger first. The sources read do not say how the next year handles the item (NCERT Ch 9 is silent on reversal). An ICAI-hosted faculty deck for BoS Foundation shows a "proforma logic" with 1 April entries that reverse the year-end adjustments (Expenses payable Dr, To Expenses; Expenses Dr, To Prepaid expenses).
**Source:** NCERT Class 11, Ch 9 §9.1, §9.3 — fetched. ICAI BoS virtual-coaching deck, "9. Financial Statements of NPO", p. 118 ("For Understanding: Outstanding Entries") — fetched (a faculty slide deck hosted on live.icai.org, not the study material itself). The study material now backs the deck: ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1, Ch. 7 Unit 1 §1.4, p. 7.26 ("Next year in the beginning entries No. (1) to (5) should be reversed") — fetched (ICAI BoS PDF).
**Example (deck, p. 118; amounts constructed).** Professional: 31 Mar `Dr Rent A/c ₹4,000 · Cr Rent payable A/c ₹4,000`; 1 Apr `Dr Rent payable A/c ₹4,000 · Cr Rent A/c ₹4,000`. Plain words: "At the year end I booked ₹4,000 rent I still owed; on 1 April I cancelled that booking so that when I pay it the payment does not count twice."

### 1.9 Closing entries: expenses and incomes are transferred to Trading / Profit and Loss by journal entry
To prepare the trading and profit and loss account the balances of all revenue and expense accounts are transferred to it by closing entries: direct expenses (opening stock, purchases, wages, carriage inwards) to the debit of Trading A/c; returns inwards and outwards first netted against sales and purchases; sales to the credit of Trading A/c; other expenses and losses to the debit of Profit and Loss A/c; other incomes and gains to its credit. Each such ledger account then shows the transfer and is closed to nil. NCERT also lists these among "transfer entries" in the journal proper, "also called closing entries".
**Source:** NCERT Class 11, Ch 8 §8.4 and §8.4.2; Class 11, Ch 4 §4.6 (Journal Proper, item 4 "Transfer entries") — fetched. ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1, Module 1, Chapter 7 is titled "Preparation of Final Accounts of Sole Proprietors" and its syllabus lists "Closing Adjustment Entries"; the chapter text itself was read on 20 Oct 2026: it teaches closing entries as journal entries (§1.16).
**Example (NCERT, §8.4.2).** Professional: `Dr Trading A/c ₹83,000 · Cr Purchases A/c ₹75,000 · Cr Wages A/c ₹8,000`; `Dr Profit and Loss A/c ₹43,500 · Cr Salaries ₹25,000 · Cr Rent of building ₹13,000 · Cr Bad debts ₹4,500`; `Dr Sales A/c ₹1,25,000 · Cr Trading A/c ₹1,25,000`; `Dr Commission received A/c ₹5,000 · Cr Profit and Loss A/c ₹5,000`. Plain words: "At year end, moved the year's purchases and wages onto the trading page, the salary, rent and bad debts onto the profit page, and the sales and commission across to their pages, so those accounts start the next year empty."

### 1.10 The result and drawings are closed to the proprietor's capital
Net profit is found on the profit and loss account (net profit = gross profit + other incomes − indirect expenses) and transferred to capital: `Profit and Loss A/c Dr, To Capital A/c`; a net loss is `Capital A/c Dr, To Profit and Loss A/c`. Drawings reduce the capital account, so "the drawings account is closed by transferring its balance to his capital account", though the balance sheet shows drawings as a deduction from capital. The balance sheet format shows "Capital, Add Profit" (less drawings) as the owner's side.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11, Ch 8 §8.4.3; §8.6.2 item (8) "Drawings"; Fig. 8.7, Fig. 8.9; Ch 4 §4.6 item 4 — fetched.
**Example.** Professional (NCERT, Ch 8 Fig. 8.9): capital ₹12,000 + profit ₹4,500 = ₹16,500: `Dr Profit and Loss A/c ₹4,500 · Cr Capital A/c ₹4,500`. Drawings (constructed): Ramesh drew ₹36,000 in the year: `Dr Capital A/c ₹36,000 · Cr Drawings A/c ₹36,000`. Plain words: "The year's profit of ₹4,500 was added to Ramesh's capital; the ₹36,000 he took out for himself was taken off it."

### 1.11 Closing entries versus a computed result: the textbooks teach closing entries; none read describes a result computed without them
NCERT, which is the national baseline, teaches closing entries as the way the profit and loss account is prepared ("It is achieved by transferring the balances of revenues and expenses to the trading and profit and loss account"). It also says the statements "are prepared on the basis of trial balance and additional information", i.e. they are built from the trial balance. Of the sources read, **none** states that a result may be computed from the trial balance without the closing entries being posted, and none forbids it. The Technical Guide and AS 1 prescribe what the statements show, not how the ledger is closed. (ICAI BoS chapter text: not fetched.)
**Source:** NCERT Class 11, Ch 8 §8.3, §8.4 — fetched; ICAI Technical Guide Ch VI — fetched (silent on closing).
**Example (constructed).** Professional: a book that posts closing entries ends the year with `Dr Profit and Loss A/c ₹4,500 · Cr Capital A/c ₹4,500`; a book that only computes shows profit ₹4,500 (income ₹1,30,000 less expenses ₹1,25,500) on the statement while the ledger still carries each income and expense account open. Plain words: "Either I move the ₹4,500 profit into Ramesh's capital in the book, or I only work it out on the statement and leave the accounts as they stand."
(See §3.)

### 1.12 The balance sheet is the carry-forward; real and personal accounts continue, nominal accounts start the next year at nil
The balance sheet is a statement of ledger balances that have not been transferred to trading and profit and loss account and "are to be carried forward to the next year with the help of an opening entry made in the journal at the beginning of the next year". "The balance sheet of an accounting period becomes the opening trial balance of the next accounting period." Revenue and expense accounts are closed to nil by 1.9, so they start the next year empty. Closing stock of one year becomes the opening stock of the next. Capital is carried at the figure after adding profit (and deducting drawings), as one balance. The cash book and ledger accounts are balanced by carrying the balance down ("Balance c/d" and "Balance b/d" on the first day of the new period).
**Source:** NCERT Class 11, Ch 8 §8.6, §8.7; Ch 9 §9.2 (closing stock paragraph); Ch 4 §4.6 item 1 and §4.1.4 (balancing the cash book) — fetched.
**Example (NCERT, §8.7).** Professional: `Dr Furniture A/c ₹15,000 · Dr Debtors A/c ₹15,500 · Dr Bank A/c ₹5,000 · Dr Cash A/c ₹1,000 · Cr Capital A/c ₹16,500 · Cr 10% Long-term loan A/c ₹5,000 · Cr Creditors A/c ₹15,000`. Plain words: "On 1 April I opened the new book with what stood on the balance sheet: furniture, debtors, bank and cash on one side; capital ₹16,500, the loan and creditors on the other. Sales, wages and rent start at nil."

### 1.13 Final accounts by entity: sole trader, partnership, not-for-profit
**Sole proprietor:** Trading and Profit and Loss Account "for the year ended …" and Balance Sheet "as at …" (1.9, 1.10). NCERT notes there is no prescribed form of balance sheet for proprietary and partnership firms (the company format of Schedule III is for companies).
**Partnership:** the profit and loss account is followed by a Profit and Loss Appropriation Account, "merely an extension of the Profit and Loss Account", through which partners' salary, commission, interest on capital and interest on drawings are made and the remaining profit is shared. Under the fixed-capital method, drawings, salary, interest and profit go to the partners' current accounts and the capital stays fixed; under the fluctuating method all go to the single capital account, which may sometimes show a debit balance. Detail belongs to topic 14.
**Not-for-profit:** Receipts and Payments Account (summary of the cash book, starts with opening and ends with closing cash and bank balances, "not a part of the double entry system"), Income and Expenditure Account (accrual, equivalent to profit and loss; "does not start with any opening balance"), and Balance Sheet; the surplus or deficit is added to or deducted from the Capital/Accumulated Fund. The Income and Expenditure Account is prepared from the Receipts and Payments Account plus opening and closing balances, adjusting accrued and prepaid items. Detail belongs to topic 17.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11, Ch 8 §8.6.1; NCERT Class 12 Partnership Accounts, Ch 1 §1.4, §1.4.1, §1.5.1 (Fig. 1.3) — fetched. NPO: ICAI BoS virtual-coaching deck "9. Financial Statements of NPO", Concepts 1–7 — fetched (faculty slide deck on live.icai.org). An NCERT Class 12 chapter on not-for-profit organisations was **not found** in the 2026-27 reprint chapters fetched (leac101–leac104 are partnership, leac201–203 company).
**Example.** Partnership (NCERT, Class 12 Fig. 1.3 illustration, Illustration 1): net profit ₹35,660 less Amit's salary ₹12,000, Babu's commission ₹5,000, interest on capitals ₹7,200, plus interest on drawings ₹540, leaves ₹12,000 shared: `Dr Profit and Loss Appropriation A/c ₹12,000 · Cr Partners' Capital A/cs ₹12,000` (Amit ₹6,000, Babu ₹4,000, Charu ₹2,000). Plain words: "After paying the partners their salary, commission and interest, ₹12,000 was left; it was divided 3:2:1 into their capital books." NPO (constructed): club surplus ₹8,000: `Dr Income and Expenditure A/c ₹8,000 · Cr Accumulated Fund A/c ₹8,000`; plain: "Income exceeded spending by ₹8,000; added to the club's accumulated fund."

### 1.14 The accounting period is normally a year; interim statements can be made for other spans
Financial statements are prepared at regular intervals, normally one year. An interim span is possible, for example when a partner retires. The Technical Guide's formats are for "the year ended"; the balance sheet is "as at" a date.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11, Ch 2 §2.2.4 "Accounting Period Concept" — fetched. ICAI Technical Guide, Ch VI Part I and II forms — fetched.
**Example (constructed).** Professional: no entry; a statement of profit and loss for 1 April 2026 to 30 September 2026 shows `Income ₹2,40,000 − Expenses ₹1,90,000 = Profit ₹50,000`. Plain words: "A half-year look at the profit: ₹50,000 earned between April and September."

### 1.15 The BoS on the trial balance: a statement at a date, in two forms, taken monthly or at period end, and not conclusive
A trial balance is "a statement and not an account": a list of ledger balances (or the totals of each side) on a particular date, which is stated at its head, with the columns "Dr. Amount (Total or Balance)" and "Cr. Amount (Total or Balance)". It can be prepared at any time and, to check arithmetic accuracy, "generally ... at monthly intervals", but it is "preferable to prepare it at the end of the reporting period which may be month end/quarter end/year end" before the statements are drawn up; financial statements are normally prepared from an *agreed* trial balance. Agreement is not conclusive proof: errors remain if a transaction was not entered in the journal, a wrong amount was written in both columns, a wrong account was named, an entry was not posted, or it was posted twice. The BoS does not show opening, period and closing columns.
**Source:** ICAI BoS, Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 2 Unit 3 *Trial Balance*, §3.1–§3.3, pp. 2.67–2.68 — fetched (ICAI BoS PDF) https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74608bos60479-fnd-cp2-u3.pdf. Study material, not a pronouncement.
**Example.** Professional (own figures): the opening entry `Dr Cash A/c ₹5,000 · Cr Capital A/c ₹5,000` gives a trial balance as at 30 April: Cash ₹5,000 Dr; Capital ₹5,000 Cr; totals ₹5,000 and ₹5,000. Plain words: "On 30 April I list every account's balance in two columns, owed to me or owed by me, and the two columns must add to the same figure."

### 1.16 The BoS on closing and adjusting entries: they are journal entries, and the year-end adjustments are reversed on the first day of the next year
At the end of the year the nominal accounts are transferred to the Trading and Profit and Loss Accounts by *closing entries* in the journal (`Trading A/c Dr · To Opening Stock A/c`, `Trading A/c Dr · To Purchases A/c`, `Profit and Loss A/c Dr · To each expense`, `each income Dr · To Profit and Loss A/c`; gross profit to the Profit and Loss Account; net profit `Profit and Loss A/c Dr · To Capital A/c`, a net loss the reverse). Before that, the adjusting entries for expenses accrued, income accrued, income received in advance, expenses prepaid and stock of materials are passed. The BoS adds: "Next year in the beginning entries No. (1) to (5) should be reversed." The drawings are `Capital A/c Dr · To Drawings`, and balances of real and personal accounts are carried forward in the balance sheet.
**Source:** ICAI BoS, Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 7 *Preparation of Final Accounts of Sole Proprietors*, Unit 1 §1.3–§1.4, pp. 7.9–7.23; adjustments (1)–(5) and the note after them, pp. 7.23–7.26; Ch. 2 Unit 4 §4.7(ii), p. 2.96 — fetched (ICAI BoS PDF) https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74616bos60479-fnd-cp7-u1.pdf; https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74609bos60479-fnd-cp2-u4.pdf. Study material, not a pronouncement.
**Example.** Professional (BoS pattern, own figures): rent outstanding ₹14,000 at 31 March: `Dr Rent A/c ₹14,000 · Cr Outstanding Rent A/c ₹14,000`; on 1 April the next year `Dr Outstanding Rent A/c ₹14,000 · Cr Rent A/c ₹14,000`; net profit ₹43,000 closed: `Dr Profit and Loss A/c ₹43,000 · Cr Capital A/c ₹43,000`. Plain words: "At year end I booked the ₹14,000 rent I still owed; on 1 April I cancel it so that paying it later does not count twice." / "The year's profit of ₹43,000 is added to my capital."

### 1.17 When records are incomplete, the result can be computed from capital without closing entries
Where the revenue and expense detail is not known, the BoS finds the year's profit by comparing capital at two dates: closing capital + drawings − fresh capital introduced − opening capital = profit (loss if negative). Capital is the balancing figure of a statement of affairs drawn up at each date. The profit-and-loss account is not drawn up from ledger balances; the result is computed, not closed to capital by entries.
**Source:** ICAI BoS, Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 9 *Accounts from Incomplete Records*, §3 and §3.1, pp. 9.6–9.7; §3.3, pp. 9.10–9.12 — fetched (ICAI BoS PDF) https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74620bos60479-fnd-cp9.pdf. Study material, not a pronouncement.
**Example.** Professional (the BoS figures): closing capital ₹12,500 + drawings ₹3,000 − fresh capital ₹3,750 − opening capital ₹10,000 = profit ₹1,750. If it is then written into the books: `Dr Profit and Loss A/c ₹1,750 · Cr Capital A/c ₹1,750` (own derivation; the BoS shows the computation). Plain words: "My business was worth ₹10,000 at the start and ₹12,500 at the end; I put in ₹3,750 and took out ₹3,000, so the year earned ₹1,750."

## 2. Where the sources disagree, or are silent
- **Closing entries vs computed result.** NCERT Ch 8 teaches closing entries; NCERT Ch 9 shows adjustments entered in the statements by addition/deduction as well as by journal entry. No source read says whether the ledger must carry the closing entries. Technical Guide and AS 1 are silent on how the ledger is closed.
- **Opening/period/closing trial balance.** No source reads as expecting it. NCERT dismisses the totals and totals-cum-balances forms as not used in practice. Silent on any "working trial balance" that adds adjustment columns.
- **Reversal of year-end adjustments next year.** NCERT Ch 9 silent; the ICAI-hosted faculty deck shows 1 April reversing entries; the deck is not a pronouncement or study material. (NCERT instead says closing stock "becomes the opening stock of the next year".)
- **Format of the balance sheet.** NCERT Ch 8 §8.6.1 says there is "no prescribed form of Balance sheet, for a proprietary and partnership firms"; ICAI's Technical Guide (June 2022) recommends formats for non-corporate entities. The two are consistent in time (the Technical Guide recommends; it does not prescribe by law) but the NCERT statement is out of date against it.
- **NCERT NPO coverage.** Not found in the Class 12 chapters fetched; I cannot say from the sources whether it was removed in the 2026-27 reprint.
- **Textbook dating.** NCERT Ch 8 headings say 2026 but its prose around Fig. 8.3 still says "year ended March 31, 2017", and Ch 9 uses 2017 throughout: a reprint inconsistency with no effect on the rules.
- **The Guidance Note's date and reach, settled (FAQ, 20 Oct 2026).** The Guidance Note on Financial Statements of Non-Corporate Entities applies to accounting periods beginning on or after **1 April 2024** (Q1) and supersedes the 2022 Technical Guide from that date (Q3); it *prescribes* the formats (Q4), allows line items to be added or substituted and nil lines omitted (Q7–Q8), and requires the previous period's figures beside the current ones except in a first set of statements (Q9, citing Framework ¶42). It covers business or professional non-corporate entities — sole proprietors, partnerships, societies, trusts, associations — but not where ICAI has given specific guidance, as for not-for-profit entities (Q2 exception (c), Q6). A household, which carries on no business, is not in its list. From 1 April 2024 non-company entities are *MSME* or *Large* (Q10); an MSME that uses its exemptions says so in a note (Q10, Additional requirements (1)). The Note's own text is still not read; the 2022 Technical Guide citations above stand for what it superseded.
- **BoS Foundation Ch. 2, 7 and 9 read (20 Oct 2026).** The BoS teaches closing entries as journal entries (§1.16) and also a computed result, from capital comparison, where records are incomplete (§1.17): the two are both in the BoS, for different kinds of book. It says the adjustments (1)–(5) are reversed at the start of next year (p. 7.26), which the NCERT chapter does not say. It describes the cash basis only in the theory chapter (Ch. 1 Unit 2, pp. 1.38–1.39: cash basis profit is "cash received ... less cash paid") and gives no cash-basis adjustments.

## 4. Answers to the GAPS items this topic settles
- **F2 (prepaid / outstanding — a paid-ahead asset, an owed-not-paid liability, and a year-end wizard):** Practice teaches both items as year-end adjustments: prepaid becomes a current asset and is deducted from the expense; outstanding becomes a current liability and is added to the expense (NCERT Class 11 Ch 9 §9.3, §9.4). Accrued income and income received in advance are the mirror cases (§9.5, §9.6). Whether a "wizard" is needed is not a question the sources address; the next-year handling is not settled, see §3.
- **F3 (closing to capital — folding drawings and the result into capital so the next year opens with one figure):** Textbook practice closes net profit and drawings to the capital account and carries capital forward as one balance (NCERT Class 11 Ch 8 §8.4.3, §8.6.2 (8), §8.7). Whether the transfer is posted or only computed is not settled, see §3. For a partnership under the fixed-capital method the capital stays fixed and the result and drawings go to current accounts (NCERT Class 12 Ch 1 §1.4.1).
- **D9, D10:** open decisions of an app standard, whose text I was told not to read; the topic's questions are answered above.
