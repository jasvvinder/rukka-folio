# 11 — Errors and corrections

**Questions (from the outline):** Rectification of errors: one-sided vs two-sided errors, before vs after the trial balance, the suspense account. Cash short or over. A correcting entry vs replacing the entry. The errors a balanced trial balance does not show: omission, commission, principle, compensating.
**Settles (tracing only):** GAPS A12; D16a

NCERT = NCERT Accountancy Class 11 *Financial Accounting* Part I ch 6 "Trial Balance and Rectification of Errors" (printed pp. 180–), reprint 2026-27, text read from the PDF https://ncert.nic.in/textbook/pdf/keac106.pdf. BoS = ICAI Board of Studies, Foundation Paper 1, ch 2 Unit 6 virtual-class slides (study material, not a pronouncement), read in full: https://live.icai.org/bos/vcc-2nd-batch-recorded-lectures/pdf/Unit%206_Rectification%20of%20errors.pdf. AS 5 read as an extract from the ICAI page.

## 1. Findings

### 1.1 A balanced trial balance is not proof the books are right
A trial balance whose debit and credit totals agree shows only that every debit has a matching credit and that posting and balancing are arithmetically right; it is "not a conclusive proof of the accuracy of the accounts" (NCERT §6.2.1, pp. 182–183; §6.4, p. 189). Two examples NCERT gives: the right amount debited to the wrong account; and an equal debit and credit of the wrong amount. NCERT's objectives of the trial balance are arithmetical accuracy, locating errors and helping prepare the statements (§6.2).
**Source:** NCERT Part I ch 6 §6.2–6.4 pp. 181–189 — fetched.
**Example.** Professional: right `Dr Rent A/c ₹2,000 · Cr Cash A/c ₹2,000`; wrongly posted `Dr Landlord A/c ₹2,000 · Cr Cash A/c ₹2,000` — the trial balance still agrees. Plain words: "Paid ₹2,000 rent but wrote it against the landlord's name as if we were paying off a debt — the totals tally, the books are still wrong."

### 1.2 The four kinds of error, and which the trial balance shows
NCERT §6.4.1 classifies errors into commission, omission, principle and compensating (the BoS slides group them as errors of principle and clerical errors — omission, commission, compensating).
- **Omission.** Complete omission (the transaction is not in the books of original entry) leaves the trial balance agreeing; partial omission (recorded but not posted to one account) makes it disagree (NCERT §6.4.3 p. 190, with §6.6.1 and §6.6.2; BoS slide 3).
- **Commission.** Wrong posting, totalling, balancing or amount; NCERT says it is of a clerical nature and "most of the errors of commission affect" the trial balance (§6.4.2, pp. 189–190) — but its own rectification examples include commission errors that do not (posting to the wrong person's account, §6.6.1 (d)).
- **Principle.** A breach of accounting principles, such as treating capital as revenue or recording a credit purchase of machinery through the purchases book; does not affect the trial balance (§6.4.4, p. 190).
- **Compensating.** Two or more errors whose net effect on debits and credits is nil, e.g. purchases book overcast by ₹10,000 and sales returns book undercast by ₹10,000; does not affect agreement (§6.4.5, pp. 190–191).
**Source:** NCERT Part I ch 6 §6.4.1–6.4.5 pp. 189–191 — fetched; BoS Unit 6 slides 2–3 — fetched.
**Example.** (one per kind). Complete omission: `Dr Mohan A/c ₹10,000 · Cr Sales A/c ₹10,000` never written. Plain words: "Sold ₹10,000 of goods to Mohan on credit and never wrote it down." Principle: `Dr Repairs A/c ₹30,000 · Cr Bank A/c ₹30,000` for new shutters (should be `Dr Shop Building`). Plain words: "Paid ₹30,000 for new shutters but called it repairs." Compensating: purchases overcast `Dr Purchases A/c` ₹10,000 too much and sales returns undercast `Dr Sales Returns A/c` ₹10,000 too little. Plain words: "One book added up ₹10,000 too high, another ₹10,000 too low — they cancel."

### 1.3 Classify for correction by where the error sits: one account or two
For correcting, NCERT sorts errors into those that do not affect the trial balance (usually in two accounts, "two sided errors") and those that do (usually one account, "one sided"); the distinction matters because the first is rectified by an ordinary journal entry, the second needs a suspense account or a neutralising entry in the account (§6.6, p. 192). The BoS slides use the same test (Type 1: one account — under/overcasting, not posting one side, wrong side; Type 2: both accounts — principle, compensating, complete omission) (slide 5). Names of errors do not decide it: a complete omission, an error of principle and a posting to the wrong account are two-sided; an uncast book, a short posting and a wrong-side posting are one-sided.
**Source:** NCERT Part I ch 6 §6.6, 6.6.1, 6.6.2 pp. 192–196 — fetched; BoS slides 5–6 — fetched.
**Example.** Professional: two-sided: `Dr Furniture A/c ₹8,000 · Cr Purchases A/c ₹8,000`; one-sided: `Dr Mohan A/c ₹10,000 · Cr Suspense A/c ₹10,000`. Plain words: "Furniture ₹8,000 had been put with goods bought — moved to furniture." / "Mohan's ₹10,000 sale was never copied to his page — put in through the suspense page until we find it."

### 1.4 Two-sided errors: cancel the wrong effect and restore the right one, in one journal entry
The method: cancel the wrong debit or credit by reversing it and restore the correct one; the entry debits the account with short debit or excess credit and credits the account with excess debit or short credit (NCERT §6.6.1, p. 192). Worked examples: sale to Mohan recorded as ₹1,000 instead of ₹10,000 → `Dr Mohan ₹9,000 · Cr Sales ₹9,000`; recorded ₹12,000 instead of ₹10,000 → `Dr Sales ₹2,000 · Cr Mohan ₹2,000`; posted to Ram instead of Mohan → `Dr Mohan ₹10,000 · Cr Ram ₹10,000`; rent paid shown as payment to landlord → `Dr Rent ₹2,000 · Cr Landlord ₹2,000` (pp. 193–194). The same journal entry serves before or after the trial balance (BoS slide 6). Each rectification is journalised with a narration naming the error ("... now corrected"), as in NCERT Illustration 1 (p. 205).
**Source:** NCERT Part I ch 6 §6.6.1 pp. 192–194, Illustration 1 p. 205 — fetched; BoS slide 6 — fetched.
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Mohan A/c ₹9,000 · Cr Sales A/c ₹9,000` (narration: credit sale to Mohan of ₹10,000 entered as ₹1,000, now corrected). Plain words: "The sale to Mohan was written as ₹1,000 but was ₹10,000 — added the missing ₹9,000 to both Mohan and Sales, with a note saying why."

### 1.5 One-sided errors: before the trial balance, correct in the account; after it, through a suspense account
Before the trial balance is prepared, a one-account error is corrected directly in the ledger account where it occurred; after the trial balance, by a journal entry using a suspense account (BoS slide 5). NCERT gives the same two routes for one-sided errors: an explanatory note or additional posting in the affected account, or a journal entry through suspense (§6.6.2, p. 196). Steps through suspense: identify the account; find the excess or short debit or credit; credit the account for excess debit or short credit, or debit it for excess credit or short debit; complete the entry with suspense on the other side (p. 199). Examples: sale to Mohan ₹10,000 not posted to his account → `Dr Mohan ₹10,000 · Cr Suspense ₹10,000`; posted as ₹12,000 → `Dr Suspense ₹2,000 · Cr Mohan ₹2,000`; purchases book overcast by ₹1,000 → `Dr Suspense ₹1,000 · Cr Purchases ₹1,000` (pp. 199–202).
**Source:** NCERT Part I ch 6 §6.6.2 pp. 196–202 — fetched; BoS slide 5 — fetched.
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Suspense A/c ₹1,000 · Cr Purchases A/c ₹1,000`. Plain words: "The month's goods-bought book was added up ₹1,000 too high; the individual suppliers' pages are fine. Took ₹1,000 off Purchases, balanced by the suspense page."

### 1.6 The suspense account holds a trial-balance difference only until the errors are found
If the trial balance does not tally because of one-sided errors, the difference is put on the shorter side as a Suspense account so the work can go on to the financial statements. NCERT's example: credit column ₹50,000 against debit ₹35,000 because ₹15,000 of sales to Diwakar was not posted to his account; ₹15,000 goes to the debit as suspense. When the errors are found the amounts move from suspense to the right accounts and suspense closes; the account "is not placed in any particular category of accounts and is just a temporary phenomenon". If errors remain unlocated at the year end, suspense stays in the statements and is carried forward to the next year (§6.6.2 pp. 197–199; §6.6.3 p. 202).
**Source:** NCERT Part I ch 6 pp. 197–199, 202 — fetched.
**Example.** Professional: to make the books agree `Dr Suspense A/c ₹15,000`; when found `Dr Diwakar and Sons A/c ₹15,000 · Cr Suspense A/c ₹15,000`. Plain words: "Totals were ₹15,000 apart; wrote ₹15,000 on a suspense page to be able to carry on. Found the missing sale to Diwakar — moved it there and the suspense page is empty."

### 1.7 Correct by an additional entry or a neutralising note, never by erasing
An error found in a book of original entry before it is posted may be crossed out with a single line, the right amount written above and initialled; an amount posted wrongly to the right account may be corrected the same way or by an additional posting for the difference with an explanatory note. "Errors should never be corrected by erasing or overwriting" — it reduces the authenticity of the records and suggests concealment. The better way is to note the correction on the appropriate side to neutralise the error: Shyam credited short by ₹190 gets an additional ₹190 credit labelled "Difference in amount posted short" (§6.6.2, pp. 196–197). So there are two cases: before posting, strike-and-initial replaces the entry; after posting, a separate correcting entry that leaves the original visible.
**Source:** NCERT Part I ch 6 §6.6.2 pp. 196–197 — fetched.
**Example.** Professional: Shyam's account was credited ₹1,310 instead of ₹1,500; the extra posting is `Cr Shyam's A/c ₹190`, balanced `Dr Suspense A/c ₹190` (suspense route of §1.5, since the trial balance is already struck). Plain words: "Shyam paid us ₹1,500 but his page shows ₹1,310. Did not rub it out; added ₹190 on his credit side with the note 'posted short'."

### 1.8 An error found in a later year goes to a Profit and Loss Adjustment Account, not to the old heads
If an error from an earlier year is found after the statements were finalised, income-and-expense items are not corrected through the old expense or income account but through a *Profit and Loss Adjustment Account*, so the current year's result is not distorted; the nominal accounts were already closed (NCERT §6.6.3, p. 202; BoS slide 8, which also says personal and real accounts are rectified as usual and that these amounts are prior period items). AS 5 defines prior period items as "income or expenses which arise in the current period as a result of errors or omissions" in preceding periods (¶4.3) and requires their nature and amount to be separately disclosed in the profit and loss statement (¶15). NCERT says this is taught in detail at a more advanced stage; no worked example is in the Class 11 text.
**Source:** NCERT Part I ch 6 §6.6.3 p. 202 — fetched; BoS slides 4, 8 — fetched; AS 5 (revised 1997) ¶4.3, ¶15 — fetched (extract) https://indasaccess.icai.org/Volume-III/AS/asb.html?a=108
**Example.** Professional: last year's shop rent of ₹3,000 was never booked: `Dr Profit and Loss Adjustment A/c ₹3,000 · Cr Landlord A/c ₹3,000`. Plain words: "Found in May that March's rent of ₹3,000 was missed. Entered it now, against a special 'last-year corrections' page, so this year's rent is not wrongly higher."

### 1.9 Finding the error: halves and nines
If the trial balance differs by an amount divisible by 2, an item of half the difference may have been posted to the wrong side; a complete omission of a posting equal to the difference is the other possibility. If the difference is divisible by 9, a transposition of figures (₹459 posted as ₹954, difference ₹495) is likely. Other steps: recast the totals; compare with the ledger; compare with the previous year; re-check balances and postings (§6.5, p. 191).
**Source:** NCERT Part I ch 6 §6.5 p. 191 — fetched.
**Example.** Professional: ₹750 received from Karim posted to the debit of Karim instead of the credit: debit total exceeds by ₹1,500; correct with `Dr Suspense A/c ₹1,500 · Cr Karim A/c ₹1,500` (the NCERT shape for "posted to the debit instead of the credit", Illustration 1 (vii), p. 205, which uses twice the amount). Plain words: "Karim paid ₹750 but we wrote it on the wrong side of his page; totals ₹1,500 apart, so we looked for a ₹750 item on the wrong side."

### 1.10 Cash short or over
Neither NCERT Part I ch 6, the BoS slides, nor the other NCERT chapters searched say anything about a difference between the cash book and cash counted. What the sources do give is the general rule that any unexplained one-sided difference is held in suspense until found (§1.6). Whether a cash-count difference may be so held, or goes at once to an expense or income head, is not stated; one web search for Indian sources returned only non-Indian blog material, which is not used here (search, no source accepted).
**Source:** none found; absence checked in NCERT Part I ch 4–6 and Part II ch 8–9 text and the BoS slides — fetched. Update 20 Oct 2026: the BoS study material (Ch. 2 Unit 5, Ch. 2 Unit 6) was also searched and has no cash-short-or-over head; its only related rule, for an owner's mixed book, is §1.11.
**Example.** None — no rule written. See §3.

### 1.11 An unexplained cash shortage in an owner's mixed book: the BoS debits the owner's capital
When the books are incomplete and the cash is short because the proprietor withdrew an amount without any entry being made in the cash book, the proprietor's capital account is debited. "In fact, it will be necessary to debit or credit the proprietor's capital account in respect of all unidentified amounts which cannot be adjusted anywhere else." The BoS states this for an owner's book that mixes business and private money; it gives no rule for a shortage with another cause (a petty cashier, a collection box) and no cash-short-or-over head.
**Source:** ICAI BoS, Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 9 *Accounts from Incomplete Records*, §4.1 *General techniques*, p. 9.15, item (3) — fetched (ICAI BoS PDF) https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74620bos60479-fnd-cp9.pdf. Study material, not a pronouncement.
**Example.** Professional (own figures): cash book ₹12,000, cash counted ₹11,000, the owner admits taking ₹1,000: `Dr Capital A/c (Drawings) ₹1,000 · Cr Cash A/c ₹1,000`. Plain words: "The cash box was ₹1,000 short of the book; the owner took it and did not write it down, so it is charged to him."

### 1.12 What the BoS study material adds on rectification: stages, "statements" in the account, suspense, and the next year
Errors "should never be corrected by overwriting"; the correction is another suitable entry, and how it is made depends on the stage at which the error is found: (a) before the trial balance, (b) after the trial balance but before the final accounts, (c) in the next accounting period. (a) Errors that touch one side of an account, or two accounts in a way that no complete journal entry can correct, are corrected by a *rectification statement* written in the account itself ("By omission of posting ₹50"); others by an ordinary rectification entry. (b) After the trial balance every error is corrected by a complete journal entry, using the suspense account for the one-sided part; agreement reached by a suspense account "is not real" and effort must be made to locate the errors. (c) In the next year the balance in the suspense account may be carried forward "or even" transferred to capital; a correction of an expense or income of the earlier year goes through a *Profit and Loss Adjustment Account* (*Prior Period Items*), because debiting the old head to this year's account would falsify this year's profit; these items are charged after the current year's profit is found and are disclosed separately.
**Source:** ICAI BoS, Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 2 Unit 6 *Rectification of Errors*, §6.5, p. 2.131; §6.5.1, pp. 2.131–2.133; §6.5.2, pp. 2.138–2.139; §6.5.3, p. 2.147 — fetched (ICAI BoS PDF) https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74611bos60479-fnd-cp2-u6.pdf. Study material, not a pronouncement.
**Example.** Professional: the sales book for November was added up ₹100 short, so the trial balance was out by ₹100 and the difference was parked in Suspense; rectified after the trial balance: `Dr Suspense A/c ₹100 · Cr Sales A/c ₹100`. Next-year case (BoS): purchases book cast ₹1,000 short, difference left in Suspense: `Dr Profit and Loss Adjustment A/c ₹1,000 · Cr Suspense A/c ₹1,000`. Plain words: "The sales book was added ₹100 too low; the trial balance gap sat in Suspense until I found it and credited sales." / "Found only next year: I charge the old ₹1,000 to a special adjustment account so this year's purchases stay right."

## 2. Where the sources disagree, or are silent
- **Commission errors and the trial balance.** NCERT §6.4.2 says most errors of commission affect the trial balance, yet §6.6.1 (d) rectifies commission errors (posting to the wrong person) that do not. The BoS slide 3 says commission errors, which it describes as wrong totalling and wrong-side posting, "does not allow the Trial balance to agree", while NCERT §6.2.2 says debiting a correct amount to the wrong account leaves the trial balance agreeing. The one-sided/two-sided test (§1.3) avoids the problem.
- **Omission.** NCERT and BoS agree: complete omission leaves the trial balance agreeing, partial omission does not.
- **Classification.** NCERT has four heads (commission, omission, principle, compensating); BoS groups three of them as "clerical" and keeps principle apart.
- **Silent:** cash short or over (but see §1.11 for an owner's mixed book); any rule for replacing (amending) a posted entry rather than adding a correcting one beyond NCERT §6.6.2; a correction in a locked or closed period other than §1.8; the ICAI Guidance Note on Non-Corporate Entities (not fetched).
- AS 5 ¶4.3 is a corporate-reporting definition; how a household or small book discloses a prior-period correction is not stated.
- **BoS study material vs the BoS slide on commission errors (checked 20 Oct 2026).** The slide (above) says commission errors do not let the trial balance agree. The study material itself (Ch. 2 Unit 6 §6.3, pp. 2.128–2.130) lists wrong casting, wrong balancing, posting an amount on the wrong side or at the wrong amount, and omitting a posting as errors that *do* affect the trial balance, and "posting an amount in a wrong account but on the correct side" as one that does *not* (p. 2.129(b)(iii)). That agrees with NCERT §6.2.2; the slide was over-general. The study material also classes errors into the same four heads as NCERT — principle, omission, commission, compensating (p. 2.128) — and its diagram groups the last three as "clerical errors" (p. 2.130).
- **Prior-period items:** the BoS states the rule for a book with a profit and loss account (§1.12); how a household or trust book with no profit and loss account would show a prior-year correction is not stated.

## 4. Answers to the GAPS items this topic settles
- **A12** (practice: a separate *Correction* entry, `Dr right head · Cr wrong head`, no money moving): matches the textbook two-sided rectification (§1.4, NCERT §6.6.1 (e), p. 194) — a separate journal entry with narration. The textbooks do not use amend-in-place for a posted entry (§1.7). A closed or later year goes to a Profit and Loss Adjustment A/c (§1.8).
- **D16a** (open decision; text not read): the topic's questions are answered in §1.3–1.8; cash short or over is not settled (§3 row 1).
