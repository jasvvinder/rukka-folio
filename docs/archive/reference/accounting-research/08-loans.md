# 08 — Loans

**Questions (from the outline):** Loan accounts. An EMI split into principal and interest from the lender's statement. Overdraft. KCC. Interest on loans from relatives or friends.
**Settles (tracing only):** GAPS A2, D4; F-06; D15, D-opt-3

Source key: **[N4]** NCERT Class 11 Accountancy, Financial Accounting Part I, Ch. 4 (keac104.pdf, 2026-27 reprint), the ledger of a "6% Loan Account". **[N8]** Part II Ch. 8 "Financial Statements – I" (keac201.pdf). **[N9]** Part II Ch. 9 "Financial Statements – II", §9.3 and §9.5 (keac202.pdf). **[A9]** ICAI AS 9 ¶8.2 (read at indasaccess.icai.org). URLs in §5.

## 1. Findings

### 1.1 A loan received is a liability, credited in a loan account
When a loan is taken, the lender's loan account is credited and cash or bank is debited. In the textbook's ledger, "6% Loan Account" has a credit entry (₹25,000 on 5 April from Dayal Traders) and closes with a balance carried down.
**Source:** [N4] ledger of "6% Loan Account" and the transaction "Apr. 05 Loan from M/s Dayal Traders @ 6% ₹25,000" — fetched
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Bank A/c ₹25,000 · Cr Dayal Traders Loan A/c ₹25,000`. Plain words: "Took a loan of ₹25,000 from Dayal Traders and put it in the bank."

### 1.2 Classification: current or long-term liability
Short-term loans and bank overdraft are current liabilities (payable within a year, usually out of current assets). Long-term loans (from banks and financial institutions) are all liabilities other than current liabilities, usually payable after one year of the balance-sheet date. The textbook's final-accounts example carries a "10% long-term loan" of ₹5,000 as a liability.
**Source:** [N8] classification of current and long-term liabilities, and the Ankit example — fetched
**Example.** Professional: `Cr Loan from Ramesh A/c ₹50,000` shown under long-term liabilities if repayable after a year. Plain words: "I owe Ramesh ₹50,000, to be repaid in two years."

### 1.3 Interest on a loan is an expense; unpaid interest at year end is an outstanding expense
Interest on loans is named among typical outstanding expenses. Interest earned but not yet received (including interest on a loan the firm has given) is accrued income. Each is carried by an adjusting entry: `Interest A/c Dr · To Outstanding interest A/c` for the borrower; `Accrued interest A/c Dr · To Interest income A/c` for the lender.
**Source:** [N9] §9.3 Outstanding Expenses ("interest on loan") and §9.5 Accrued Income ("interest on loan") — fetched
**Example.** Professional (borrower): `Dr Interest on Loan A/c ₹1,500 · Cr Outstanding Interest A/c ₹1,500`. Plain words: "Ramesh's loan has earned ₹1,500 interest this year that I have not yet paid."

### 1.4 Interest is measured on a time basis
For the lender, interest accrues, in most circumstances, on a time basis determined by the amount outstanding and the rate applicable.
**Source:** [A9] ¶8.2 — fetched (indasaccess.icai.org page quoting AS 9)
**Example.** Professional: ₹50,000 at 12% a year for 3 months = ₹1,500: `Dr Interest Receivable A/c ₹1,500 · Cr Interest Income A/c ₹1,500` (lender's book). Plain words: "My friend owes me ₹50,000 at 12%; three months' interest is ₹1,500."

### 1.5 Paying interest on a loan; bank overdraft interest
The textbook shows interest on a loan paid as an expense in the final accounts (Ankit: interest paid on loan ₹5,000). Bank interest or charges on an overdraft are entered on the Cr side of the bank column and appear in the bank statement before the book (see topic 05).
**Source:** [N8] trading and profit and loss account of Ankit ("Interest paid on loan ₹5,000"); [N4] §4.1.2 (bank charges and interest) — fetched
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Interest on Loan A/c ₹500 · Cr Bank A/c ₹500`. Plain words: "Paid ₹500 interest on the loan from the bank."

### 1.6 Repaying principal; EMI split; KCC; cards; relatives' loans: not covered
The sources I read do not show a repayment entry for loan principal, an instalment split into principal and interest from the lender's statement, a Kisan Credit Card account, or the special case of loans between relatives and friends. Repayment is the reverse of 1.1 (Dr loan account, Cr bank), but I did not see this in the text, so it is not given as a sourced rule. I did not read hire-purchase or instalment-purchase chapters.
**Source:** none (silent)

### 1.7 A partner's loan repaid by equal instalments: each payment is principal plus interest on the balance
When a loan is to be paid off in a number of equal instalments with interest on the balance, the BoS divides the loan into equal parts and calculates the interest for each period on what is outstanding; each payment is the instalment on account of the loan plus the interest for the period. The loan account is credited with the interest and debited with the cash paid, so the balance falls by the instalment only. The BoS example: ₹30,000 at 6% in four equal annual instalments: year 1 interest ₹1,800, payment ₹7,500 + ₹1,800 = ₹9,300, balance ₹22,500; year 2 interest ₹1,350, payment ₹8,850. This is an equal-principal schedule, not an equated monthly instalment; the BoS gives no rule for splitting a bank-debited EMI, and says only that standing-instruction payments such as EMIs are known to the customer from the statement and entered then (Ch. 3 §5(vi), p. 3.10).
**Source:** ICAI BoS, Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 10 Unit 4 §4.6 *Paying a partner's loan in instalment*, pp. 10.143–10.144 — fetched (ICAI BoS PDF) https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74624bos60479-fnd-cp10-u4.pdf; Ch. 3 §5(vi), p. 3.10 — fetched https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74612bos60479-fnd-cp3.pdf. Study material, not a pronouncement.
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Interest A/c ₹1,800 · Cr Ramesh's Loan A/c ₹1,800` (interest for the year, 6% on ₹30,000) and `Dr Ramesh's Loan A/c ₹9,300 · Cr Cash A/c ₹9,300` (₹7,500 principal + ₹1,800 interest); loan balance ₹22,500. Plain words: "Paid Ramesh ₹9,300 this year: ₹7,500 off the loan and ₹1,800 interest at 6% on the ₹30,000; ₹22,500 is still owed to him."

### 1.8 An overdraft is the credit balance of the bank column, and the balance sheet lists overdraft and cash credit as short-term loans
The BoS cash book shows an overdraft as a bank column whose credit side is bigger than its debit side (balanced with the difference carried down on the debit side, then brought down on the credit side); a credit opening balance in the bank column is an overdraft. The pass book, which is the bank's copy of the account, shows the same thing as a debit balance, "the overdraft balance for the customer (i.e. customer owes to bank)". The bank allows an overdraft to its good customers; the facility "is generally secured and must be cleared later". The BoS model balance sheet of a sole proprietor lists, under *Short Term Loans*, "Cash Credit", "Overdrafts" and "Other Loans", apart from trade payables; its statement of affairs lists "Loans, Bank overdraft" among liabilities; and a partnership balance sheet shows "Bank Overdraft" as a liability. Interest on an overdraft is charged by the bank and entered in the cash book when the customer sees the statement.
**Source:** ICAI BoS, Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 2 Unit 5 §5.2, p. 2.110 and answer 5, p. 2.121; Ch. 3 §1 p. 3.2, §3 pp. 3.4–3.5, Illustration 4 p. 3.24; Ch. 7 Unit 1 §1.6, p. 7.39; Ch. 9 §3.1, p. 9.8; Ch. 10 Unit 4 Illustration 6, p. 10.144 — fetched (ICAI BoS PDF) https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74610bos60479-fnd-cp2-u5.pdf; https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74612bos60479-fnd-cp3.pdf; https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74616bos60479-fnd-cp7-u1.pdf. Study material, not a pronouncement.
**Example.** Professional: bank column closes `Cr ₹8,000`, shown as `Overdraft ₹8,000` under short-term loans; interest charged by the bank ₹160: `Dr Interest on Overdraft A/c ₹160 · Cr Bank A/c ₹160`. Plain words: "My bank book shows I have drawn ₹8,000 more than I put in, so I owe the bank ₹8,000; the bank charged ₹160 interest."

## 2. Where the sources disagree, or are silent
- **EMI split:** NCERT Part I/II (extracted text) is silent. ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1 was read on 20 Oct 2026: equal-principal instalments with interest are in §1.7, but no EMI split of a bank-debited instalment.
- **KCC (Kisan Credit Card):** no textbook or ICAI source found or fetched. A running loan used as money (drawn, spent, repaid, interest charged) is not described.
- **Overdraft limit/cash credit:** NCERT mentions overdraft as a Cr bank balance and a current liability only (topic 05).
- **Interest on loans from relatives or friends:** AS 9 ¶8.2 covers interest accrual for the lender; whether an interest-free loan is to be accounted for differently is not addressed. AS 9 applies to enterprises; a household is outside its scope in the text I read (scope paragraph not read).
- **Borrower's interest:** AS 9 is about revenue (the lender's side); the borrower's side rests on NCERT [N9] §9.3 only.
- **BoS Foundation read (20 Oct 2026).** The BoS covers a partner's loan repaid by equal instalments (§1.7) and the overdraft and "Cash Credit" heads (§1.8). It does not describe a KCC, a running loan drawn and repaid like money, an EMI split from a lender's statement, interest on a loan from a relative or friend, or an interest-free loan (no hit in the chapters read). It names "Cash Credit" as a short-term loan head without saying what the ledger account looks like.

## 4. Answers to the GAPS items this topic settles
- **A2 (loans; instalment as two-debit line):** the loan account is a credit-balance liability (§1.1–1.2); interest is an expense (§1.3, §1.5). The split of one bank payment into principal and interest is not settled, see §3.
- **D4 (KCC as a running loan account used as money):** not settled, see §3.
- **F-06, D15, D-opt-3:** open findings/decisions; the questions are in §3.
