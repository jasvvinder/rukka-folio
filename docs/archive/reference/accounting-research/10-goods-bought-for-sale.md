# 10 — Goods bought for sale

**Questions (from the outline):** Without a stock count: what the result means, and what the book must say. With a closing-stock figure: the one entry practice uses.
**Settles (tracing only):** GAPS D1

Scope: only what a result means with and without a stock figure, and the one closing-stock entry. How stock is counted or valued is out of scope and was not researched. NCERT = NCERT Accountancy Class 11 *Financial Accounting* Part I / Part II, reprint 2026-27, text read from the PDFs (printed page numbers).

## 1. Findings

### 1.1 Goods bought for resale are debited to a Purchases account when bought
Goods bought for resale go to the Purchases account, a debit balance that appears in the trial balance and is carried to the trading account (NCERT Part I ch 6, Fig. 6.2 illustrative trial balance, p. 182; Part II ch 8 §8.4.4, p. 293). Anything that is not goods for resale (furniture, machinery, stationery) does not go to Purchases: recording a credit purchase of machinery in the purchases book is an error of principle (NCERT Part I ch 6 §6.4.4, p. 190; ICAI BoS slides, Unit 6, p. 3: "Purchases of fixed assets debited to purchases A/c instead of the asset A/c").
**Source:** NCERT Part I ch 6 pp. 182, 190; Part II ch 8 p. 293 — fetched (keac106.pdf, keac201.pdf); ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1, ch 2 Unit 6 slides — fetched (study material, not a pronouncement) https://live.icai.org/bos/vcc-2nd-batch-recorded-lectures/pdf/Unit%206_Rectification%20of%20errors.pdf
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Purchases A/c ₹75,000 · Cr Cash A/c ₹75,000` (rice and pulses bought to sell). Plain words: "Bought ₹75,000 of goods for the shop and paid cash."

### 1.2 Without a stock figure, cost of goods sold is taken as purchases plus direct expenses
Where there is no opening or closing stock, cost of goods sold = purchases + direct expenses, "as there is no unsold stock, the presumption here is that all the goods purchased have been sold." The textbook adds that in practice some goods are unsold at the end of the period. So a result worked out from sales less purchases (less direct expenses) is right only if nothing bought is left unsold; if goods remain, that result understates the profit by the cost of the goods left (the same figures give gross profit ₹42,000 without the stock and ₹57,000 with stock of ₹15,000).
**Source:** NCERT Part II ch 8 §8.4.4, pp. 293–294 — fetched (https://ncert.nic.in/textbook/pdf/keac201.pdf)
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Purchases A/c ₹75,000 · Cr Cash A/c ₹75,000`; `Dr Cash A/c ₹1,25,000 · Cr Sales A/c ₹1,25,000`; wages `Dr Wages A/c ₹8,000 · Cr Cash A/c ₹8,000`. Result without stock: ₹1,25,000 − (₹75,000 + ₹8,000) = ₹42,000 gross profit. Plain words: "Bought goods ₹75,000, sold goods ₹1,25,000, paid ₹8,000 wages. We have not counted what is left on the shelf, so ₹42,000 assumes every bought item was sold."

### 1.3 What a book that counts no stock must say
No source I read prescribes wording for a book that does not count stock. What the sources do establish: the presumption in §1.2 is the stated basis of the result; accounting policies employed in preparing financial statements must be disclosed (Framework ¶40); and a result that is incomplete is not cured by disclosure alone (Framework ¶81). Whether the sentence "no closing stock is counted; the result assumes all goods bought were sold" is enough is not settled (§3).
**Source:** ICAI Framework ¶40, ¶81 — fetched (ICAI PDF) https://indasaccess.icai.org/Volume-III/AS/asb.html?a=101; NCERT Part II ch 8 §8.4.4 — fetched.
**Example.** Professional: no entry (a statement, not a posting). Plain words: "Result for the year = sales ₹1,25,000 − goods bought ₹75,000 − wages ₹8,000 = ₹42,000. Stock in hand not counted."

### 1.4 With a closing-stock figure, the one entry is Closing Stock Dr · Trading Account Cr
Closing stock (cost of unsold goods at the end of the period) "does not normally form part of trial balance, and is brought into books with the help of" `Closing stock A/c Dr · To Trading A/c`. This opens an asset account, closing stock, which goes to the balance sheet; the same figure is credited on the credit side of the trading account, reducing cost of goods sold: cost of goods sold = opening stock + purchases + direct expenses − closing stock. Closing stock of one year becomes opening stock of the next and appears in the next year's trial balance.
**Source:** NCERT Part II ch 8 §8.4.4, pp. 294–296; Part II ch 9 §9.2, pp. 320–322 — fetched.
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Closing Stock A/c ₹15,000 · Cr Trading A/c ₹15,000`. Plain words: "On 31 March, counted the goods still unsold on the shelf: ₹15,000 at cost. Added to what we own; the year's profit rises by ₹15,000 to ₹57,000 gross."

### 1.5 The alternative route: adjust Purchases instead
NCERT also records that "sometimes the opening and closing stock are adjusted through purchases account": `Closing stock A/c Dr · To Purchases A/c` and `Purchases A/c Dr · To Opening stock A/c`; the trial balance then shows adjusted purchases and closing stock and no opening stock, and neither stock is shown separately in the trading account. Which route a small book uses is not stated; the result is the same.
**Source:** NCERT Part II ch 9 §9.2, p. 321 — fetched.
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Closing Stock A/c ₹15,000 · Cr Purchases A/c ₹15,000`. Plain words: "Counted ₹15,000 of unsold goods; took it off the goods-bought total, so the year shows only the goods actually used up."

### 1.6 The stock figure is an input at the year end, not a running ledger
Under the entry in §1.4 the stock account exists only as the year-end adjustment; the textbook does not describe a running stock ledger. How the figure is obtained is out of scope. NCERT adds, under prudence, that closing stock is valued at cost or market value whichever is lower (Part I ch 2 §2.2.11, p. 34) — recorded here only as a pointer to the valuation question this note does not research.
**Source:** NCERT Part II ch 9 §9.2; Part I ch 2 §2.2.11 — fetched.
**Example.** Professional: as §1.4. Plain words: "Stock is counted once, on the year's last day, and written in as a single figure."

### 1.7 The BoS: the periodic (physical-count) system suits small enterprises; the count gives the closing figure and any loss is hidden inside cost of goods sold
Under the periodic inventory system the closing inventory is found by an actual physical count at a date; opening inventory and purchases are known, and cost of goods sold is the residual figure. Goods not in the closing inventory are assumed to have been sold, so cost of goods sold includes any loss from pilferage, damage or fraud, which cannot be identified separately. The system "is used by small enterprises where it is easy to control physical inventory" and "is not considered suitable for medium or larger enterprises". It needs a physical count more than once a year if quarterly or half-yearly statements are wanted. For the year-end figure the count is taken in the last week of the financial year or the first week of the next, and purchases and sales between the count and the year end are adjusted; the value is cost or net realisable value, whichever is lower. The BoS does not say what a book that never counts stock must state.
**Source:** ICAI BoS, Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 4 *Inventories*, §4 and §4.1 *Periodic inventory system*, p. 4.8; §4.3 table, row 4, p. 4.10; §6 *Inventories taking*, p. 4.19 — fetched (ICAI BoS PDF) https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74613bos60479-fnd-cp4.pdf. Study material, not a pronouncement.
**Example.** Professional (own figures): opening stock ₹15,000, purchases ₹1,50,000, stock counted at year end ₹30,000, so goods sold at cost ₹1,35,000: `Dr Closing Stock A/c ₹30,000 · Cr Trading Account ₹30,000` (the entry of §1.4). Plain words: "Counted what is left in the shop at year end: ₹30,000. So what I sold cost ₹1,35,000. If any goods went missing, that loss is hidden inside this figure."

## 2. Where the sources disagree, or are silent
- NCERT gives two routes (trading account credit; adjusting purchases) and does not choose between them.
- Silent: any source prescribing the wording a book must carry when stock is not counted; practice of small shops that never count stock (no source on trader practice read); the ICAI Technical Guide and the non-corporate Guidance Note — not fetched.
- AS 2 (Valuation of Inventories) is a valuation standard and was not read; it is out of scope.
- **BoS Foundation Ch. 4 read (20 Oct 2026).** The BoS names the periodic system as the small enterprise's system and says that cost of goods sold then includes any loss (§1.7); it does not prescribe wording for a book with no count, and says nothing of a shop that never counts stock.

## 4. Answers to the GAPS items this topic settles
- **D1** (goods bought for sale are spent when bought; no closing stock; the report should say no stock is counted): practice is that purchases are debited when bought (§1.1) and, absent a stock figure, the result presumes all were sold (§1.2); the wording of the statement is not settled (§3 row 1). With a figure, the one entry is Closing Stock Dr · Trading A/c Cr (§1.4).
