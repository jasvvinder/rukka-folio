# 07 — Advances and imprest

**Questions (from the outline):** The imprest system. Staff and labour advances. Advances to a person who is not an employee or a member. Settling against bills, returning the unspent part, writing off what has no bills.
**Settles (tracing only):** GAPS A10; F-09; D12

Source key: **[N4]** NCERT Class 11 Accountancy, Financial Accounting Part I, Ch. 4 "Recording of Transactions – II", §4.1.3–4.1.4 (keac104.pdf, 2026-27 reprint). **[N9]** Part II Ch. 9 "Financial Statements – II", §9.4 (keac202.pdf). URLs in §5.

## 1. Findings

### 1.1 Imprest system: a fixed float, reimbursed to the full amount
Under the imprest system a definite sum (the textbook uses ₹2,000) is given to the petty cashier at the start of a period. The petty cashier makes small payments out of it and, when a substantial part has been spent (the textbook says ₹1,780), is reimbursed by the head cashier the amount spent, so he again holds the full imprest at the start of the next period. Reimbursement may be weekly, fortnightly or monthly, depending on how often small payments occur.
**Source:** [N4] §4.1.3 — fetched
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Petty Cash A/c ₹2,000 · Cr Cash A/c ₹2,000` (float handed over). Plain words: "Gave the shop boy ₹2,000 as the float for small expenses."

### 1.2 Petty cash book: payments analysed into columns, one receipts column
The petty cash book has one amount column on the receipts side and several analysis columns on the payments side for the commonest heads, a Miscellaneous column for the rest and a Remarks column. Columns for date, voucher number and particulars are common. At period end the amount columns are totalled; the total column shows what was spent and is to be reimbursed. The petty cash book is balanced periodically; the balance is the cash with the petty cashier and is carried to the next period.
**Source:** [N4] §4.1.3 ("Posting from the Petty Cash Book" and the May 2017 example with ₹2,000 imprest and ₹1,487 spent) — fetched
**Example.** Professional: month's payments total ₹1,487, balance ₹513; reimbursement `Dr Petty Cash A/c ₹1,487 · Cr Cash A/c ₹1,487`. Plain words: "The boy spent ₹1,487 this month and has ₹513 left; I gave him ₹1,487 to bring the float back to ₹2,000."

### 1.3 Posting petty payments to the ledger
A petty cash account is opened in the ledger. It is debited with the amount given to the petty cashier. Each expense account is debited with the period's column total (narrated "petty cash account"), and the petty cash account is credited with the total spent ("sundries as per petty cash book"). The balance of the petty cash account is the actual cash with the petty cashier. NCERT also says the petty cash system may be operated through the main cash book, in which case no separate petty cash book is kept.
**Source:** [N4] §4.1.3 — fetched
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Postage A/c ₹210 · Dr Conveyance A/c ₹380 · Dr Stationery A/c ₹225 · Cr Petty Cash A/c ₹815`. Plain words: "From the float, ₹210 went on stamps, ₹380 on fares and ₹225 on stationery."

### 1.4 Source documents for payments
For payments the document may be any invoice, bill or receipt on the basis of which payment was made; the cash book itself is not the source.
**Source:** [N4] §4.1.4 (source documents paragraph) — fetched
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Conveyance A/c ₹60 · Cr Petty Cash A/c ₹60` (voucher: auto fare slip). Plain words: "Auto fare ₹60, slip kept."

### 1.5 An advance paid to an employee: the only sourced case is salary paid ahead
The textbook treats a salary paid in advance to an employee on joining as an *advance salary* (prepaid salary), a current asset, and reduces the salary expense by the same amount.
**Source:** [N9] §9.4 (₹5,000 of the ₹25,000 salary paid in advance; entry `Prepaid salary A/c Dr · To Salary A/c`) — fetched
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Prepaid Salary A/c ₹5,000 · Cr Salary A/c ₹5,000`. Plain words: "Of the salary I paid, ₹5,000 was an advance to the new clerk, so it is not this year's cost."

### 1.6 Settlement against bills, returned balance, advances to non-employees: not covered
NCERT and the sources I read do not describe how an advance given for a purpose (to a mason, driver, labour contractor, caretaker, committee member) is settled against bills, how an unspent part returned is recorded, or how an advance with no bills is written off. The imprest float of 1.1–1.3 is the closest sourced mechanism: the float is given, spending is vouchered, and reimbursement is made against the vouchers. I have not shown a Dr/Cr example here because no source gives one.
**Source:** none (silent); nearest [N4] §4.1.3

### 1.7 The BoS imprest rules: a fixed float, reimbursed only against vouchers, and the petty cashier receives no other cash
The BoS makes the same imprest system and adds its working rules: (i) the fixed sum should be enough for the likely small payments of a short period, say a week or a fortnight; (ii) reimbursement is made only when the petty cashier presents a statement of payments supported by vouchers, and is limited to the amount actually disbursed; (iii) vouchers are filed in order; (iv) no payment without authorisation, and payments above a specified limit are made only by the main cashier; (v) the petty cashier may not receive any cash except the reimbursement. The petty cash book has one receipts column (cash from the main cashier only) and analysis columns for the payments, with a sundries column that is itself analysed; it is balanced like the simple cash book, and the analysed totals are then debited to the respective accounts. The BoS's illustration treats payments "say below ₹200" as petty.
**Source:** ICAI BoS, Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 2 Unit 5 §5.4 *Petty cash book* and *Imprest system of petty cash*, pp. 2.112–2.114 (Illustration 4) — fetched (ICAI BoS PDF) https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74610bos60479-fnd-cp2-u5.pdf. Study material, not a pronouncement.
**Example.** Professional (own figures): float ₹5,000 given to Suresh, the petty cashier; at the week's end his vouchers total ₹3,200 (conveyance ₹1,200, postage ₹800, stationery ₹1,200). Reimbursed ₹3,200: `Dr Conveyance A/c ₹1,200 · Dr Postage A/c ₹800 · Dr Stationery A/c ₹1,200 · Cr Cash A/c ₹3,200`; Suresh again holds ₹5,000. Plain words: "Gave Suresh ₹5,000 for small bills. He came back with bills for ₹3,200; I gave him ₹3,200 to bring his float back to ₹5,000."

## 2. Where the sources disagree, or are silent
- **Staff and labour advances, advances to non-employees, settling against bills, returning the unspent part, writing off with no bills:** NCERT Part I and Part II (extracted text) and AS 9 are silent. ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1 Ch. 2 and Ch. 7 were read on 20 Oct 2026 (§1.7 and the update below) and are silent on these points; the Intermediate material was **not fetched**.
- **Imprest for the petty cashier vs an advance to a person:** NCERT teaches the imprest only as a petty-cashier float inside the cash book. It does not call a single-purpose advance to a worker an imprest.
- Traditional practice (rokad bahi) not researched in this note.
- **BoS Foundation Ch. 2 and Ch. 7 read (20 Oct 2026).** The BoS imprest (§1.7) is a float for a petty cashier only. It also classes "Prepaid salary" as a personal account that is an asset (Ch. 2 Unit 1 table, p. 2.29(s)) and says salary paid in advance "is an asset ... not an expense" (Ch. 7 Unit 1 answer 3, p. 7.67), which corroborates §1.5; its statement of affairs lists "Loans and advances" as an asset line (Ch. 9 §3.1, p. 9.8). It is silent on advances to labourers, to a person who is not an employee, on settling an advance against bills, on a returned balance and on writing off an advance that has no bills (no hit in the chapters read).

## 4. Answers to the GAPS items this topic settles
- **A10 (advances to people who are not book members):** the imprest float, its vouchered spending and full reimbursement are sourced (§1.1–1.4, [N4] §4.1.3); a salary advance is a current asset (§1.5, [N9] §9.4). The multi-head settlement, return of unspent money, top-up and no-bills cases are not settled, see §3.
- **F-09, D12:** open; the questions behind them are in §3.
