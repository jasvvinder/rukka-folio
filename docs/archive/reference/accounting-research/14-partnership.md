## 0. The headline answer: is the Appropriation Account posted?
**In the textbook, yes: it is posted as journal entries and the partners' accounts are credited by them.** NCERT 12-I ch. 1 §1.5.1 gives the Profit and Loss Appropriation Account as an *account* ("merely an extension of the Profit and Loss Account", p.8) and gives, with it, a numbered list of **journal entries** (p.8-9): the profit is transferred into the Appropriation Account; interest on capital, salary and commission are each first credited to the partners' accounts through an expense account and then transferred into the Appropriation Account; interest on drawings is debited to the partners and transferred in; and the final balance is debited to the Appropriation Account and credited to the partners' capital/current accounts "individually". The partner's current/capital account proformas (Fig. 1.1 and 1.2, p.6-7) list the credits as *Interest on capital, Salary, Commission, Profit and Loss Appropriation (share of profit)* and the debits as *Drawings, Interest on drawings*. So the partners' balances move by these entries, and the Appropriation Account shows how the profit was divided. The textbook says "it is customary to prepare" the account (p.8), which means the account is the customary *form*; the entries behind it are taught as real entries. Nothing in NCERT 12-I ch. 1 teaches a "computed, not posted" version.
The ICAI exposure draft agrees that the partner's account moves (its Notes 3a/3b show remuneration, interest, withdrawals and share of profit as movements in each partner's capital/current account) but differs on where interest and remuneration sit (see 1.3 and §2). The ICAI BoS study material agrees with NCERT that it is posted (Ch. 10 Unit 1 §1.8, pp. 10.15–10.17: the net profit is transferred from the debit side of the Profit and Loss Account to the credit side of a Profit and Loss Appropriation Account, and each adjustment is recorded in it).

## 1. Findings

### 1.1 When there is no deed (the textbook defaults)
If the deed is silent, NCERT 12-I teaches five accounting defaults. (a) **Profit sharing:** profits and losses are shared equally, whatever each partner's capital. (b) **Interest on capital:** none; it is allowed only if expressly agreed. (c) **Interest on drawings:** none charged. (d) **Interest on a partner's loan to the firm:** 6% per annum. (e) **Salary or remuneration:** none; no partner is entitled to a salary for taking part in the business unless the deed provides for it. The chapter adds that, subject to contract, a partner who makes a private profit from a firm transaction, the firm's property, connection or name, or who runs a competing business, must account for that profit to the firm. The chapter also states that "In the absence of any instruction", partners' capital accounts are kept by the fluctuating-capital method (§1.4(b), p.6).
**Source:** NCERT 12-I ch. 1 §1.2.1 *Provisions of Partnership Act Relevant for Accounting*, items (a)-(e) and (i)-(ii), p.4; repeated in the chapter Summary, item 3, p.35; Test Your Understanding I, p.4-5 — fetched. (The textbook attributes these to the Indian Partnership Act; this note cites only the textbook, per the outline.)
**Example (a).** Professional: profit `₹90,000` shared `Dr Profit and Loss Appropriation A/c ₹90,000 · Cr Ramesh Capital A/c ₹45,000 · Cr Suresh Capital A/c ₹45,000` although capitals are ₹5,00,000 and ₹1,00,000. Plain words: "No deed. The year's profit of ₹90,000 is split half and half between Ramesh and Suresh."
**Example (d).** Professional: `Dr Interest on Partner's Loan A/c ₹6,000 · Cr Suresh Loan A/c ₹6,000` for a ₹1,00,000 loan for a year (the entry form is mine; NCERT states only the 6% entitlement). Plain words: "Suresh lent the firm ₹1,00,000 with nothing agreed; the firm owes him ₹6,000 interest a year."

### 1.2 Fixed capital and fluctuating capital
*Fixed:* each partner has two accounts. The Capital account does not move except for fresh capital or a part-withdrawal of capital agreed among the partners, and always shows a credit balance. Drawings, interest on drawings, salary, commission, interest on capital and share of profit or loss all go to a separate Partner's Current Account, which may be debit or credit. Capital is shown on the liabilities side; the current account on the liabilities side if credit, on the assets side if debit. *Fluctuating:* one Capital account per partner takes all of these items, so its balance changes and can become a debit.
**Source:** NCERT 12-I ch. 1 §1.4 and §1.4.1 *Distinction between Fixed and Fluctuating Capital Accounts*, Fig. 1.1 and Fig. 1.2, p.5-7 — fetched
**Example.** Professional (fixed): `Dr Bank A/c ₹1,00,000 · Cr Ramesh Capital A/c ₹1,00,000` for fresh capital; `Dr Ramesh Current A/c ₹40,000 · Cr Cash A/c ₹40,000` for drawings (NCERT lists *Drawings* on the debit side of the current account, Fig. 1.1; the cash leg is the ordinary drawings entry of Class 11, ch. 3). Plain words: "Ramesh's ₹5,00,000 capital stays ₹5,00,000. His withdrawals, interest and profit share are kept in his separate current account, and it can run in his favour or against him."

### 1.3 The Profit and Loss Appropriation Account and its entries
The profit is first transferred from the Profit and Loss Account to the Appropriation Account. Then:
1. **Interest on capital:** (a) `Interest on Capital A/c Dr · To Partners' Capital/Current A/cs`; (b) `Profit and Loss Appropriation A/c Dr · To Interest on Capital A/c`.
2. **Interest on drawings:** (a) `Partners' Capital/Current A/cs Dr · To Interest on Drawings A/c`; (b) `Interest on Drawings A/c Dr · To Profit and Loss Appropriation A/c`.
3. **Salary / commission:** (a) `Salary (Commission) to Partner A/c Dr · To Partner's Capital/Current A/c`; (b) `Profit and Loss Appropriation A/c Dr · To Salary (Commission) to Partner A/c`.
4. **Share of profit:** `Profit and Loss Appropriation A/c Dr · To Partners' Capital/Current A/cs (individually)`; if a loss, the reverse.
NCERT's note: "In case firm suffers a loss, no interest on capital, salary, remuneration is to be allowed to partners." The Appropriation Account is debited with interest on capital, salary, commission and the share of profit, and credited with the profit and interest on drawings (Fig. 1.3).
**Source:** NCERT 12-I ch. 1 §1.5 and §1.5.1 *Profit and Loss Appropriation Account*, Journal Entries 1-6, Note and Fig. 1.3, p.8-9; Summary item 5, p.35 — fetched
**Worked example (all figures mine, on the textbook's entries).** Ramesh and Suresh share 3 : 2; fixed capitals ₹5,00,000 and ₹3,00,000; interest on capital 6%; salary to Suresh ₹60,000; drawings ₹40,000 and ₹30,000 with interest on drawings ₹1,000 and ₹750; net profit ₹3,00,000. Interest on capital ₹30,000 and ₹18,000. Divisible profit = 3,00,000 + 1,750 − 48,000 − 60,000 = ₹1,93,750, split ₹1,16,250 and ₹77,500.
Professional:
- `Dr Profit and Loss A/c ₹3,00,000 · Cr P&L Appropriation A/c ₹3,00,000`
- `Dr Interest on Capital A/c ₹48,000 · Cr Ramesh Current A/c ₹30,000 · Cr Suresh Current A/c ₹18,000`, then `Dr P&L Appropriation A/c ₹48,000 · Cr Interest on Capital A/c ₹48,000`
- `Dr Ramesh Current A/c ₹1,000 · Dr Suresh Current A/c ₹750 · Cr Interest on Drawings A/c ₹1,750`, then `Dr Interest on Drawings A/c ₹1,750 · Cr P&L Appropriation A/c ₹1,750`
- `Dr Salary to Partner A/c ₹60,000 · Cr Suresh Current A/c ₹60,000`, then `Dr P&L Appropriation A/c ₹60,000 · Cr Salary to Partner A/c ₹60,000`
- `Dr P&L Appropriation A/c ₹1,93,750 · Cr Ramesh Current A/c ₹1,16,250 · Cr Suresh Current A/c ₹77,500`
Plain words: "The year's profit was ₹3,00,000. Each partner gets 6% on his capital (₹30,000 and ₹18,000). Suresh, who runs the shop, gets ₹60,000 salary. Each is charged interest on what he took out (₹1,000 and ₹750). What is left, ₹1,93,750, is split 3 to 2: ₹1,16,250 to Ramesh and ₹77,500 to Suresh. Each partner's current account gets all these credits and charges." Check: credits 48,000 + 60,000 + 1,93,750 − 1,750 = ₹3,00,000, the profit.
**ICAI view (exposure draft).** The draft's profit and loss format has a line *Partners' remuneration* ("wherever applicable") before *Profit before tax*, and its finance-cost list separates *Interest on partners'/members' capital* from other interest. In that presentation salary and interest on capital are shown as charges above the profit line, not as appropriations of it. Its Notes 3a/3b still show them as credits to the partner's capital/current account.
**Source (ICAI):** ICAI ED-NCE, Part II *Statement of Profit and Loss* (line X) and General Instructions for the Statement of Profit and Loss, item 4 *Finance Costs* (PDF pp.32, 34); Notes 3a, 3b (PDF pp.52-53) — fetched (exposure draft)

### 1.4 Interest on capital: method
Interest is allowed only if agreed, at the agreed rate, for the period the capital stayed in the business. Opening balance earns for the whole year; additional capital earns from the date it came in to the year end; for a withdrawal, interest runs on the opening capital up to the date of withdrawal and on the reduced capital for the rest of the year (or, alternatively, on each amount for the time it remained in business). NCERT says it is "generally provided for in two situations": capitals unequal and profits equal, or capitals equal and profits unequal.
**Source:** NCERT 12-I ch. 1 §1.5.2 *Interest on Capital*, p.15-16 and Illustration 5 (p.17) — fetched
**Example.** Professional: Ramesh opens at ₹5,00,000 and adds ₹1,00,000 on 1 October; 6% p.a.; interest = 5,00,000 × 6% + 1,00,000 × 6% × 6/12 = ₹33,000: `Dr Interest on Capital A/c ₹33,000 · Cr Ramesh Current A/c ₹33,000`. Plain words: "Ramesh gets 6% on ₹5,00,000 for the year and 6% on the extra ₹1,00,000 for the half-year it was in: ₹33,000."

### 1.5 Interest on drawings: methods
Charged only if the deed provides, at the agreed rate, for the period the money was out. Three methods: (a) a *fixed amount every month*: interest on the total for an average period of 6½ months if drawn on the first of each month, 6 months if drawn mid-month, 5½ months if drawn at month end; (b) a *fixed amount every quarter*: on the total for 7½ months if drawn at the start of each quarter, 4½ months if at the end; (c) *varying amounts at varying dates*: the *product method*, each withdrawal multiplied by the months from its date to the year end, the products added and interest taken on the total × rate × 1/12. NCERT also works each withdrawal separately for the same result.
**Source:** NCERT 12-I ch. 1 §1.5.3 *Interest on Drawings*, p.20-23 (the quarterly illustration is on p.21-23; the product method begins on p.23) — fetched
**Example.** Professional: Suresh draws ₹30,000 on the first day of each quarter (₹1,20,000 in the year); 8%; interest = 1,20,000 × 8% × 7½/12 = ₹6,000: `Dr Suresh Current A/c ₹6,000 · Cr Interest on Drawings A/c ₹6,000` (this is NCERT's own illustration). Plain words: "Suresh took ₹30,000 every quarter. At 8% he is charged ₹6,000 for the use of that money."

### 1.6 Partner's salary and commission
Payable only if the deed provides. Credited to the partner's capital/current account through a *Salary to Partner* or *Commission to Partner* account and transferred to the Appropriation Account (entries in 1.3). No salary, commission or interest on capital is allowed if the firm makes a loss (NCERT's note, p.9).
**Source:** NCERT 12-I ch. 1 §1.2.1(e), p.4; §1.5.1 entries 4-5 and Note, p.9 — fetched
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Commission to Partner A/c ₹5,000 · Cr Ramesh Current A/c ₹5,000`, then `Dr P&L Appropriation A/c ₹5,000 · Cr Commission to Partner A/c ₹5,000`. Plain words: "The deed gives Ramesh ₹5,000 commission for bringing in orders; it is added to his account and comes off the profit before the split."

### 1.7 Guarantee of minimum profit to a partner
A partner (usually a new one) may be guaranteed a minimum share of profit. If his share by ratio falls short, the deficiency is made good by the guaranteeing partners in their profit-sharing ratio, unless one or more partners alone gave the guarantee, in which case only they bear it.
**Source:** NCERT 12-I ch. 1 §1.6 *Guarantee of Profit to a Partner* and Illustration 10, p.26-28; Summary item 6, p.36 — fetched
**Example.** NCERT's own figures: Mohit and Rohan share 2 : 1; Rahul is admitted for ¼ with a guarantee of ₹50,000; profit ₹1,60,000. Shares by ratio 80,000 / 40,000 / 40,000; deficiency ₹10,000 borne 6,667 / 3,333. Professional (the Appropriation Account's content written as an entry): `Dr P&L Appropriation A/c ₹1,60,000 · Cr Mohit Capital A/c ₹73,333 · Cr Rohan Capital A/c ₹36,667 · Cr Rahul Capital A/c ₹50,000`. Plain words: "Rahul's share on the ratio would be ₹40,000, but he was promised at least ₹50,000, so Mohit and Rohan make up the ₹10,000 between them, 2 to 1."

### 1.8 Past adjustments (errors found after the profit was divided)
An omitted or wrong item (interest on capital, interest on drawings, salary, commission) found after the accounts are final and the profit divided is corrected by an adjusting entry, without altering the old accounts: either through a *Profit and Loss Adjustment Account*, or directly between the partners' capital accounts after working out each partner's net effect.
**Source:** NCERT 12-I ch. 1 §1.7 *Past Adjustments* and Illustration 14, p.32-34; Summary item 7, p.36 — fetched
**Example.** NCERT's figures: Rameez (capital ₹50,000) and Zaheer (₹1,00,000), equal partners; 6% interest on capital was omitted. Professional (adjustment account): `Dr P&L Adjustment A/c ₹9,000 · Cr Rameez Capital A/c ₹3,000 · Cr Zaheer Capital A/c ₹6,000`, then `Dr Rameez Capital A/c ₹4,500 · Dr Zaheer Capital A/c ₹4,500 · Cr P&L Adjustment A/c ₹9,000`. Direct: `Dr Rameez Capital A/c ₹1,500 · Cr Zaheer Capital A/c ₹1,500`. Plain words: "We forgot to give each partner his interest and split the profit equally instead. Fixing it: Rameez had ₹1,500 too much, Zaheer ₹1,500 too little, so ₹1,500 moves from Rameez's account to Zaheer's."

### 1.9 Settlement between partners
The textbook's one direct partner-to-partner entry is the net transfer in a past adjustment (1.8): `Dr one partner's capital · Cr another's`. Interest on a partner's *loan* to the firm (1.1(d)) is separate from capital. A debit balance in a current account is shown as an asset (§1.4(a)). A partner paying a firm cost out of his own pocket, a partner repaying the firm, and settling a current account in cash are not described in the pages read.
**Source:** NCERT 12-I ch. 1 §1.7 (p.33) and §1.4(a) (p.5) — fetched. Own-pocket payment: not found in NCERT 12-I ch. 1-2.
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Ramesh Capital A/c ₹1,500 · Cr Zaheer Capital A/c ₹1,500`. Plain words: "₹1,500 is moved from Ramesh's account to Zaheer's to square what was wrongly divided."

### 1.10 A change of profit-sharing ratio during a year
When existing partners change their ratio without admission or retirement, one partner gains share and others lose. NCERT's examples change the ratio from the first day of the year ("with effect from April 1"). The change is settled by: goodwill (credit the sacrificing partners, debit the gaining partners, in the amounts of sacrifice/gain); reserves and accumulated profit or loss transferred to the partners' accounts in the **old** ratio on the date of change; and revaluation of assets and liabilities in the old ratio. For an admission part-way through a year NCERT dates the reserve transfer on the date of admission (Illustration 22, admission on 15 April). The sources I read do not say how the *year's* profit is split between the part of the year before the change and the part after.
**Source:** NCERT 12-I ch. 2 §2.9 *Change in Profit Sharing Ratio among the Existing Partners* and Illustration 29 (printed p.93-95); §2.6 *Adjustment for Accumulated Profits and Losses* and Illustration 22 (p.76-77) — fetched. Mid-year time-apportionment: keyword search of the chapter text found nothing; I did not read every illustration, so this is "not found", not "stated as absent".
**Example (NCERT Illustration 29 figures).** Dinesh, Ramesh, Suresh change from 3 : 3 : 2 to equal from 1 April; goodwill adjustment: Professional: `Dr Suresh Capital A/c ₹7,500 · Cr Dinesh Capital A/c ₹3,750 · Cr Ramesh Capital A/c ₹3,750`. Plain words: "From 1 April all three share equally. Suresh now gets a bigger share, so he pays Dinesh and Ramesh ₹3,750 each for it." Reserve (Illustration 22 figures): `Dr General Reserve A/c ₹20,000 · Cr Rajinder Capital A/c ₹16,000 · Cr Surinder Capital A/c ₹4,000`, "The ₹20,000 reserve built up so far is credited to the two old partners 4 to 1 when Narender joins."

### 1.11 An outside, sleeping or nominal partner
Not taught in the sources read. NCERT 12-I ch. 1 and ch. 2 contain no entry for a partner who invests but does not work, or a partner whose entries are made by another. The only mention of "outside" in ch. 2 is outside *liabilities* in goodwill valuation. By the textbook's rules a partner who does no work gets no salary unless the deed gives one (1.1(e)), and otherwise shares by ratio and keeps the same capital/current accounts as any partner.
**Source:** NCERT 12-I ch. 1, ch. 2 — fetched; keyword search for sleeping / nominal / outside partner found nothing; Grewal, Goel, ICAI BoS not fetched.

### 1.12 What ICAI asks each partner's account to show
For each owner's capital and current account the Guidance Note exposure draft asks for these items separately each year: opening balance; capital introduced; remuneration; interest; withdrawals; share of profit or loss (% and amount); closing balance.
**Source:** ICAI ED-NCE, General Instructions for the Balance Sheet, note 6A *Owners Funds*; Notes 3a and 3b (PDF pp.21, 52-53) — fetched (exposure draft)
**Example.** Professional, Ramesh from 1.3 (fixed capital; current account): opening `₹0`, remuneration `₹0`, interest `₹30,000`, withdrawals `(₹41,000)` incl. interest on drawings, share of profit `₹1,16,250`, closing `₹1,05,250`. Plain words: "Ramesh's current account at year end: ₹30,000 interest and ₹1,16,250 profit in, ₹41,000 out, ₹1,05,250 owed to him." (Mapping of interest on drawings into the withdrawals column is mine; the draft does not say.)

### 1.13 The BoS confirms the no-deed defaults, and adds that interest on a partner's loan is a charge, not an appropriation
With no agreement, no partner has a right to salary; no interest is allowed on capital or charged on drawings; interest at 6% a year is allowed on a partner's loan to the firm; profits and losses are shared equally; and interest and salary, where agreed, are paid only if there is profit. The BoS adds, in its solved illustration, that interest on a partner's loan "is not an appropriation of profit" and is charged to the Profit and Loss Account before the profit is divided. Its example: capitals ₹40,000 and ₹10,000, A also lent ₹20,000 on 1 July; profit ₹50,600; interest on A's loan for 6 months at 6% is ₹600; the remaining ₹50,000 is shared equally, ₹25,000 each. The BoS also says that when a partner takes money out for domestic purposes his capital account may be debited, or a separate Drawings (Current) Account opened; with fixed capitals the initial capital stays in the capital account and everything later goes through the current account, with no interest on the current-account balance; with fluctuating capitals all of it goes through the capital account.
**Source:** ICAI BoS, Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 10 Unit 1 §1.5 *Rules in the absence of Partnership Deed*, pp. 10.11–10.12; §1.7 *Accounts*, p. 10.14; §1.8 Illustration 2, pp. 10.16–10.17; §1.9 *Fixed and fluctuating capital*, pp. 10.19–10.21 — fetched (ICAI BoS PDF) https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74621bos60479-fnd-cp10-u1.pdf. Study material, not a pronouncement. (This agrees with §1.1, §1.2 above.)
**Example.** Professional (the BoS figures): `Dr Interest on Partner's Loan A/c ₹600 · Cr A's Account ₹600` (the BoS shows only the profit and loss charge; the account credited is not named), then `Dr Profit and Loss Appropriation A/c ₹50,000 · Cr A's Capital A/c ₹25,000 · Cr B's Capital A/c ₹25,000`. Plain words: "A lent the firm ₹20,000 for half a year; with no deed he gets 6%, ₹600, taken off before the split. The remaining ₹50,000 is shared half and half."

### 1.14 Interest on capital comes only out of profit; if profit is less than the interest, the profit is shared in the capital ratio
Subject to the partners' contract, interest on capital is provided out of profits only; in a loss none is provided. Where the profit is less than the interest on capital ("insufficient profits"), "the amount of profit is distributed in the ratio of capital as partners get profit by way of interest on capital". The BoS says nothing of a partner's salary in the same case.
**Source:** ICAI BoS, Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 10 Unit 1 §1.9, p. 10.21 (*Net loss and Interest on Capital*) — fetched (ICAI BoS PDF) https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74621bos60479-fnd-cp10-u1.pdf. Study material, not a pronouncement.
**Example.** Professional (own figures): capitals ₹1,00,000 (Ramesh) and ₹60,000 (Suresh), interest at 10% would be ₹10,000 and ₹6,000 = ₹16,000, but the profit is only ₹8,000: shared 5 : 3, `Dr Profit and Loss Appropriation A/c ₹8,000 · Cr Ramesh Current A/c ₹5,000 · Cr Suresh Current A/c ₹3,000`. Plain words: "The deed gives each 10% on capital, which needs ₹16,000, but the year made only ₹8,000; it is split in proportion to capital, ₹5,000 and ₹3,000."

### 1.15 A bill paid from a partner's own pocket is capital he has brought in
Among the fresh investments by proprietors or partners the BoS lists "payments made to creditors out of personal cash" and money put in from private sources; the profit-by-capital-comparison method deducts such "fresh capital introduced". The BoS also treats a partner's *loan* to the firm as a separate thing, advanced "in addition to capital" and carrying interest at 6% unless agreed otherwise (Ch. 10 Unit 1, p. 10.31). It does not say how to tell a partner's own-pocket payment from a loan if the partners have not agreed which it is.
**Source:** ICAI BoS, Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 9 §4.5, pp. 9.22–9.23, and §3.1, p. 9.6; Ch. 10 Unit 1 summary points 4 and 7, pp. 10.31–10.32 — fetched (ICAI BoS PDF) https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74620bos60479-fnd-cp9.pdf; https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74621bos60479-fnd-cp10-u1.pdf. Study material, not a pronouncement.
**Example.** Professional: Ramesh pays the firm's supplier Kapoor Traders ₹4,000 from his own pocket: `Dr Kapoor Traders A/c ₹4,000 · Cr Ramesh Capital A/c ₹4,000` (a current account if capitals are fixed, §1.2). Plain words: "Ramesh paid the firm's ₹4,000 bill out of his own pocket; it counts as money he put in."

### 1.16 Profit up to the date a partner leaves or dies: time basis or turnover basis
For a partner who dies part-way through a year, the BoS finds his share of profit to the date of death by one of two methods: *time basis* (profit assumed earned evenly through the year) or *turnover (sales) basis* (last year's profit rate on sales applied to sales since the year began). The profit is calculated through a *Profit and Loss Suspense Account* and the share credited to the deceased partner's account. The BoS states this for death; it does not give a rule for splitting a year's profit between two ratios when the *ratio changes* mid-year without a partner leaving (§1.17 shows what it does on a change of ratio).
**Source:** ICAI BoS, Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 10 Unit 5 §5.3, pp. 10.173–10.174 — fetched (ICAI BoS PDF) https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74625bos60479-fnd-cp10-u5.pdf. Study material, not a pronouncement.
**Example.** Professional (the BoS figures): Neha, one-sixth partner, dies on 31 May; last year's sales ₹4,00,000 and profit ₹60,000 (15%); sales 1 April to 31 May ₹1,00,000, so profit ₹15,000 and Neha's share ₹2,500: `Dr Profit and Loss Suspense A/c ₹2,500 · Cr Neha's Capital A/c ₹2,500`. Plain words: "Neha died on 31 May. The shop made 15 paise profit on each rupee of sales last year; sales so far ₹1,00,000, so profit ₹15,000, her sixth is ₹2,500."

### 1.17 A change of ratio: the gaining partners pay the losing ones through their capital accounts, and no goodwill account is raised
When the profit-sharing ratio changes among the existing partners, goodwill is valued and the gaining partners compensate the sacrificing partners through the partners' capital accounts in the gain/sacrifice ratio; "in no case the goodwill account is to be raised" for an internally generated goodwill. The BoS example: A, B, C equal partners change to 4 : 3 : 2; goodwill ₹90,000; A gains 1/9, B no change, C loses 1/9, so A pays C ₹90,000 × 1/9 = ₹10,000. (NCERT's version is in §1.10.)
**Source:** ICAI BoS, Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 10 Unit 2 §2.1, pp. 10.43–10.44; §2.6, pp. 10.61–10.62 — fetched (ICAI BoS PDF) https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74622bos60479-fnd-cp10-u2.pdf. Study material, not a pronouncement.
**Example.** Professional (the BoS figures): `Dr A's Capital A/c ₹10,000 · Cr C's Capital A/c ₹10,000`. Plain words: "A, B and C used to share equally and now share 4 : 3 : 2. A takes a bigger share, so A pays C one-ninth of the firm's goodwill, ₹10,000, through their capital accounts; nothing is added to the books for goodwill."

## 2. Where the sources disagree, or are silent
- **Interest on capital and partner salary: appropriation or charge.** NCERT 12-I treats them as appropriations of profit (below the profit line); the ICAI exposure draft shows partners' remuneration and interest on partners' capital as charges before arriving at profit. Both credit the partner's account. Only the exposure draft was read; the final Guidance Note may differ.
- **Interest on capital when profit is too small.** NCERT says no interest or salary in a *loss*; it does not say what happens when there is a profit smaller than the interest and salary together. Silent.
- **Own-pocket payments, settling a current account, drawings entered for an absent partner:** silent in NCERT 12-I ch. 1-2.
- **Outside / sleeping partner:** silent in NCERT; ICAI draft has no separate treatment.
- **Mid-year ratio change and the year's profit:** silent in the chapter text searched.
- **Practice in small firms (bahi-khata, who keeps a current account):** none of the sources fetched describes it.
- **ICAI BoS material (Foundation Paper 1 Ch. 10 and Ch. 9, read 20 Oct 2026):** compared with NCERT in §1.13–§1.17. The BoS agrees with NCERT on the no-deed defaults; it treats partner-loan interest as a charge before the appropriation account (Ch. 10 Unit 1 p. 10.17); it allows drawings to be debited to capital or to a separate drawings/current account (p. 10.14); and it states a rule for insufficient profit (profit shared in the capital ratio, p. 10.21) that NCERT does not. It is silent on a sleeping, nominal or outside partner (its only mention of a family is the HUF, as a relation arising from status and not from contract, Ch. 10 Unit 1 p. 10.3). Its death-date profit example prints the previous year's profit as ₹2,25,000 and then computes from ₹1,25,000 (p. 10.174); the sales-basis example beside it is consistent and is the one used in §1.16. NCERT is a school textbook and the draft Guidance Note is a presentation format; neither is a bookkeeping manual for small firms.

## 4. Answers to the GAPS items this topic settles
- **B1 (appropriation computed or posted; interest on capital optional, paid only out of profit):** the textbook posts it by journal entries (§0, 1.3). Interest on capital is optional — allowed only if agreed (1.1(b), 1.4). "Only out of profit": only the loss case is settled (no interest, salary or remuneration in a loss, §1.5.1 Note); a profit smaller than the interest is not settled, see §3.
- **B2 (fixed capital plus current account vs one fluctuating account):** both are taught; fluctuating is the default when nothing is said (1.2; NCERT 12-I §1.4). The ICAI exposure draft allows both (1.12).
- **B3 (a partner pays a firm cost from his pocket):** not settled; the textbook has no such entry, see §3.
- **B4 (partnership openings are each partner's agreed figures):** not settled for the opening entry itself; the textbook says all partner money is recorded through capital accounts, see §3.
- **B5 (outside partner's drawings entered on instruction):** not settled, see 1.11 and §3.
- **F-07, D7, D14, D19:** text not read (tracing only); the findings above answer the topic's questions.
