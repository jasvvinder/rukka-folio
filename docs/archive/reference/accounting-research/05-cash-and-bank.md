# 05 — Cash and bank

**Questions (from the outline):** Cash book columns. Contra entries. Bank reconciliation statement. Credit card and wallet as accounts. Cash withdrawals and deposits.
**Settles (tracing only):** GAPS A6, C1; D17, D18

Source key used below: **[N4]** NCERT Class 11 Accountancy, Financial Accounting Part I, Ch. 4 "Recording of Transactions – II" (file keac104.pdf, 2026-27 reprint), §4.1. **[N5]** same book, Ch. 5 "Bank Reconciliation Statement" (keac105.pdf). Full URLs in §5. Page numbers are not given because the extracted text did not reliably show them; section headings are.

## 1. Findings

### 1.1 The cash book is both a book of first entry and the cash/bank ledger
A cash book records every cash receipt on the left (Dr) and every cash payment on the right (Cr), starting from the cash or bank balance at the start of the period. It serves as both journal and ledger for cash. When it is kept, cash transactions are not entered in the journal, and no separate cash (or bank) account is opened in the ledger.
**Source:** [N4] §4.1 (opening paragraph) — fetched
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Cash A/c ₹5,000 · Cr Ramesh A/c ₹5,000` (entered on the receipts side of the cash book; the ledger posting is to Ramesh's account only). Plain words: "Received ₹5,000 in cash from Ramesh."

### 1.2 Single-column and double-column cash book; the bank column is the bank account
A single-column cash book has one amount column on each side and records cash only. A double-column cash book adds a bank column on each side. Receipts into the bank go on the Dr side, withdrawals and payments through the bank on the Cr side. When the bank column is kept, the bank account is not opened in the ledger; the bank column serves as the bank account.
**Source:** [N4] §4.1.1 and §4.1.2 (and the posting passage after Fig. 4.5) — fetched
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Bank A/c ₹12,000 · Cr Kapoor Traders A/c ₹12,000` (cheque received from Kapoor Traders and banked the same day: bank column, Dr side). Plain words: "Kapoor Traders paid ₹12,000 by cheque; put it straight into the bank."

### 1.3 Contra entries: cash deposited or withdrawn
When cash is paid into the bank, the amount is written on the Dr side in the bank column and on the Cr side in the cash column. The reverse is entered when cash is withdrawn. Both sides appear in the cash book, so the letter **C** (contra) is written in the L.F. column and the entry is *not posted* to any ledger account. A cheque received but banked on a later day is entered as cash on the date of receipt; on the day of banking it is a contra (Dr bank column, Cr cash column).
**Source:** [N4] §4.1.2 ("both the entries are recorded in the cash book... C, which stands for contra"; "This is a contra entry"; posting passage: "Entries marked C ... are ignored while posting") — fetched
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Bank A/c ₹20,000 · Cr Cash A/c ₹20,000` (contra, marked C). Plain words: "Put ₹20,000 of the cash in hand into the bank." Withdrawal: `Dr Cash A/c ₹10,000 · Cr Bank A/c ₹10,000` — "Took ₹10,000 out of the bank for the till."

### 1.4 Balances: cash is always Dr; the bank column can go Cr (overdraft)
The cash column always has a debit balance, because cash payments cannot exceed receipts plus the opening balance. The bank column can have a credit balance, which is an overdraft: cash withdrawn from the bank exceeds what was deposited. NCERT lists bank overdraft among current liabilities in the balance sheet. A cash book is balanced like a ledger account and the balance is carried down.
**Source:** [N4] §4.1.2 and §4.1.4; NCERT Part II Ch. 8 "Financial Statements – I" (classification of current liabilities, keac201.pdf) — fetched
**Example.** Professional: bank column closes `Cr ₹3,200` — shown as `Bank overdraft ₹3,200` under current liabilities. Plain words: "The bank book shows I owe the bank ₹3,200; I have drawn more than I put in."

### 1.5 Bank charges, interest, credits and dishonoured cheques enter the bank column
Charges, interest or commission debited by the bank go on the Cr side of the bank column. Amounts credited by the bank go on the Dr side. A cheque received from a customer and returned unpaid is entered on the Cr side of the bank column against the customer's name, restoring the position before the cheque was received.
**Source:** [N4] §4.1.2 (last paragraphs before Fig. 4.5) — fetched
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Bank Charges A/c ₹120 · Cr Bank A/c ₹120`. Plain words: "Bank took ₹120 as service charges." Dishonour: `Dr Kapoor Traders A/c ₹12,000 · Cr Bank A/c ₹12,000` — "Kapoor Traders' cheque for ₹12,000 bounced; he owes us again."

### 1.6 Why cash book and passbook differ, and the reconciliation statement
The bank statement (passbook) is the bank's copy of the account; it shows deposits as credits and withdrawals as debits, so a favourable balance is a credit in the passbook while it is a debit in the cash book. The two should agree but usually do not, for two reasons only: **timing differences** and **errors**. Timing differences listed: (a) cheques issued but not yet presented; (b) cheques paid in but not yet collected; (c) direct debits by the bank (charges, overdraft interest, bounced or stopped cheques); (d) amounts deposited directly by customers; (e) interest and dividends collected by the bank; (f) payments by the bank on standing instruction; (g) deposited cheques or discounted bills dishonoured. Errors may be the firm's or the bank's. The **Bank Reconciliation Statement** is a statement, dated at its head, that starts from the cash-book (or passbook) balance and adds or deducts these items until it reaches the other balance.
**Source:** [N5] §5.1, §5.1.1(a)–(g), §5.1.2, §5.2 — fetched
**Example.** From the textbook's pattern (own figures): cash book balance ₹50,000; cheque issued ₹6,000 not presented; cheque deposited ₹6,000 not yet credited; dividend collected by bank ₹8,000; charges ₹400 not entered. Passbook = 50,000 + 6,000 − 6,000 + 8,000 − 400 = ₹57,600. Professional (entries the firm makes afterwards): `Dr Bank A/c ₹8,000 · Cr Dividend Income A/c ₹8,000` and `Dr Bank Charges A/c ₹400 · Cr Bank A/c ₹400`. Plain words: "Bank has credited ₹8,000 dividend I did not know of, and taken ₹400 charges; write both into my bank book."

### 1.7 Direction of each adjustment, starting from the cash-book balance
Starting from a favourable cash-book balance: add cheques issued but not presented, direct deposits and bank credits (interest, dividend); deduct cheques deposited but not yet credited, bank charges, standing-instruction payments and dishonoured items. If the passbook balance is the starting point, every treatment is reversed. An overdraft (credit in the cash book, debit in the passbook) is the unfavourable case, with its own two starting points. Errors are corrected by the rules of rectification (Ch. 6).
**Source:** [N5] §5.2.1 (the four situations; steps (i)–(viii) for favourable balances) — fetched
**Example.** Professional: BRS lines `Balance as per cash book ₹50,000 · + Cheques issued not presented ₹6,000 · − Cheque deposited not credited ₹6,000 · …`. Plain words: "Start from my book's ₹50,000; cheques I wrote but the shop has not banked yet make the bank's figure higher; cheques I banked but the bank has not cleared make it lower."

### 1.8 What NCERT says about doing it in practice
NCERT says the statement can be prepared without or after adjusting the cash book balance, and that "in practice, the bank reconciliation statement is prepared after adjusting the cash book balance". The extracted text I read shows the "without adjusting" method (§5.2.1) in detail; I did not find the "after adjusting" section in that text.
**Source:** [N5] §5.2 — fetched (the promised later section: not seen)
**Example.** Professional: the adjusting entries are those of 1.5/1.6 posted into the cash book first (`Dr Bank A/c ₹8,000 · Cr Dividend Income A/c ₹8,000`). Plain words: "First write in what the bank did that I missed, then compare."

### 1.9 Cash withdrawals and deposits as a ledger statement
Deposits and withdrawals between cash and bank are contra entries (1.3). The sources teach this only for a business's own cash and its own bank account.
**Source:** [N4] §4.1.2 — fetched
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Cash A/c ₹10,000 · Cr Bank A/c ₹10,000`. Plain words: "Drew ₹10,000 from the bank."

### 1.10 A sale paid by credit or debit card is a cash sale to the seller; the bank's commission is a selling expense
From the seller's point of view a sale through a credit or debit card is equivalent to a cash sale. The seller submits the card slips to his bank; the bank credits the seller's bank account (and debits the card issuer) and charges a commission, 1% to 4% on each transaction, debited at once to the seller's bank account. The BoS describes the card holder's side only in words: the issuing bank sends a monthly statement; a debit card debits the holder's account immediately; a credit card is paid in full or in part, and interest is charged if it is not paid in full. It gives no entries for the holder.
**Source:** ICAI BoS, Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 2 Unit 5 *Cash Book*, §5.5 *Entries for sale through credit/debit cards*, pp. 2.117–2.118 — fetched (ICAI BoS PDF) https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74610bos60479-fnd-cp2-u5.pdf. Study material, not a pronouncement.
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Bank A/c ₹5,000 · Cr Sales A/c ₹5,000`; commission at 2%: `Dr Commission A/c ₹100 · Cr Bank A/c ₹100`. Plain words: "Sold goods for ₹5,000 on a customer's card; the bank put ₹5,000 into my account and took ₹100 as its charge."

### 1.11 The BoS chapter on the reconciliation statement: how often, and when the cash book must be corrected
Reconciliation is done at regular intervals, "such as monthly or quarterly"; how often depends on the size of the organisation and the number of transactions. Correcting (adjusting) the cash book before preparing the statement is "completely optional" if reconciliation is done during the year; if it is done at the end of the year, the cash book *must* be adjusted so the balance sheet shows the correct bank balance. The adjusted cash book takes in the cash book's own errors and omissions (bank charges, interest debited by the bank, direct receipts and payments by the bank, dishonoured cheques or bills); timing differences (cheques issued but not presented, cheques deposited but not cleared) stay in the statement; errors in the pass book are not adjusted in the cash book. Payments the bank makes on standing instruction, such as insurance premium or loan EMIs, are known to the customer only from the pass book or statement and are entered then. Business houses should also obtain a stamped and signed bank certificate of the balance at the year end.
**Source:** ICAI BoS, Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 3 *Bank Reconciliation Statement*, §2 *Background* and §3 *Bank pass book*, p. 3.4; §5 causes (vi), p. 3.10; §7.2.1 *Meaning of adjusted cash book*, p. 3.25 — fetched (ICAI BoS PDF) https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74612bos60479-fnd-cp3.pdf. Study material, not a pronouncement.
**Example.** Professional (own figures): cash book bank balance ₹40,000; the pass book shows bank charges ₹300 and an insurance premium paid on standing instruction ₹1,500 not yet in the cash book; a cheque of ₹2,000 issued has not been presented. Adjust the cash book: `Dr Bank Charges A/c ₹300 · Dr Insurance Premium A/c ₹1,500 · Cr Bank A/c ₹1,800` (adjusted balance ₹38,200). Statement: adjusted cash book ₹38,200 + cheque issued not presented ₹2,000 = pass book ₹40,200. Plain words: "The bank has taken ₹300 charges and paid ₹1,500 insurance for me. I write both into my bank book first; then the only difference left is the ₹2,000 cheque the shop has not banked yet."

### 1.12 Cash drawn from the bank for private use is drawings, not a contra entry
A contra entry covers cash moved between the business's own cash and bank: "cash is withdrawn from bank for use in the office" (bank column on the payments side, cash column on the receipts side, letter C, no further posting). In the incomplete-records chapter, "cash withdrawals to meet family expenses" are listed among the items of drawings.
**Source:** ICAI BoS, Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 2 Unit 5, §5.2 point 4 *Contra entries*, p. 2.109; Ch. 9 *Accounts from Incomplete Records*, §4.4 *Distinction between business expenses and drawings*, p. 9.22 — fetched (ICAI BoS PDF) https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74610bos60479-fnd-cp2-u5.pdf; https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74620bos60479-fnd-cp9.pdf.
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Drawings A/c ₹10,000 · Cr Bank A/c ₹10,000` (for the house), against the contra `Dr Cash A/c ₹10,000 · Cr Bank A/c ₹10,000` (for the shop till). Plain words: "Drew ₹10,000 from the bank for house expenses: it is the owner's own withdrawal, not money moved to the till."

## 2. Where the sources disagree, or are silent
- **Credit card and wallet as accounts:** NCERT Part I Ch. 4–5 and Part II Ch. 8–9 (extracted text) do not mention credit cards or wallets. ICAI Foundation (BoS) Chapter 3 is titled "Bank Reconciliation Statement" (confirmed 20 Oct 2026 in the Aug 2026 edition, contents and chapter text read). Silent; see §3. (Update 20 Oct 2026: the BoS chapter text was read; it covers the seller's side of cards only — see below.)
- **Bank overdraft as a card-like running credit:** NCERT treats overdraft only as a credit balance in the bank column; it does not discuss a limit, interest accrual or a cash-credit account.
- **BRS "after adjusting the cash book":** announced in [N5] §5.2 but not seen in the extracted text (possible extraction gap).
- **Personal drawings or a household's withdrawals** are outside the contra rule as taught (business cash and bank only).
- Traditional practice (rokad bahi) was not researched in this note.
- **BoS Foundation Ch. 2 Unit 5 and Ch. 3 read (20 Oct 2026).** The BoS covers a credit or debit card only from the seller's side (§1.10) and says nothing of a card holder's own credit-card account, a wallet or a UPI balance; silent, see §3. It describes the overdraft as the credit balance of the bank column (Ch. 2 Unit 5, p. 2.110; Ch. 3 §3, p. 3.5: a debit balance in the pass book is the overdraft), and its model balance sheet lists "Cash Credit" and "Overdrafts" as two separate short-term loan heads (Ch. 7 Unit 1 §1.6, p. 7.39); it gives no account or entry for a cash-credit limit and does not mention KCC.
- **BRS "after adjusting the cash book":** the gap noted above is filled by the BoS (§1.11, Ch. 3 §7.2.1): adjusting is optional during the year and required at the year end. NCERT's "in practice ... after adjusting" and the BoS agree in direction; the BoS adds the year-end requirement.

## 4. Answers to the GAPS items this topic settles
- **A6 (bank statement → entries):** practice teaches that each bank-statement line is matched to the cash book; items in the statement but not in the book (charges, interest, direct credits, standing payments, dishonours) are entered into the bank column (§1.5, §1.6). Each line then takes the account of what it was (customer, expense, income). The two-debit instalment line is not covered by these sources (see topic 08). NCERT [N5] §5.1.1(c)–(g).
- **C1 (credit card as a money account that sits Cr):** not settled, see §3. Only the overdraft (Cr bank column) is sourced [N4 §4.1.2]; the BoS treats cards from the seller's side only (§1.10).
- **D17, D18:** open decisions; the questions they raise (credit card/wallet, overdraft) are in §3.
