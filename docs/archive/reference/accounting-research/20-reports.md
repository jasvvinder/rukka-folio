# 20 — Reports

**Questions (from the outline):** What each entity type is expected to produce. Ledger statement for any period. Day book. Positions. Ageing of persons. What every report must state at its head: the book, the date or range, the basis, whether it is live or closed, and when it was made.
**Settles (tracing only):** GAPS A8; D9, D10

Method note. Same as topic 19: NCERT PDFs (Reprint 2026-27) read in full for the sections cited; the ICAI Technical Guide (June 2022) and AS 1 read at source; the ICAI Guidance Note (Aug 2023) **not** readable (see §5, item 4). Figures marked "constructed" are mine.

## 1. Findings

### 1.1 Sole proprietor: a Trading and Profit and Loss Account and a Balance Sheet, from the trial balance
"The firm usually prepares the following financial statements: 1. Trading and Profit and Loss Account, 2. Balance Sheet." The first shows performance (profit or loss); the second shows position (assets, liabilities, capital). They are prepared from the trial balance and additional information. Objectives: a true and fair view of financial performance and of financial position. Notes and explanatory statements form part of the financial statements.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11, Ch 8 §8.3 and Summary item 1 — fetched.
**Example (NCERT, §8.4).** Professional: Ankit's trading and profit and loss account for the year to 31 March: income ₹1,30,000 (sales ₹1,25,000 + commission ₹5,000), expenses ₹1,25,500, profit ₹4,500; `Dr Profit and Loss A/c ₹4,500 · Cr Capital A/c ₹4,500`. Plain words: "A year's page of what came in and what went out: ₹4,500 left over, added to Ankit's own money in the business."

### 1.2 Partnership: the same two statements plus the appropriation account and the partners' capital (and current) accounts
After the profit and loss account, a Profit and Loss Appropriation Account (an "extension of the Profit and Loss Account") shows how profit is shared after salary, commission, interest on capital and interest on drawings. Partners' capital accounts are kept fixed (with current accounts) or fluctuating.
**Source:** NCERT Class 12 Accountancy Part I, Ch 1 §1.4, §1.4.1, §1.5.1 and Fig. 1.3 — fetched. (Topic 14 holds the detail.)
**Example (NCERT, Ch 1 Illustration 1, rounded).** Professional: `Dr Profit and Loss Appropriation A/c ₹12,000 · Cr Partners' Capital A/cs ₹12,000` (Amit ₹6,000, Babu ₹4,000, Charu ₹2,000). Plain words: "After the partners' salary, commission and interest, ₹12,000 was left to share three ways in the 3:2:1 ratio."

### 1.3 Not-for-profit bodies: Receipts and Payments Account, Income and Expenditure Account, Balance Sheet
Clubs, hospitals, educational institutions and similar bodies "conventionally prepare Receipts and Payments Account and Income and Expenditure Account to show periodic performance and Balance Sheet to show financial position". The Receipts and Payments Account is a classified summary of the cash book with opening and closing cash and bank balances, is not part of the double-entry system, and cannot show surplus or deficit; it "is not favoured except where the activities are simple and modest, involve no carry over from one period to the next and it has no assets, apart from cash balance and no liabilities". The Income and Expenditure Account is on the accrual basis, excludes capital receipts and payments, and its balance is the surplus or deficit. Detail belongs to topic 17.
**Source:** ICAI BoS virtual-coaching deck "9. Financial Statements of Not-for-Profit Organisations", Concepts 1–4 — fetched (a faculty slide deck hosted on live.icai.org). Update 20 Oct 2026: the study-material chapter itself was read — ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1, Ch. 8 §1, p. 8.2 says non-profit bodies "prepare Receipts and Payments Account and Income and Expenditure Account ... and Balance Sheet" — fetched (ICAI BoS PDF https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74619bos60479-fnd-cp8.pdf). NCERT Class 12 NPO chapter — **not found** in the reprint chapters fetched.
**Example (constructed).** Professional: subscriptions received ₹40,000 in cash: `Dr Cash A/c ₹40,000 · Cr Subscriptions A/c ₹40,000`; Receipts and Payments shows ₹40,000 on the receipts side; Income and Expenditure shows only the subscriptions that belong to the year. Plain words: "The cash page shows every rupee that came in; the income page shows only what belongs to this year."

### 1.4 The ICAI recommended formats for non-corporate entities: a Balance Sheet, a Statement of Profit and Loss, and notes; a cash flow statement for Level I
The Technical Guide (superseded by the 2023 Guidance Note, not read) says a complete set normally includes a balance sheet, a statement of profit and loss, a cash flow statement and notes, including significant accounting policies. It covers sole proprietorships, HUFs, partnership firms, AOPs, societies, trusts (registered or not), statutory corporations and autonomous bodies, and any organisation engaged wholly or partly in business or professional activity "unless their activities are fully charitable in nature". It excludes LLPs and any entity for which a statute, regulator or authority prescribes formats (the Guide gives the Maharashtra Public Trust Rules formats and ICAI guidance for educational institutions and political parties as examples). The formats are "recommended"; they "can be applied by all non-corporate entities". Entities are classed Level I to IV by turnover and borrowings; Level I must comply with all Accounting Standards. A Level II–IV entity need not prepare a cash flow statement (the Guide's Chapter V says AS 3 "may not include cash flow statements … encouraged to comply"; its Annexure 2 table marks AS 3 "Not Applicable" for Levels II–IV). No NPO-specific form (Receipts and Payments, Income and Expenditure) appears in the Guide's formats.
**Source:** ICAI Technical Guide, Preface; Ch I ("Non-Corporate Entities — a wide spectrum"); Ch II; Ch V; Ch VI; Appendix A Annexures 1 and 2 — fetched. ICAI Guidance Note (Aug 2023) — **not fetched**; secondary summaries seen only in search results (cleartax.in, caclub.in) repeat that it covers the same entities, has four levels and illustrative formats, with conflicting effective dates (1 April 2024, 1 April 2025, 1 April 2026), so I do not state one.
**Example (constructed).** Professional: a Level IV sole trader's year: `Dr Rent A/c ₹96,000 · Cr Cash A/c ₹96,000` appears in the Statement of Profit and Loss under "Other expenses"; no cash flow statement is required. Plain words: "Paid a year's shop rent, ₹96,000; it shows as an expense on the profit statement; a small shop need not also draw up a cash-flow statement."

### 1.5 Every ICAI-format statement carries the entity name, the period or date, the unit, the previous period and a link to the notes
The Technical Guide's Balance Sheet form is headed "Name of the Non-Corporate Entity … Balance Sheet as at … (Rupees in …)", with columns Particulars, Note No., figures as at the end of the current and the previous reporting period (each with a DD/MM/YYYY date). The Statement of Profit and Loss is headed "Name of the Non-Corporate Entity … Statement of Profit and loss for the year ended …", with current and previous period "From … To …" in DD/MM/YYYY. General Instructions: each item on the face is cross-referenced to the notes (item 3(ii)); the unit of rounding is chosen by total income and "once a unit of measurement is used, it should be used uniformly" (item 4); comparatives for the preceding period are given except in the first statements (item 5). The illustrative statements also show the lines "Summary of significant accounting policies" and "The accompanying notes are an integral part of the financial statements". The Guide does **not** require a stated basis line, a live/closed status, or the date the report was made.
**Source:** ICAI Technical Guide, Ch VI General Instructions items 3–5; Part I and Part II forms; Appendix B — fetched.
**Example (form, constructed names).** Professional: `Kapoor Traders · Balance Sheet as at 31/03/2027 · (Rupees in thousands)`; line `Owners' Capital Account … Note 3 … ₹1,25,000 (2027) · ₹1,10,000 (2026)`. Plain words: "Each page says whose books, what date, in what unit, and gives last year beside this year."

### 1.6 Policies and basis: AS 1 asks for significant accounting policies to be disclosed in one place as part of the statements; the three fundamental assumptions need no statement unless not followed
All significant accounting policies adopted in preparing and presenting the financial statements should be disclosed (¶18, ¶24); the disclosure should form part of the financial statements (¶19, ¶25) and should "normally be disclosed in one place" (¶20, ¶25); a material change in policy, and its amount, should be disclosed (¶22, ¶26). The fundamental assumptions (going concern, consistency, accrual) are "usually not specifically stated"; if followed, "specific disclosure is not required"; "if a fundamental accounting assumption is not followed, the fact should be disclosed" (¶9, ¶27). The primary consideration in choosing policies is a true and fair view, with prudence, substance over form and materiality governing (¶16–17). AS 1 applies to every level of non-company entity (Technical Guide, Appendix A Annexure 2, row AS 1). So a book that is not on the accrual basis is, on these sources, expected to say so (¶27) and to state its policies once, in the statements. An MSME that uses ICAI's exemptions must state in a note that it is an MSME, which Level, and that it has complied with the standards as they apply to that Level.
**Source:** AS 1 ¶9–10, 16–20, 22, 24–27 — fetched (checked against the ICAI PDF, 20 Oct 2026) (indasaccess.icai.org). ICAI Technical Guide, Appendix A Annexure 1 "Additional requirements" (1), Annexure 2 — fetched.
**Example (constructed).** Professional: no entry. Note to the accounts: "Basis: cash basis; depreciation not provided." with the profit figure `Rent received ₹60,000 (cash)` on the statement. Plain words: "At the foot of the report, one line says the book counts money when it moves, and that nothing was set aside for wear and tear."

### 1.7 Positions: the balance sheet is the position statement at one date; it is "true only on that date"
A balance sheet is "a statement of assets and liabilities … at a given date"; "information contained in a balance sheet is true only on that date"; totals of assets and liabilities (with capital) always equal. It is headed "Balance Sheet of … as at …". NCERT Class 11 teaches the trial balance as a statement "as on" a date that may be prepared "at the end of any chosen period, … monthly, quarterly, half yearly or annually". A statement of affairs (assets and liabilities listed to find capital where books are single-entry) is **not** in any NCERT chapter fetched. A search result lists "Accounts from Incomplete Records" as ICAI BoS Intermediate Module 2 Chapter 10, and secondary pages describe profit as closing capital − opening capital + drawings − capital introduced; the ICAI chapter text was not fetched.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11, Ch 8 §8.6 and Summary item 3; Ch 6 §6.1 — fetched. Statement of affairs: ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1, Ch. 9 §3, pp. 9.6–9.10 — fetched (ICAI BoS PDF https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74620bos60479-fnd-cp9.pdf), see §1.11; the earlier search-only reference (https://www.icai.org/post/12430) is no longer needed.
**Example (constructed).** Professional: statement of affairs at 31 March: assets ₹2,10,000 less liabilities ₹60,000 = capital ₹1,50,000; `Dr Cash A/c ₹10,000 · Dr Stock A/c ₹1,00,000 · Dr Debtors A/c ₹1,00,000 · Cr Creditors A/c ₹60,000 · Cr Capital A/c ₹1,50,000` as the opening entry. Plain words: "Wrote down everything I owned and owed on the day; the difference, ₹1,50,000, is my own money in the business."

### 1.8 Day book: the textbooks use "day books" for the subsidiary journals, not for one book of everything
NCERT lists the books of original entry as journal proper, cash book and "other day books" (purchases, sales, purchase returns, sales returns, bills receivable, bills payable books). The cash book is "balanced in the same way as an account" and its balance carried down. NCERT does not present a single all-transactions day book or a rokad. The traditional rokad bahi was not researched here (not fetched).
**Source:** NCERT Class 11, Ch 3 §3.4 (Books of Original Entry); Ch 4 §4.1.4 — fetched. Mahajani rokad/khata bahi — **not fetched**. Corroborated by ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1, Ch. 2 Unit 1, p. 2.2 (books of original entry: "Purchase day book, Cash book, Sales day book and Purchases returns ...") and Ch. 9 §4.1, p. 9.14 ("Purchases Day Book, Sales Day Book, Purchase Return Book ...") — fetched (ICAI BoS PDF): the BoS also uses "day books" for the subsidiary books, not for one book of everything.
**Example (constructed).** Professional: sale on credit of ₹5,000 to Mohan: `Dr Mohan A/c ₹5,000 · Cr Sales A/c ₹5,000`, first entered in the Sales (journal) book. Plain words: "Sold ₹5,000 of goods to Mohan on credit; written first in the sales day book, then on Mohan's page."

### 1.9 A ledger statement for any period is not named as a report in the textbooks; the supporting practice is balancing accounts at any date and the bank passbook as "statement of account"
Each ledger account is balanced (Balance c/d and Balance b/d); a trial balance may be drawn at any chosen date. NCERT describes the bank passbook as "the statement of account of the customer maintained by the bank". "Account Current" appears in the BoS Foundation Module 1 contents (Chapter 6, "Meaning of Account Current, Methods of preparing Account Current"), but the chapter text was not fetched. No source read prescribes the content or heading of a ledger extract for a chosen range.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11, Ch 5 "Bank Reconciliation Statement", Test Your Understanding III item 1 (a true/false statement, not body text); Ch 6 §6.1; Ch 4 §4.1.4 — fetched. ICAI BoS Foundation Module 1 contents pages — fetched (contents only). Update 20 Oct 2026: in the Aug 2026 edition Ch. 6 is "Bills of Exchange and Promissory Notes" (contents pages and the chapter text read); it has no "Account Current" section, and no chapter has a statement of one account for a chosen period beyond the ledger account itself (Ch. 2 Unit 2 §2.2, p. 2.53).
**Example (constructed).** Professional: Mohan A/c, 1 April to 30 June: opening `Dr ₹2,000`, sales `Dr ₹5,000`, receipt `Cr ₹3,000`, closing `Dr ₹4,000 c/d`. Plain words: "Mohan's page for April to June: he owed ₹2,000, bought ₹5,000 more, paid ₹3,000, owes ₹4,000."

### 1.10 Ageing of debtors and creditors: no ICAI pronouncement or NCERT text read covers it
NCERT Class 11 Ch 9 §9.9 treats provision for doubtful debts as a percentage of debtors; the sources fetched do not mention an ageing schedule. Search results show ageing schedules in general accounting-course sites (secondary) and one ICAI Expert Advisory Committee query that quotes a parent company's policy; none is an ICAI pronouncement or textbook rule.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11, Ch 9 §9.9 — fetched (no ageing). Ageing in general — **search only** (https://eacopinion.icai.org/html/35/4.html is a query reproducing a company policy, not a rule).
**Example (constructed).** Professional: no entry for the schedule itself; provision from bands (constructed): `Dr Profit and Loss A/c ₹1,200 · Cr Provision for doubtful debts A/c ₹1,200` (the entry form NCERT §9.9 uses) on 30 days ₹20,000 at 1% and 90 days ₹10,000 at 10% (₹200 + ₹1,000). Plain words: "Listed who owes me by how long it has been; the older the debt, the more I set aside against it."

### 1.11 The statement of affairs in the BoS: what it is, when it is used, and how it differs from a balance sheet
When a small business keeps incomplete records, the accountant prepares a *statement of affairs* at two dates: a list laid out like a balance sheet, with capital as the balancing figure, from which opening and closing capital, and so the profit, are found. The BoS says it differs from a balance sheet: it rests on records partly single entry and partly double entry, with many assets taken from estimates and memory; capital is "merely a balancing figure" rather than coming from the capital account; omissions are hard to detect; assets are valued arbitrarily and no method is disclosed; its object is to find capital at the beginning and end of the period, where a balance sheet's is financial position. The BoS does not say that every book without a balance sheet must produce one.
**Source:** ICAI BoS, Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 9 *Accounts from Incomplete Records*, §3, pp. 9.6–9.8 (Illustration of Rakesh, pp. 9.8–9.9); §3.2, pp. 9.9–9.10 — fetched (ICAI BoS PDF) https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74620bos60479-fnd-cp9.pdf. Study material, not a pronouncement.
**Example.** Professional (the BoS figures): assets cash ₹3,200, inventory ₹34,800, debtors ₹31,000, machine ₹85,000 = ₹1,54,000; liabilities creditors ₹12,000, bank loan ₹10,000; capital (balancing) ₹1,32,000: `Dr Cash A/c ₹3,200 · Dr Inventory A/c ₹34,800 · Dr Debtors A/c ₹31,000 · Dr Machine A/c ₹85,000 · Cr Creditors A/c ₹12,000 · Cr Bank Loan A/c ₹10,000 · Cr Capital A/c ₹1,32,000`. Plain words: "On 31 March I wrote down everything the shop owns and owes; the difference, ₹1,32,000, is the owner's money in it."

### 1.12 What a sole proprietor's final accounts contain in the BoS, and which extra statements are not required
The sole proprietor's final accounts are an income statement (a Trading Account for gross profit and a Profit and Loss Account for net profit) and a position statement (the Balance Sheet, at a date). Additional statements such as a cash flow statement and a statement of changes in equity are "not mandatory for non-corporate entities". The BoS balance sheet is horizontal, with "Capital and Liabilities" on the left and "Assets" on the right, and says "there is no hard and fast rule regarding presentation".
**Source:** ICAI BoS, Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 7 Unit 1 §1.1, pp. 7.2–7.3; §1.6, pp. 7.39–7.40 — fetched (ICAI BoS PDF) https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74616bos60479-fnd-cp7-u1.pdf. Study material, not a pronouncement.
**Example.** Professional (the BoS figures): sales ₹2,00,000 and closing stock ₹30,000 against opening stock ₹15,000, purchases ₹1,50,000 and direct expenses ₹10,000 give a gross profit of ₹55,000, carried over: `Dr Trading A/c ₹55,000 · Cr Profit and Loss A/c ₹55,000`. Plain words: "The shop sold ₹2,00,000 of goods whose cost, after counting what is left, was ₹1,45,000; the ₹55,000 difference goes to the profit and loss account."

## 2. Where the sources disagree, or are silent
- **Recommended vs prescribed formats.** NCERT Class 11 Ch 8 §8.6.1 says there is "no prescribed form of Balance sheet, for a proprietary and partnership firms". ICAI's Technical Guide recommends formats. The 2023 Guidance Note (not read) may differ.
- **Cash flow for small entities.** Technical Guide Ch V says a Level II–IV entity "may not include" one and is encouraged to; Annexure 2 marks AS 3 "Not Applicable" for those Levels. The two statements of the same guide are not worded alike.
- **NPO statements.** The Technical Guide gives no NPO form and lists NPO bodies as outside it where a regulator prescribes formats; NCERT Class 12's NPO chapter was not found; the NPO model rests on an ICAI-hosted faculty deck.
- **Heading content.** The Technical Guide forms give entity name, as-at date or period, unit, comparatives and note numbers. No source read requires the basis of accounting, a live/closed status or a "prepared on" date at the head; AS 1 puts policies in a note, "in one place".
- **Day book, ledger statement, ageing, statement of affairs, rokad.** Not covered as reports in the sources read. Day books in NCERT are subsidiary journals.
- **The Guidance Note's date and reach, settled (FAQ, 20 Oct 2026).** The Guidance Note on Financial Statements of Non-Corporate Entities applies to accounting periods beginning on or after **1 April 2024** (Q1) and supersedes the 2022 Technical Guide from that date (Q3); it *prescribes* the formats (Q4), allows line items to be added or substituted and nil lines omitted (Q7–Q8), and requires the previous period's figures beside the current ones except in a first set of statements (Q9, citing Framework ¶42). It covers business or professional non-corporate entities — sole proprietors, partnerships, societies, trusts, associations — but not where ICAI has given specific guidance, as for not-for-profit entities (Q2 exception (c), Q6). A household, which carries on no business, is not in its list. From 1 April 2024 non-company entities are *MSME* or *Large* (Q10); an MSME that uses its exemptions says so in a note (Q10, Additional requirements (1)). The Note's own text is still not read; the 2022 Technical Guide citations above stand for what it superseded.
- **BoS Foundation read (20 Oct 2026).** The BoS chapter on not-for-profits (Ch. 8 §1, p. 8.2) names the Receipts and Payments Account, the Income and Expenditure Account and a Balance Sheet as final accounts of such bodies, which agrees with the faculty deck; but the Technical Guide (TG 2009 ed. ¶27, ¶32, in note 17) names the Receipts and Payments statement only for cash-basis bodies and recommends accrual for all, so the question stays open (§3). For a sole proprietor's extra statements the BoS (Ch. 7 Unit 1 §1.1, p. 7.3) says the cash flow statement and statement of changes in equity are "not mandatory for non-corporate entities"; the Technical Guide words the same point two ways (above). The BoS has no ageing schedule (no hit for "ageing" in any Foundation chapter read), no "Account Current", and no single day book.

## 4. Answers to the GAPS items this topic settles
- **A8 (Receipts & Payments, the one-month summary, the chosen-period statement, the ageing, the family summary, and print/export with a chooser):** Receipts & Payments is the NPO cash summary (ICAI BoS deck Concept 2, §1.3). The sole trader's and partnership's expected statements are the final accounts (§1.1–1.2); a one-month summary of a trial balance rests on NCERT's allowance for a trial balance at any chosen date (Class 11 Ch 6 §6.1, topic 19 §1.1). The chosen-period ledger statement, ageing and a family summary are not covered by sources read; see §3. The Technical Guide's heading elements are in §1.5.
- **D9, D10:** open decisions of an app standard whose text I was told not to read; the topic's questions on headings and basis are answered in §1.5, §1.6 and §3.
