# 12 — Entries that move no money

**Questions (from the outline):** Journal proper: write-offs, transfers between heads, settlements, adjustments.
**Settles (tracing only):** GAPS A5

NCERT = NCERT Accountancy Class 11 *Financial Accounting* Part I / Part II, reprint 2026-27, text read from the PDFs (printed page numbers): https://ncert.nic.in/textbook/pdf/keac104.pdf (Part I ch 4), keac106.pdf (ch 6), keac107.pdf (ch 7), keac202.pdf (Part II ch 9). In the textbooks the "journal proper" (also "journal residual") is the book where entries that move no money, and fit none of the special books, are written.

## 1. Findings

### 1.1 What the journal proper is for
A book kept for transactions that do not find a place in the special journals (cash book, purchases, sales and returns books) is the journal proper or journal residual. NCERT lists what goes in it: (1) opening entry; (2) adjustment entries (rent outstanding, prepaid insurance, depreciation, commission received in advance); (3) rectification entries; (4) transfer entries (drawings to capital; expense and revenue accounts to trading and profit and loss at year end, also called closing entries); (5) other entries — cancelling discount on a dishonoured cheque, purchase or sale on credit of items other than goods, goods withdrawn by the owner, goods distributed as samples, endorsement and dishonour of bills, consignment and joint venture, loss of goods by fire, theft or spoilage.
**Source:** NCERT Part I ch 4 §4.6, pp. 139–140 — fetched.
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Furniture A/c ₹8,000 · Cr Rao Furnishings A/c ₹8,000` (a credit purchase of an item other than goods, §4.6 item 5(ii)). Plain words: "Took furniture on credit from Rao Furnishings, ₹8,000 — no money paid today, so it goes in the 'other entries' book."

### 1.2 Adjustment entries bring a head up to date at year end
Adjustments update the ledger on the accrual basis: depreciation, outstanding and prepaid expenses, accrued income and income received in advance, further bad debts. They are journal entries with no cash. NCERT's depreciation adjustment is `Depreciation A/c Dr · To Concerned asset A/c`, which "does not involve any cash outflow" (Part I ch 7 §7.1.2 point 5, p. 229; Part II ch 9 §9.7, p. 327).
**Source:** NCERT Part I ch 4 §4.6 item 2 p. 139; ch 7 §7.1.2 p. 229; Part II ch 9 §9.2–9.8 pp. 320–329 — fetched.
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Depreciation A/c ₹1,500 · Cr Furniture A/c ₹1,500`; `Dr Prepaid Insurance A/c ₹2,000 · Cr Insurance A/c ₹2,000` (insurance paid for the next year, taken out of this year's cost — shape as in NCERT's prepaid examples, Part II ch 9 §9.4). Plain words: "At year end, wrote off ₹1,500 wear on the furniture." / "₹2,000 of the insurance paid belongs to next year, so moved it off this year's cost."

### 1.3 A write-off of a balance owed by a person: Bad Debts Dr · Debtor Cr
A debt that cannot be recovered is a loss; the entry is `Bad debts A/c Dr · To Debtors A/c`. A further bad debt found at year end is written off the same way; it reduces debtors and increases the year's bad debts (NCERT Part II ch 9 §9.8, p. 329; Part I ch 7 §7.11, p. 264: "Bad debts A/c Dr · To Sundry debtors A/c — Bad debts written off").
**Source:** NCERT Part II ch 9 §9.8 p. 329; Part I ch 7 p. 264 — fetched.
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Bad Debts A/c ₹2,500 · Cr Mohan A/c ₹2,500`. Plain words: "Mohan has gone bust and will never pay his ₹2,500 — struck off his page as a loss for the year."

### 1.4 Transfer entries close nominal accounts and move drawings to capital
At the end of the year drawings are transferred to the capital account, and sales, purchases, opening stock, income, gains and expenses are closed by transferring their totals to the trading and profit and loss account, by journal entries ("closing entries"). The textbook states that expense and revenue accounts are not balanced but closed by transfer (Part I ch 4 §4.6 item 4, p. 140; §4.7 pp. 141–142). The drawings entry's accounts are named only in words; the textbook's journal format for it is not shown in the text read, so the entry below is the straight reading of "drawings transferred to capital".
**Source:** NCERT Part I ch 4 §4.6 item 4, §4.7 pp. 140–142 — fetched.
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Capital A/c ₹60,000 · Cr Drawings A/c ₹60,000`. Plain words: "Owner took ₹60,000 out over the year; at the year's end moved it from his drawings page against his capital."

### 1.5 Correction entries between heads are journal entries with no money
Re-classifying an amount from the wrong head to the right one is a rectification entry through the journal proper (§4.6 item 3; NCERT ch 6 §6.6.1, pp. 192–194): `Dr Rent ₹2,000 · Cr Landlord ₹2,000` where rent was posted to the landlord's account. Topic 11 owns the detail.
**Source:** NCERT Part I ch 4 §4.6 item 3 p. 140; ch 6 §6.6.1 p. 194 — fetched.
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Rent A/c ₹2,000 · Cr Landlord A/c ₹2,000`. Plain words: "Rent of ₹2,000 had been written against the landlord's name; moved to the Rent page. No money moved."

### 1.6 Every journal entry carries a narration
Each journal proper entry in the textbooks has a brief narration explaining it: "(Bad debts written off)", "(Credit purchases from Raghu omitted to be recorded, now corrected)", "(For creating provision for doubtful debts)". The textbook prescribes no wording.
**Source:** NCERT Part I ch 7 p. 264; ch 6 Illustration 1 p. 205 — fetched.
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Bad Debts A/c ₹2,500 · Cr Mohan A/c ₹2,500 — (Mohan insolvent; written off)`. Plain words: "Written off Mohan's ₹2,500 because he is insolvent."

### 1.7 The BoS list of what goes to the journal proper
"If there is no special book meant to record a transaction, it is recorded in the journal (proper)." The BoS lists: (i) opening entries; (ii) closing entries (nominal accounts transferred to the profit and loss account); (iii) rectification entries; (iv) transfer entries (an amount moved from one account to another); (v) adjusting entries (outstanding and prepaid expenses, interest on the proprietor's capital, depreciation); (vi) dishonour of bills; (vii) miscellaneous: credit purchase of items other than goods dealt in, an allowance or charge to customers after the invoice, receipt or issue of promissory notes where no bill book is kept, an amount that becomes irrecoverable (for example, a customer's insolvency), effects of accidents such as loss of property by fire, and transfer of net profit to capital.
**Source:** ICAI BoS, Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 2 Unit 4 *Subsidiary Books*, §4.7 *Importance of journal*, pp. 2.96–2.97 — fetched (ICAI BoS PDF) https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74609bos60479-fnd-cp2-u4.pdf. Study material, not a pronouncement.
**Example.** Professional: a customer who has become insolvent owes ₹2,500: `Dr Bad Debts A/c ₹2,500 · Cr Mohan A/c ₹2,500`. Plain words: "Mohan has gone bankrupt; the ₹2,500 he owed is written off. No money moved, so it is written in the journal, not the cash book."

### 1.8 Goods taken by the owner or lost by fire: the accounts the BoS uses
Goods used other than for sale are subtracted from Purchases and the suitable account is debited: for the proprietor's personal use `Drawings A/c Dr · To Purchases A/c` (likewise donations, free samples, goods used to build an asset, goods used for repairs). Goods destroyed by fire or theft: the loss is first taken to a *Loss by Fire* (abnormal loss) account against Purchases/Trading Account; if the insurer admits a claim, the *Insurance Company* account is debited (an asset until the money is received), the part not met goes to the Profit and Loss Account, and the loss account is cleared. The BoS example: goods of ₹6,000 destroyed, claim admitted ₹4,500.
**Source:** ICAI BoS, Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 7 Unit 1 §1.5 *Certain adjustments and their treatment*, items 1 and 3, pp. 7.30–7.32; summary of adjustments, items 12–13, p. 7.58 — fetched (ICAI BoS PDF) https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74616bos60479-fnd-cp7-u1.pdf. Study material, not a pronouncement.
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Drawings A/c ₹4,000 · Cr Purchases A/c ₹4,000` (owner took goods home). Fire: `Dr Loss by Fire A/c ₹6,000 · Cr Purchases A/c ₹6,000`; `Dr Insurance Company A/c ₹4,500 · Dr Profit and Loss A/c ₹1,500 · Cr Loss by Fire A/c ₹6,000`. Plain words: "Owner took ₹4,000 of goods for the house." / "Fire destroyed ₹6,000 of stock; the insurer has accepted ₹4,500, so ₹1,500 is our loss."

### 1.9 A business cost paid from the owner's or a partner's own pocket is capital brought in
When the proprietor "met some of the business expenses from his private resources", the appropriate asset or expense account is debited and the source of the funds credited. Among the fresh investments by proprietors or partners the BoS lists "payments made to creditors out of personal cash"; the profit-by-capital-comparison method then deducts such "fresh capital introduced". Unless such items are identified, "business income will be affected".
**Source:** ICAI BoS, Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 9 *Accounts from Incomplete Records*, §4.1 item (2), p. 9.15; §4.5 *Fresh investment by proprietors/partners*, pp. 9.22–9.23; §3.1, p. 9.6 — fetched (ICAI BoS PDF) https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74620bos60479-fnd-cp9.pdf. Study material, not a pronouncement.
**Example.** Professional: the owner pays ₹3,000 shop rent from his own pocket: `Dr Rent A/c ₹3,000 · Cr Ramesh Capital A/c ₹3,000`. Plain words: "Ramesh paid the shop's ₹3,000 rent out of his own pocket; the shop owes it to him as money he put in."

## 2. Where the sources disagree, or are silent
- The textbooks teach only the bad-debt write-off. A write-off of a balance to another expense head (rent not collected, an advance with no bills) is not described in any source read; by shape it is the same as §1.3 but that is an inference, not a source.
- **Set-off of a deposit against what a person owes, or a settlement of an advance against bills, with part written off**: not in NCERT Class 11 text read, ICAI BoS slides, or AS 10/13/5/9 extracts; the Framework's definitions of asset and liability apply to the two accounts but give no entry.
- **An asset bought against a loan with no money moving**: not described; only the credit-purchase journal shape is (§1.1).
- **A partner or owner paying a business cost from his own pocket**, goods drawn by the owner or lost by theft: the textbook lists them as journal proper items (§1.1) but the text read shows no accounts for them.
- The accounts of the transfer entries (§1.4) are not shown in journal form in the text read.
- **BoS Foundation read (20 Oct 2026).** The BoS answers goods drawn or lost by fire (§1.8) and a cost paid from the owner's own pocket (§1.9). It is still silent on writing off a balance other than a customer's (rent, an advance, a relative's loan), on set-off of a deposit against rent due, on an advance settled against bills for several heads, and on an asset bought with a loan paid straight to the seller (no hit in the chapters read). Its journal-proper list (§1.7) names "an amount [that] becomes irrecoverable, say, because of the customer becoming insolvent" only for customers.

## 4. Answers to the GAPS items this topic settles
- **A5** (entries that move no money): the textbook journal proper holds opening, adjustment, rectification, transfer and other no-cash entries (§1.1). Bad-debt write-off `Dr Bad Debts · Cr Debtor` is sourced (§1.3). Asset against loan, deposit set-off against a tenant, write-off of a non-debt balance to an expense head, advance settlement with part written off, and a partner paying a firm cost are **not settled by the sources** (§3).
