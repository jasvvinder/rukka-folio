# 16 — Accounts between related entities

**Questions (from the outline):** Branch and head-office accounts. Current accounts between related entities and their reconciliation. One entity paying on another's behalf, including payments that involve three parties.
**Settles (tracing only):** GAPS A11

All findings come from one source: ICAI BoS Intermediate Paper 1 *Advanced Accounting*, Ch. 15 *Accounting for Branches including Foreign Branches* (study material, not a pronouncement; new-scheme numbering — older editions number it Ch. 13). Text read in full for §§3, 6, 7. The branch is taught as part of one business, so "related entities" below means two books of one concern. Whether a family and its members, or two separate persons, may keep the same pair is not answered (see §3).

## 1. Findings

### 1.1 Two converse accounts join the two books
An independent branch keeps full double-entry books, with a **Head Office A/c** that records everything between it and head office. Head office keeps one **Branch A/c** per branch for the same transactions. The two accounts are converse: one is Dr where the other is Cr, for the same amount. The two sets of books are connected only through these two accounts. When the books are consolidated, both accounts are eliminated. A debit balance in the Branch A/c equals the net assets at the branch; head office sees the branch as a debtor if assets exceed liabilities, otherwise as a creditor.
**Source:** ICAI BoS Inter P1, Ch. 15, §6 *Accounting for Independent Branches* — fetched (https://resource.cdn.icai.org/74721bos60485-inter-p1-cp15.pdf, pp. 15.41–15.44).
**Example.** Professional: head office remits ₹50,000 to the branch. Head office: `Dr Branch A/c ₹50,000 · Cr Bank A/c ₹50,000`. Branch: `Dr Bank A/c ₹50,000 · Cr Head Office A/c ₹50,000`. Plain words: head office's book: "Sent ₹50,000 to the Ludhiana branch." Branch's book: "Received ₹50,000 from head office."

### 1.2 A dependent branch has no books of its own; the Branch A/c sits in head office's book
Where the head office wholly controls the branch and keeps its accounts, there is a single Branch A/c in the head office book. Goods sent, expenses the head office pays for the branch, and branch assets are debited; cash remitted by the branch and goods returned are credited; the year-end stock, debtors and petty cash are brought in on the credit side so the account shows the branch's profit or loss. (Stock and trading mechanics are out of scope here.)
**Source:** ICAI BoS Inter P1, Ch. 15, §3 *Dependent Branches* and §4 (debtors method) — fetched (same PDF, pp. 15.4–15.7).
**Example.** Professional: head office pays branch rent ₹8,000. `Dr Branch A/c ₹8,000 · Cr Bank A/c ₹8,000`. Plain words: "Paid ₹8,000 rent for the Ludhiana branch."

### 1.3 Payment on another's behalf: each book records its own side; the other book does not
The chapter's table of independent-branch entries (§6) gives, for each transaction, the entry in each book: expenses the branch pays itself (no entry at head office), expenses head office pays for the branch (head office `Dr Branch A/c · Cr Bank`; branch `Dr Expenses · Cr Head Office A/c`), collection from the branch's debtors received by head office (head office `Dr Cash/Bank · Cr Branch A/c`; branch `Dr Head Office A/c · Cr Debtors`), and payment by head office for a branch purchase (head office `Dr Branch A/c · Cr Bank`; branch `Dr Purchases/Creditors · Cr Head Office A/c`). The cost is booked where it belongs by nature (branch expense) and the cash that left is booked as a debit to the other book's account.
**Source:** ICAI BoS Inter P1, Ch. 15, §6, table of entries (i)–(xiv) — fetched (pp. 15.42–15.44).
**Example.** Professional: Kapoor Traders head office pays ₹12,000 electricity bill of its branch. Head office: `Dr Branch A/c ₹12,000 · Cr Bank A/c ₹12,000`. Branch: `Dr Electricity A/c ₹12,000 · Cr Head Office A/c ₹12,000`. Plain words: head office: "Paid the branch's ₹12,000 electricity bill." Branch: "Electricity ₹12,000, paid by head office."

### 1.4 A third party's cost: branch to branch goes through head office
Transactions between two branches are usually adjusted as if each were with head office only. Worked example: the Kolkata branch spends ₹1,000 on advertising for the Delhi branch. Kolkata: `Dr Head Office A/c ₹1,000 · Cr Cash ₹1,000`. Delhi: `Dr Advertisement A/c ₹1,000 · Cr Head Office A/c ₹1,000`. Head office: `Dr Delhi Branch A/c ₹1,000 · Cr Kolkata Branch A/c ₹1,000`. Goods transferred between branches follow the same pattern (the supplying branch credits Head Office, the receiving branch debits Head Office, head office debits one Branch A/c and credits the other). The chapter says this is convenient "especially where the number of branches are large".
**Source:** ICAI BoS Inter P1, Ch. 15, §7.2(1) *Inter-Branch Transactions*, and §6 table entry (xiv) — fetched (p. 15.48 and 15.43).
**Example.** Professional: as above, three books, three entries. Plain words: Kolkata: "Paid ₹1,000 for Delhi's advertising." Delhi: "Advertising ₹1,000, paid for us by Kolkata." Head office: "Delhi now owes the concern ₹1,000, Kolkata is owed ₹1,000."

### 1.5 The balances must agree; differences are timing, and are cleared at the book that missed the item
If the Branch A/c in head office's book and the Head Office A/c in the branch's book do not tally, they must be reconciled before the final accounts. The chapter gives four causes: goods in transit; remittances (cash) in transit; income received or expenses paid directly by one side and not yet told to the other; and items recorded at one end only. Rule: adjust **only at the book where the item was not recorded**; the book that recorded it in full makes no further entry. Worked example: head office sent goods ₹10,000 the branch has not received; the branch records `Dr Goods in Transit A/c ₹10,000 · Cr Head Office A/c ₹10,000` and head office does nothing. Cash ₹15,000 remitted by the branch and not yet received: head office records `Dr Cash in Transit A/c ₹15,000 · Cr Branch A/c ₹15,000`. After all six adjustments in the chapter's example both accounts show ₹90,000.
**Source:** ICAI BoS Inter P1, Ch. 15, §7 *Adjustment and Reconciliation of Branch and Head Office Accounts*, §7.1, and worked example with "Important Points to be noted" — fetched (pp. 15.44–15.48).
**Example.** Professional: Branch A/c in head office's book ₹1,12,000; Head Office A/c in the branch's book ₹78,500; after adjusting the six differences both stand at ₹90,000 (Branch A/c Dr, Head Office A/c Cr). Plain words: "The two books disagreed by ₹33,500; after listing what each had not yet heard about, both say ₹90,000."

### 1.6 Direct payment by one side for the other, found at reconciliation
In the same worked example: ₹14,500 paid by the branch to head office's supplier (head office had not recorded it) is put right in head office's book as `Dr Sundry Creditors A/c ₹14,500 · Cr Branch A/c ₹14,500`; ₹10,500 collected by head office from a branch customer is put right in the branch's book as `Dr Head Office A/c ₹10,500 · Cr Debtors A/c ₹10,500`; head office's cost allocated to the branch, ₹12,000, as `Dr Branch Expenses A/c ₹12,000 · Cr H.O. A/c ₹12,000` in the branch's book.
**Source:** ICAI BoS Inter P1, Ch. 15, §7.1 worked example entries (iii)–(vi), and Illustration 9 (page not noted) — fetched (pp. 15.46–15.48 for the worked example).
**Example.** Professional: `Dr Sundry Creditors A/c ₹14,500 · Cr Branch A/c ₹14,500` (head office's book). Plain words: "The branch paid our supplier ₹14,500 for us; we now owe the branch ₹14,500."

### 1.7 Carried forward, not settled
In the chapter's worked example the reconciled balance (₹90,000) is carried forward ("To Balance b/d 90,000"). The chapter has no year-end "settlement" of the two accounts; the balance is a continuing position.
**Source:** ICAI BoS Inter P1, Ch. 15, §7.1 worked example (the two ledger accounts, closing line) — fetched (p. 15.48).
**Example.** Professional: `Branch A/c — To Balance b/d ₹90,000` on 1 April. Plain words: "Opening: the Ludhiana branch has ₹90,000 of the concern's money."

## 2. Where the sources disagree, or are silent
- The chapter teaches the pair for **branches of one concern only**. It is silent on separate persons (a family and a member, two relatives, two firms) and on whether such a pair carries any "branch-like" meaning of net assets.
- Silent on **who bears a cost** paid for another when the entities are not parts of one concern — the branch chapter always treats the cost as the branch's own.
- Silent on a bank-statement-style *statement of the account* sent from one side to the other (the chapter reconciles two ledger accounts; it does not show a statement format).
- NCERT Class 11 Ch. 5 (bank reconciliation) concerns a bank and a customer, not two related entities; it was not used.
- A web search returned only unrelated third-party "branch accounting" notes; none used.
- **ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1 read (20 Oct 2026).** Chapters 1–10 were searched for a head-office/branch pair, a current account between related entities and a statement of account. The only accounts the Foundation chapters keep between two parties of one concern are a partner's capital and current accounts with the firm (Ch. 10 Unit 1 §1.9, pp. 10.19–10.20); there are no converse accounts and no reconciliation of two books. The Foundation material is silent on every row below.

## 4. Answers to the GAPS items this topic settles
- **A11:** partly. Pair accounts named after the other book, equal and opposite, reconciled, and adjusted only at the book that missed the item (Ch. 15 §6, §7). A household's account with a family, a family's account with a family business, three-book entries and year-end settling are **not settled**, see §3 and note 15 §3.
