# Contractor / job-work — Bal Aluminium & PVC Works

**About this book:** One-owner contracting business with a site supervisor. Pradeep Bal fabricates and fits aluminium and uPVC windows and partitions. One builder pays a mobilisation advance, is billed in running bills, holds 5% retention that stays Dr and ages until it is released; material is bought on credit, one supplier is paid in advance (so the supplier owes the business until the invoice arrives), and the site supervisor runs labour floats as advances — one float ends with ₹1,200 of lost slips written off.

**Period:** 01 Apr 2025 – 31 Mar 2026 (FY 2025-26) · every month closed · year closed. Sample report — the figures are illustrative.

| Person | Role in this example |
|:---|:---|
| Pradeep Bal | owner · admin, no entry limit · approves the supervisor's advances and over-limit entries |
| Jagtar Singh | site supervisor · member, entry limit ₹5,000 · holds the labour floats as advances in his own advance account |
| Sandhu Builders | customer: advance → running bills → retention (party) |
| Mann Residency | customer: one small job (party) |
| Gill Residency | customer: one job in January (party) |
| Alu-Tech Profiles | supplier on credit (party) |
| Glass Mart | supplier paid in advance, then invoiced (party) |

How to read it: amounts are in ₹, with paise shown only when they are not zero. Every statement is written the way a ledger book is, with the plain meaning first and the true side in brackets — Money in (Dr), Money out (Cr), You will get (Dr), You will give (Cr). An account statement runs Date · Entry · Particulars (*To …* on the Dr side, *By …* on the Cr side) · Dr · Cr · Balance · Side; it opens with the balance brought forward and ends with the closing balance carried down, the totals and the opening balance for the new year. Income and expense heads have no opening balance and end with their total for the year.
