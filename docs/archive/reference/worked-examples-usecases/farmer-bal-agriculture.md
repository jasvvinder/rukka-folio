# Farmer — Bal Agriculture (Amar Bal)

**About this book:** One-owner farm book — KCC loan account, fertiliser khata, MSP wheat, basmati through an arhtiya, milk on a ten-day cycle. Amar Bal farms wheat, basmati and a few buffaloes. The Kisan Credit Card is a **loan account** (it opens with money owed to the bank): drawdowns and repayments are Transfers, the bank's interest debits are Money out from the loan account. Fertiliser goes on the dealer's khata, wheat is procured at MSP and paid straight into the bank, basmati is sold through an arhtiya who deducts his charges, milk goes to the dairy society on a ten-day cycle and is paid monthly, PM-Kisan instalments arrive by DBT, and one month-end cash count comes up short.

**Period:** 01 Apr 2025 – 31 Mar 2026 (FY 2025-26) · every month closed · year closed. Sample report — the figures are illustrative.

| Person | Role in this example |
|:---|:---|
| Amar Bal | farmer · the only member (admin) — nothing needs a second person's approval |
| Bhatti Khad Store | fertiliser dealer — khata (party) |
| Garg & Co. | arhtiya (commission agent) — gross sale on his khata, charges deducted, net paid (party) |
| Verka Milk Society | dairy society — a bill every ten days, paid on the 7th of the next month (party) |

How to read it: amounts are in ₹, with paise shown only when they are not zero. Every statement is written the way a ledger book is, with the plain meaning first and the true side in brackets — Money in (Dr), Money out (Cr), You will get (Dr), You will give (Cr). An account statement runs Date · Entry · Particulars (*To …* on the Dr side, *By …* on the Cr side) · Dr · Cr · Balance · Side; it opens with the balance brought forward and ends with the closing balance carried down, the totals and the opening balance for the new year. Income and expense heads have no opening balance and end with their total for the year.
