# 04 — Year end and reports

**Status:** Draft, 20 Oct 2026 — for a bookkeeper's review.

This document says how a book is checked at the end of each month, how it is closed at the end of the year, and what every report must contain. It covers the people who keep a rokad (day book), a khata and a ledger by hand: households, joint families, their small businesses (shop, contractor, farm, truck, landlord, partnership) and trusts or committees. It describes bookkeeping practice only; it does not claim that a book kept this way meets the Accounting Standards or any official format. How an entry is posted is in *02 — Accounts and transactions*; what is special about each kind of book is in *03 — Entities*.

Plain words are used first and the true side follows in brackets where it helps, for example *Money in (Dr)*. Every example gives one line in professional terms and one line in diary words. Rupees use Indian grouping.

## 1. Month end

### 1.1 A month end is a check, not a close

**Rule.** At the end of each month the book is checked, not closed. Three things are done: the cash in hand is counted and compared with the book; every bank account is reconciled with its bank statement (rule 1.2); and the month is locked, so that a mistake found later is put right by a new correcting entry dated today, never by changing the locked month. Nothing is moved to *Own money* at a month end. Statements for any period remain available. Only the year end (section 2) closes the ledger.

A trial balance, or any statement, may be taken at any date; the month-end check is the usual moment to take one (rule 3.2).

**Source:** ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 2 Unit 3 §3.1, p. 2.67 (a trial balance may be prepared at month end, quarter end or year end); NCERT Class 11 Accountancy Part I, Ch 2 §2.2.4 (interim statements for spans other than a year). Locking a month and counting cash: none settles it — ruled 20 Oct 2026 (O-19-5): the sources allow interim statements and say nothing on closing the ledger before the year end, so the month end is a recognised check and nothing more.

**Example.** Professional: cash counted on 30 April is ₹4,850 against a book balance of ₹5,000; the shortage is entered as a new entry dated 30 April or the next day: `Dr Cash short / over ₹150 · Cr Cash ₹150`. Plain words: "Counted the cash box: ₹4,850, book says ₹5,000. Wrote the ₹150 difference under *Cash short / over*. April is now locked."

### 1.2 Every bank account is reconciled at each month end, by correcting the book first

**Rule.** At each month end, and always at the year end, every bank account is compared with its bank statement. The two balances usually differ for two reasons only: *timing* (a cheque written but not yet presented; a cheque banked but not yet cleared) and *items the book has not yet recorded* (bank charges, interest, a direct credit from a customer, a payment the bank made on standing instruction, a dishonoured cheque). The method is:

1. Enter in the book every item that is on the statement but missing from the book. This is the "adjust the book first" method.
2. The book balance is now the *adjusted balance*.
3. List the timing differences that remain: cheques written but not yet presented are added to the adjusted balance, cheques banked but not yet cleared are deducted from it.
4. The result must equal the statement balance. If not, there is an error, to be found and corrected before the month is locked.

The reconciliation is dated and names the bank account. Where the same amount appears twice or is missing, the error may be the book's or the bank's; only the book's errors are corrected in the book.

**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Part I, Ch 5 §5.1, §5.1.1(a)–(g), §5.2, §5.2.1 (causes of difference, direction of each adjustment). ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 3 *Bank Reconciliation Statement*, §2 and §3, p. 3.4 (done at regular intervals, "monthly or quarterly", according to size); §7.2.1, p. 3.25 (adjusting the cash book is optional in the year, but required at the year end so that the balance sheet shows the right bank balance; timing differences stay in the statement; standing-instruction payments are entered when the statement shows them). Every month rather than quarterly: none settles it — ruled 20 Oct 2026 (O-05-4): a statement taken in from the bank makes monthly cheap, and it belongs to the month-end check.

**Example.** Professional (own figures, on the BoS pattern): book balance ₹40,000; the statement shows bank charges ₹300 and an insurance premium paid on standing instruction ₹1,500 not yet in the book; a cheque for ₹2,000 has not been presented. Correct the book: `Dr Bank charges ₹300 · Dr Insurance ₹1,500 · Cr Bank ₹1,800`; adjusted balance ₹38,200. Then ₹38,200 + cheque not presented ₹2,000 = statement ₹40,200. Plain words: "The bank took ₹300 and paid ₹1,500 insurance for me. I wrote both in my book first. The only difference left is a ₹2,000 cheque the shop has not banked yet."

Layout of the reconciliation:

```
Bank reconciliation — {bank account} — as on {date}
Balance in my book                              40,000
  Less: items on the statement I had not written  1,800   (charges 300, insurance 1,500)
Balance in my book, corrected                   38,200
  Add: cheques I wrote, not yet presented         2,000
Balance as the bank statement shows             40,200
```

## 2. Year end

### 2.1 The book posts the closing entries itself

**Rule.** At the end of the year the book makes the closing entries; the person never types them. They are printed in the year-end section of the reports (rule 5.9) so that they can be read.

1. Every income head and every expense head is closed to the year's result, and so stands at nothing on the first day of the next year.
2. The result is then closed to *Own money*: a profit adds to it, a loss reduces it.
3. *Drawings*, where the book keeps them, are closed to *Own money* and reduce it.
4. For a **partnership**, the result goes first to the *P&L Appropriation Account* and from there to the partners' accounts (rule 6.4).
5. For a **trust or committee**, the surplus or deficit goes to the *General Fund* (rule 6.7).
6. For a **joint family**, the family's own result goes to the family's own money.

The closing entries are real entries in the ledger, so every account's page shows the transfer and ends the year at nothing.

**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Part II, Ch 8 §8.4, §8.4.2, §8.4.3, §8.6.2 item (8) (expenses and incomes transferred to the Trading and Profit and Loss Account by closing entries; profit transferred to capital; drawings closed to capital); NCERT Class 11 Part I, Ch 4 §4.6 item 4 (transfer entries). ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 7 Unit 1 §1.3–§1.4, pp. 7.9–7.23, and p. 7.19 (drawings closed to capital). The BoS Ch. 9 §3, pp. 9.6–9.7, computes the result by comparing capital at two dates, without closing entries, but only for records that are incomplete. Posting rather than computing: ruled 20 Oct 2026 (O-19-1, O-19-6) on the textbook method, because every book here keeps full double entry.

**Example.** Professional (NCERT Ch 8, Ankit): income ₹1,30,000, expenses ₹1,25,500: `Dr Profit and Loss ₹4,500 · Cr Own money (Capital) ₹4,500`. Drawings (own figures), Ankit took ₹36,000 for himself: `Dr Own money ₹36,000 · Cr Drawings ₹36,000`. Plain words: "The year left ₹4,500 over, added to Ankit's own money in the business. The ₹36,000 Ankit took out for his own use is taken off his own money."

### 2.2 Year-end adjustments are optional, off by default, and the report says which way the book was kept

**Rule.** The book is kept on money paid and received, and on what people owe or are owed when it arises (the khata basis): nothing is recorded for the passage of time alone. At the year end a business book (shop, contractor, farm, truck, a landlord's business book, partnership) or a trust or committee may opt in to adjustments; if it does not, none is made. A household or joint-family book stays on the khata basis, including a household that lets property (*03*, 6). The adjustments are:

| Adjustment | What it does | Entry (professional) | Plain words |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Outstanding** (expense of the year not yet paid) | adds to the year's expense; a thing you will give | `Dr Wages ₹500 · Cr Wages outstanding ₹500` | "Still owe the worker ₹500 for this year; count it as this year's wages." |
| **Prepaid** (paid, but for next year) | takes off the year's expense; a thing you will get in service | `Dr Prepaid salary ₹5,000 · Cr Salary ₹5,000` | "Paid ₹5,000 in advance; not this year's cost." |
| **Accrued** (income earned, not yet received) | adds to the year's income | `Dr Accrued commission ₹1,500 · Cr Commission ₹1,500` | "Earned ₹1,500, not yet paid to me; count it as this year's." |
| **Received in advance** (income received for next year) | takes off the year's income; a thing you will give | `Dr Subscriptions received ₹3,000 · Cr Subscriptions received in advance ₹3,000` | "A club that keeps no member accounts was paid three months ahead; not this year's income." (A tenant's advance already sits on his own account.) |
| **Depreciation** (wear on a thing owned) | a rate per thing owned; shows as an expense | see rule 2.3 | |
| **Closing stock** | a closing-stock *rupee figure* typed by the person | see rule 2.4 | |

If the book opts in, the head of every report says so (rule 4.1), and the adjustments appear in the year-end section. The outstanding, prepaid, accrued, in-advance and stock adjustments are reversed on the first day of the next year (rule 2.5). Depreciation is not reversed.

**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Part II, Ch 9 §9.1 (why profit is not cash received less cash paid), §9.3 (outstanding expense), §9.4 (prepaid), §9.5 (accrued income), §9.6 (income received in advance). AS 1 ¶27 (a book not kept on the accrual basis discloses the fact). ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 1 Unit 2, p. 1.38 (cash-basis profit is cash received less cash paid, with no adjustments). Off by default and optional: none settles it — ruled 20 Oct 2026 (O-01-1, O-19-4; O-17-3 for the trust), and the household and joint family kept out by owner ruling, 21 Oct 2026: the people who keep these books record money as it moves and a live position needs no year-end estimates; the report says which way the book was kept.

**Example.** Professional (NCERT §9.1): insurance ₹1,200 paid on 1 July for twelve months; the year ends 31 March. With adjustments on: `Dr Prepaid insurance ₹300 · Cr Insurance ₹300`; the year's insurance is ₹900. With them off, the year's insurance is ₹1,200. Plain words: "Paid ₹1,200 insurance in July. Nine months of it is this year's cost. If I keep it simple, I count all ₹1,200 this year and the head of the report says so."

### 2.3 Depreciation is charged only on opt-in, at a rate per thing owned

**Rule.** By default things owned stay at what they cost and no depreciation is charged. A business book (shop, contractor, farm, truck, a landlord's business book, partnership) or a trust or committee may opt in at the year end. Then each thing owned carries its own yearly rate, and the charge is an expense of the year:

`Dr Depreciation ₹x · Cr {thing owned} ₹x`

When the book has not opted in, every report that shows things owned prints the standing note in rule 4.3. When it has, the report shows each thing at cost, the depreciation so far and what is left.

**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Part I, Ch 7 §7.4.3, p. 232 and AS 10 ¶50, ¶52 (depreciation is a charge of the year); ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 5 §1.3, p. 5.5 (not charging it gives neither the true profit nor the true value of the asset); AS 1 ¶27 (a departure is disclosed). Not charging it by default: none settles it — ruled 20 Oct 2026 (O-09-1): households, farms and trucks in these books do not keep it; the book makes no compliance claim and the note states the fact. Where a trust's fund buys a thing owned and depreciation is opted in, the fund is released to the year's income in step with the depreciation (O-17-3).

**Example.** Professional: a delivery cart bought for ₹60,000, rate 10% a year: `Dr Depreciation ₹6,000 · Cr Cart ₹6,000`; shown as cost ₹60,000, depreciation ₹6,000, left ₹54,000. Plain words: "The cart cost ₹60,000. I set aside ₹6,000 for a year's wear; it is now shown as worth ₹54,000."

### 2.4 Closing stock is a figure the person types, and nothing more

**Rule.** The book does not count or value goods. A shop that wants a stock figure types one rupee amount at the year end; it is an opt-in adjustment (rule 2.2) and reduces the year's cost of what was bought for sale. A book that types none prints the standing note in rule 4.3. A closing figure is measured against the goods on the shelf at the opening, which the opening entry put into *Goods bought for sale* (01, opening balances of things owned); a book that typed no opening goods may type a closing figure only if the shelf was in fact empty at the opening. Stock valuation as such is outside this document.

**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Part II, Ch 8 §8.4.4, p. 294 (the presumption that everything bought has been sold); ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 4 §4.3, p. 4.10 (goods not in inventory are taken as sold). The wording of the note: none settles it — ruled 20 Oct 2026 (O-10-1).

**Example.** Professional: goods bought for sale in the year ₹1,50,000; closing stock typed ₹30,000; `Dr Closing stock ₹30,000 · Cr Goods bought for sale ₹30,000` (reversed on 1 April). Plain words: "Bought ₹1,50,000 of goods. ₹30,000 of it is still on the shelf, so only ₹1,20,000 is this year's cost."

### 2.5 An adjustment is reversed on the first day of the next year

**Rule.** If the book opted in to the timing adjustments of rule 2.2 (outstanding, prepaid, accrued, in advance) or typed a closing-stock figure, the book cancels each one with an opposite entry dated the first day of the next year. This stops a later payment or receipt from counting twice. It applies only to a book that opted in; depreciation is not reversed.

**Source:** ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 7 Unit 1 §1.4, p. 7.26 ("Next year in the beginning entries No. (1) to (5) should be reversed"); the ICAI BoS virtual-coaching deck "9. Financial Statements of NPO", p. 118, shows the same (a faculty slide deck, not the study material). NCERT Class 11 Part II, Ch 9 §9.2, p. 321 gives the same entry for stock (*Purchases A/c Dr. To Opening stock A/c* in the next year). Ruled 20 Oct 2026 (O-19-3), accepted on the BoS text.

**Example.** Professional (BoS pattern, own figures): 31 March `Dr Rent ₹14,000 · Cr Outstanding rent ₹14,000`; 1 April `Dr Outstanding rent ₹14,000 · Cr Rent ₹14,000`; when the rent is paid in May, `Dr Rent ₹14,000 · Cr Cash ₹14,000` brings the new year's Rent back to nil, so the ₹14,000 counts only in the old year. Plain words: "At the year end I wrote the ₹14,000 rent I still owed. On 1 April I cancelled that note so that paying it in May leaves the new year with nothing for it."

### 2.6 What carries forward and what starts again at nothing

**Rule.** The year's closing balances become the next year's opening balances; the person types nothing.

*Carries forward:*
- balances of persons: *You will get* and *You will give*;
- money: *Cash in hand* and each bank account (reconciled to the statement);
- things owned, at cost (less depreciation if opted in);
- deposits, advances and loans, in their own accounts on the person's page;
- funds (for a trust or committee), each at its own balance;
- *Own money*, as one figure after the year's result and drawings (a partnership: each partner's capital account and current account, each at its own balance; or each partner's one account, where capital is kept fluctuating — rule 6.5; a joint family and each sub-family: the running account between the two books, which sits within own money in both, at its own balance, nil where the family settled it — rule 6.6; *03*, 3.2, 3.5).

*Starts again at nothing:*
- every income head and every expense head;
- *Drawings*.

The carry-forward report (rule 5.9) lists these, opening against closing, and the opening balances of the new year equal the closing balances of the old.

**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Part II, Ch 8 §8.6, §8.7 (the balance sheet becomes the opening trial balance of the next period; revenue and expense accounts start the next year at nil); NCERT Class 11 Part I, Ch 4 §4.1.4 (balance carried down, brought down on the first day). ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 7 Unit 1 §1.4 and §1.8, p. 7.43 (balances of real and personal accounts carried forward).

**Example.** Professional (NCERT Ch 8 §8.7): the new book opens `Dr Furniture ₹15,000 · Dr Debtors ₹15,500 · Dr Bank ₹5,000 · Dr Cash ₹1,000 · Cr Own money ₹16,500 · Cr Loan ₹5,000 · Cr Creditors ₹15,000`; sales, wages and rent start at nil. Plain words: "On 1 April the new book opens with what I had and owed at the end of the old one: ₹16,500 own money. Sales, wages and rent start from zero."

## 3. Trial balance

### 3.1 The trial balance lists closing balances only

**Rule.** A trial balance is a dated list of the balance of every account, in two columns: one for the accounts whose balance is on the *Money in (Dr)* side, one for the *Money out (Cr)* side. The two columns must be equal. It carries the head of rule 4.1. It shows closing balances only: no opening, no period movements and no totals of each side. An account's own opening, movements and closing are in its statement (rule 5.6).

```
Trial balance — {book} — as on 31 March 2027
Account                        Dr (Money in side)    Cr (Money out side)
Cash in hand                          1,000
Capital (Own money)                                      12,000
Sales                                                  1,25,000
Goods bought for sale               75,000
...
Total                              1,62,000              1,62,000
```

A person's account whose balance is nil may be left out. A zero line may be shown in its usual column.

**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Part I, Ch 6 §6.1 and Fig. 6.1 (a statement of balances at a date; columns of debit and credit balance); §6.3.1–§6.3.3 (the balances method is "the most widely used method in practice"; the totals method "not widely used in practice"; the totals-cum-balances method "not used in practice"). ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 2 Unit 3 §3.2, p. 2.68 (one form, "Dr Amount (Total or Balance)", dated at its head). No source read shows opening, period and closing columns. Closing balances only: ruled 20 Oct 2026 (O-19-2).

**Example.** Professional (NCERT Ch 8 §8.3, Ankit): Cash `Dr ₹1,000`, Capital `Cr ₹12,000`, Sales `Cr ₹1,25,000`, Goods bought for sale `Dr ₹75,000`, and so on; both columns total ₹1,62,000. Plain words: "Listed every account's balance on 31 March. Both sides came to ₹1,62,000, so the posting adds up."

### 3.2 When a trial balance is taken, and what agreement does and does not prove

**Rule.** The book can produce a trial balance as on any date. It is usually taken at each month end (with the check of section 1) and always at the year end, before the year's result is worked out and closed. Equal columns show that the posting adds up; they do not show that every entry is right. A transaction left out altogether, entered at a wrong amount on both sides, put to the wrong account, or posted twice leaves the columns equal. For that reason the month-end counts and bank reconciliations stay part of the check.

**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Part I, Ch 6 §6.1 (any chosen period: monthly, quarterly, half-yearly or annual). ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 2 Unit 3 §3.1–§3.3, pp. 2.67–2.68 (generally at monthly intervals; preferable at the end of the period; agreement is not conclusive proof of accuracy).

**Example.** Professional: opening entry `Dr Cash ₹5,000 · Cr Own money ₹5,000`; trial balance on 30 April: Cash ₹5,000 Dr, Own money ₹5,000 Cr, totals ₹5,000 and ₹5,000. Plain words: "On 30 April I listed every balance. The two sides match, so I know I did not forget one half of an entry; I still count the cash."

## 4. What every report states

### 4.1 The report head

**Rule.** Every report, of every book, begins with the same head of five lines:

1. **The book's name.**
2. **The date or the period** ("as on 31 March 2027", or "1 April 2026 to 30 September 2026").
3. **The basis, in one plain line.** For a book on the default basis: *Kept on money paid and received, and what people owe; no year-end adjustments.* A book that opted in says so in the same line: *Kept on money paid and received, and what people owe; year-end adjustments made: prepaid, outstanding, depreciation.* naming those it made.
4. **Whether the books are open:** *Year open – figures may change* or *Year closed*.
5. ***Made on {date and time}.***

The head is repeated on every page of a printed report, and an exported file carries the same five lines.

**Source:** AS 1 ¶18–¶20, ¶24–¶25, ¶27 (all significant policies disclosed, in one place, as part of the statements; a departure from accrual disclosed). The ICAI Technical Guide on Financial Statements of Non-Corporate Entities (June 2022), Ch VI Part I and II forms and General Instructions items 3–5, gives name, date or period, unit and the previous period's figures at the head; it asks for no basis line, no open-or-closed status and no date made. The basis line: ruled 20 Oct 2026 (O-20-1) on AS 1. Status and date made: none settles it — ruled 20 Oct 2026 (O-20-2): no source requires either, but a book that changes while in use needs them.

**Example.** Professional: no entry. Head: `Sharma Textile — Profit & Loss — 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027 — Kept on money paid and received, and what people owe; no year-end adjustments. — Year closed — Made on 3 April 2027, 10:42.` Plain words: "Every page says whose book, which dates, how it was kept, whether the year is still open, and when I printed it."

### 4.2 Previous year beside this year

**Rule.** A year-end report (position, the year's income and expenses, and for trusts the three statements) shows last year's figure beside this year's, except in a book's first year.

**Source:** ICAI Technical Guide on Financial Statements of Non-Corporate Entities (June 2022; superseded from 1 April 2024, FAQ Q3 — cited for what its forms show), Ch VI General Instructions item 5 (comparatives for the preceding period, except in the first statements). The ICAI FAQ on the Guidance Note on Financial Statements of Non-Corporate Entities, Q9, says the same (citing Framework ¶42). Applied to every book here as a plain practice, not as a claim of following a format.

**Example.** Professional: Own money `₹1,25,000 (2027) · ₹1,10,000 (2026)`. Plain words: "This year's figure and last year's side by side, so you can see what changed."

### 4.3 Standing notes a report prints when they apply

**Rule.** A report that shows the matter below prints its note at the foot, in these words.

| When | The note |
|---|---|
| The report shows things owned and the book has not opted in to depreciation | *Things owned are shown at what they cost; no depreciation is charged.* |
| The book sells goods and typed no closing stock, and the report gives a result or works it into own money — a position and its make-up, a month's summary, the year's statement, the balance sheet (*02*, 6.2) | *No stock is counted. Everything bought for sale is treated as sold or used in the year it was bought.* |
| Own money is negative (what is owed is more than what is had) | *Owes more than it owns.* |
| The position shows money put into a business (*03*, 1.9) | *The business is shown at the money put into it, not at its worth.* (With more than one: *The businesses are shown at the money put into them, not at their worth.*) |
| A trust or committee charged the construction of a building to the year's expenditure (*02*, 5.8) | *The cost of {building}, ₹{x} to date, is charged to expenditure and is not shown as a thing owned.* |
| The year is open | the head already says *Year open – figures may change*; no further note |
| The book opted in to year-end adjustments this year for the first time, or stopped making them | *This year the book {made / stopped making} year-end adjustments; last year's figures beside them are {without / with} them. They change this year's result by ₹{x}.* |

**Source:** things owned: NCERT Class 11 Part I, Ch 7 §7.4.3; AS 10 ¶50, ¶52; AS 1 ¶27; ruled 20 Oct 2026 (O-09-1) — the wording is the book's own, no source gives it. Stock: NCERT Class 11 Part II, Ch 8 §8.4.4, p. 294 and ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 4 §4.3, p. 4.10; wording ruled 20 Oct 2026 (O-10-1). Construction charged to expenditure: owner ruling, 13 Oct 2026 (O-09-7), disclosed as the no-depreciation departure is (AS 1 ¶27); the wording is the book's own. Negative own money: none settles it — ruled 20 Oct 2026 (O-04-4): a negative figure on its usual side states the fact more plainly than the older form that moves it to the other side. Change of basis: AS 1 ¶22, ¶26 (a change of policy is disclosed with the amount by which it affects the statements); NCERT Class 11 Part I ch. 2 §2.2.10; wording: owner ruling, 21 Oct 2026.

**Example.** Professional: no entry. Plain words: at the foot of a household's position — "*Things owned are shown at what they cost; no depreciation is charged.*"

## 5. The reports every book produces

Every report below carries the head of rule 4.1. Amounts are in rupees.

### 5.1 Accounts list with opening balances

**Rule.** A list of every account in the book, grouped by kind (money, persons, things owned, deposits and loans, own money, income, expense), showing each account's opening balance and its side. For a new book the opening balances are those at the day it was opened; for later years, those carried forward (rule 2.6).

| Account | Kind | Opening | Side |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash in hand | Money | 12,500 | Dr |
| Mohan | Person | 2,000 | Dr (You will get) |
| Loan from Sunil | Loan | 50,000 | Cr (You will give) |
| Shop fittings | Thing owned | 60,000 | Dr |
| Own money | Own money | 24,500 | Cr |

**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Part II, Ch 8 §8.7 and ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 7 Unit 1 §1.8, pp. 7.43–7.44 (the opening entry lists each asset and liability and the balancing capital). The report itself: none settles it — ruled 20 Oct 2026 (O-20-3), the list of reports every book gives.

**Example.** Professional: `Dr Cash ₹12,500 · Dr Mohan ₹2,000 · Dr Shop fittings ₹60,000 · Cr Loan from Sunil ₹50,000 · Cr Own money ₹24,500` (the balancing figure: 74,500 − 50,000). Plain words: "The list of every page in my book and what it held on the first day."

### 5.2 Ledger

**Rule.** The ledger of every account: one page per account, showing its opening balance, every entry in date order (date, entry number, the other account, a note), the two amount columns *Money in (Dr)* and *Money out (Cr)*, the running balance with its side, the totals of the two columns and the closing balance. As in the textbook ledger, the opening balance is entered in the amount column of its side (*Balance b/f*) and the closing balance on the other side (*Balance c/d*), so both columns add up to the same *Total*; the printed ledger ends *c/d · Total · b/d* (ADR 2026-09-24b §9; `docs/02-ledger-rules.md` §8.1). It may be taken for one account or for all, for any period.

**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Part I, Ch 4 §4.1.4 and Ch 6 §6.1 (each account balanced, Balance c/d and Balance b/d); ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 2 Unit 2 §2.2, p. 2.53 (ledger account format). Ruled 20 Oct 2026 (O-20-3).

**Example.** Professional: Mohan, opening `Dr ₹2,000`; sale `Dr ₹5,000`; receipt `Cr ₹3,000`; closing `Dr ₹4,000`. Plain words: "Mohan's page: he owed ₹2,000, bought ₹5,000 more, paid ₹3,000, now owes ₹4,000."

### 5.3 Day Book (the rokad)

**Rule.** A single list of every entry in the book, in date order and, within a date, in the order made. Each line: date, entry number, what it was, the accounts it touched and the amount. It runs for any period. It is the book's rokad: on any day it shows everything that was written.

The textbooks use "day books" for the separate sales, purchases and returns books; this document means the single rokad.

```
Day Book — {book} — 1 April 2026 to 30 April 2026
Date    No.  Entry                         Money in (Dr)    Money out (Cr)
2 Apr   1    Sold to Mohan on credit       Mohan 5,000      Sales 5,000
5 Apr   2    Mohan paid in cash            Cash 3,000       Mohan 3,000
```

**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Part I, Ch 3 §3.4 (books of original entry; day books are subsidiary books); ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 2 Unit 1, p. 2.2, and Ch. 9 §4.1, p. 9.14 (the same use of "day books"). Neither source has a single book of everything. A single, date-ordered Day Book: none settles it — ruled 20 Oct 2026 (O-20-7): it is the rokad the people who keep these books already write.

**Example.** Professional: `Dr Mohan ₹5,000 · Cr Sales ₹5,000`, then `Dr Cash ₹3,000 · Cr Mohan ₹3,000`. Plain words: "Sold ₹5,000 to Mohan on 2 April; on 5 April he paid ₹3,000. Both on the day's page, in order."

**Layouts when printed or exported.** There is one record of entries and no separate journal book. When the Day Book is printed or exported, the user chooses how its entries are laid out, and the entries are the same in every layout:
- **the list** above;
- **the journal layout**, for an accountant. Each entry is a Dr line, then a *To* line indented beneath it for the credit, then the note in brackets as the narration, with two amount columns. An entry with more than one account on a side has one line per account. No L.F. (ledger folio) column is printed, because the account's name identifies its ledger page. As in the textbook journal, the layout also carries the entries that are not typed: **it opens with the opening entry** when the period starts on the book's first day, and **ends with the year-close entries** (rule 2.1) when the period includes the last day of a closed year, each dated as made;
- **the Cash Book** (rule 5.4), which takes only the entries that move cash or bank.

In any layout, a filter *Journal entries only* gives the entries a textbook journal proper holds (*01 — Accounting foundations*, 3.6): corrections, write-offs, transfers between heads, goods taken for home.

```
Day Book (journal layout) — {book} — April 2026
Date    Particulars                              Dr ₹      Cr ₹
2 Apr   Mohan A/c ......................... Dr   5,000
          To Sales A/c                                    5,000
        (Sold to Mohan on credit — entry 1)
5 Apr   Cash A/c .......................... Dr   3,000
          To Mohan A/c                                    3,000
        (Mohan paid in cash — entry 2)
```

**Source (layouts):** NCERT Class 11 Part I, Ch 3 §3.4 (journal format: date, particulars with the Dr account and *To* the Cr account, L.F., debit and credit amounts, narration). The journal proper holds opening, closing, adjusting, rectification and transfer entries: NCERT ch. 4 introduction and §4.6 (pp. 139–140); ICAI BoS Module 1 ch. 2 unit 4 §4.7 (pp. 2.96–2.97). Both citations are taken from *01 — Accounting foundations*, 3.2 and 3.6; the textbooks were not re-read for this rule. One record with several layouts and no separate journal book: owner, 20 Oct 2026.

### 5.4 Cash Book (cash and bank)

**Rule.** The Cash Book lists every receipt and every payment of cash and of each bank account, in date order, receipts (*Money in*) on the left and payments (*Money out*) on the right, starting from the opening cash and bank balances and ending with the closing ones. Cash is always on the *Money in (Dr)* side or nil; a bank account that has been overdrawn is shown on the other side. A bank account has its own column. A transfer between cash and bank appears on both sides. Cash taken from the bank for the owner's own use is *Drawings*, not a transfer.

No cash-flow statement is made for any book (rule 5.10).

```
Cash Book — {book} — April 2026
Date   Particulars          Cash     Bank  |  Date   Particulars       Cash    Bank
1 Apr  Opening balance     12,500   40,000 |  3 Apr  Rent paid                  6,000
5 Apr  Mohan                3,000          |  9 Apr  Cash to bank     3,000
9 Apr  Cash from shop (to bank)       3,000 | 30 Apr  Closing balance 12,500  37,000
```

**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Part I, Ch 4 §4.1 and §4.1.4 (the cash book as both book of first entry and the cash and bank ledger; balanced like an account), and Ch 5 (cash is always a debit balance; the bank column may go credit). ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 7 Unit 1 §1.1, p. 7.3 (a cash flow statement is not mandatory for non-corporate entities). The Cash Book as a standard report: ruled 20 Oct 2026 (O-20-3, O-20-5).

**Example.** Professional: `Dr Cash ₹3,000 · Cr Mohan ₹3,000` on the receipts side; `Dr Bank ₹3,000 · Cr Cash ₹3,000` for a deposit. Plain words: "Mohan gave ₹3,000 in cash; later I put ₹3,000 into the bank."

### 5.5 Trial balance

The trial balance of rule 3.1 is a report of every book, at any date.

### 5.6 Statement of any account for any period

**Rule.** A statement of one account for the dates chosen has these parts, in this order:

1. the head (rule 4.1) with the account's name;
2. the **opening balance** on the first day, with its side;
3. every entry in date order, each with date, entry number, note, the other account, the two amount columns and the **running balance with its side**;
4. the **totals** of the two columns, of the period's entries only (the opening balance is not part of them);
5. the **closing balance** with its side.

For a person, the closing line reads *You will get ₹x* or *You will give ₹x*. The opening plus the movements equals the closing; nothing is left out of the period.

```
Mohan — 1 April 2026 to 30 June 2026
Opening balance                                         2,000 Dr
Date    No.  Note                    Dr         Cr      Balance
12 Apr  14   Cloth sold on credit    5,000              7,000 Dr
28 May  31   Received, cash                     3,000   4,000 Dr
Total for the period                 5,000      3,000
Closing balance — You will get                          4,000 Dr
```

**Source:** none settles it — ruled 20 Oct 2026 (O-20-6): no source read prescribes the content of an account extract; the form follows the ledger account (ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 2 Unit 2 §2.2, p. 2.53) and adds the running balance and notes, which are what the readers of these books use.

**Example.** Professional: opening `Dr ₹2,000`, sale `Dr ₹5,000`, receipt `Cr ₹3,000`, closing `Dr ₹4,000`. Plain words: "Mohan's page April to June: he owed ₹2,000, bought ₹5,000 more, paid ₹3,000, owes ₹4,000."

### 5.7 Who owes what, with ageing

**Rule.** A list of every person with a balance: name, the amount, whether *You will get* or *You will give*, and the balance placed in age bands counted from the day each amount arose: **up to 30 days**, **31–90 days**, **over 90 days**. Money received from or paid to a person settles the **oldest** amount first. The two sides are listed separately and totalled; the totals equal the sum of the persons' balances in the trial balance.

```
Who owes what — as on 31 March 2027
You will get
Person       Up to 30    31–90   Over 90      Total
Mohan           4,000        —         —      4,000
Gurdeep           —      6,000     2,500      8,500
Total           4,000    6,000     2,500     12,500
```

If Gurdeep paid ₹2,000 on 31 March, the ₹2,000 would first settle the oldest ₹2,500 of what he owed, leaving ₹500 over 90 days.

**Source:** none settles it — ruled 20 Oct 2026 (O-20-9): no ICAI pronouncement or NCERT text read has an ageing schedule (NCERT Class 11 Part II, Ch 9 §9.9 treats only a percentage provision for doubtful debts, and the ICAI BoS Foundation chapters read have none). The users of these books ask who owes what, and since when.

**Example.** Professional: no entry; the list reads the persons' accounts. Plain words: "Who owes me, and how long they have owed it. The oldest dues are the ones to ask about first."

### 5.8 Position: What I have, What I owe, Own money

**Rule.** The position is the book's balances at a date, in three parts:

- **What I have** (assets): *Cash in hand* (with petty cash); each bank account in credit; wallets; money *You will get* from persons; advances; retention held by others; things owned (at cost; rule 4.3); deposits (fixed and recurring) and deposits paid; money in a business the owner holds, at the money put in (*03*, 1.9; rule 4.3).
- **What I owe** (liabilities): money *You will give* to persons; a bank account overdrawn; credit cards; cash-credit and KCC loans; loans taken; deposits held.
- **Own money**: what remains after what is owed is taken from what is had. At the year end it equals the opening *Own money*, plus money put in during the year, plus the year's result (less a loss), less drawings.

What I have equals What I owe plus Own money. A trust or committee shows its funds in the place of Own money (rule 6.7). Money owed to one person and by the same person is shown as one balance on that person's account, not netted against another person's.

```
Position — {book} — as on 31 March 2027         This year    Last year
What I have
  Cash in hand                                     1,000        1,000
  Bank — SBI                                       5,000        3,500
  You will get (persons)                          15,500       12,500
  Things owned (at cost)                          15,000       15,000
  Total                                           36,500       32,000
What I owe
  You will give (persons)                         15,000       15,000
  Loan from Sunil                                  5,000        5,000
  Total                                           20,000       20,000
Own money                                         16,500       12,000
```

**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Part II, Ch 8 §8.6 and Summary item 3 (a balance sheet is a statement of assets and liabilities at a date, true only on that date; capital is the balancing figure); ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 7 Unit 1 §1.6, pp. 7.39–7.40 (no hard and fast rule on presentation). Plain headings and the three parts: ruled 20 Oct 2026 (O-20-3); negative own money by O-04-4.

**Example.** Professional (NCERT Ch 8): `Dr Profit and Loss ₹4,500 · Cr Capital ₹4,500` raises capital from ₹12,000 to ₹16,500 in the position. Plain words: "On 31 March the book had ₹36,500 and owed ₹20,000, so ₹16,500 of it is my own money."

### 5.9 The year's income and expenses

**Rule.** Every book shows its year's income heads and expense heads with a total for each, and the difference. The name and the result's name depend on the kind of book (section 6). Income and expense heads carry no opening balance and end the year at nothing (rule 2.1). The report may be asked for any period; for a part of the year it shows the heads for that part only. A thing that is not income or expense is never shown here: money borrowed or lent, money returned, a deposit given or coming back, and transfers between the book's own accounts.

The **year-end section** that follows a year close lists: the closing entries (rule 2.1) in the order made; any year-end adjustments made (rule 2.2); and the **carry-forward**, which lists every account that carries (rule 2.6), opening against closing, and states that income and expense heads start the next year at nothing.

**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Part II, Ch 8 §8.4 (the statement of income and expenses for the year); Part I Ch 1 §1.5.10 (profit as revenue less expenses, which loans and returned money are not). Carry-forward: NCERT Class 11 Part II, Ch 8 §8.6–§8.7. Ruled 20 Oct 2026 (O-20-3, O-13-5).

**Example.** Professional: `Dr Profit and Loss ₹4,500 · Cr Own money ₹4,500`. Plain words: "A year's page of what came in and what went out: ₹4,500 left over."

### 5.10 No cash-flow statement, and no statement of affairs except to open a book

**Rule.** No book produces a cash-flow statement. The Cash Book gives the year's movement of money for every book; for a trust or committee the Receipts & Payments statement does (rule 6.7). No book produces a statement of affairs after it has opened, because every book is kept in full double entry and so its position is always at hand (rule 5.8). A statement of affairs is used once, to open a book that has never had one: the person lists what they have and what they owe on the opening day and the difference is *Own money*, as set out in *01 — Accounting foundations*.

**Source:** ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 7 Unit 1 §1.1, p. 7.3 (a cash flow statement and a statement of changes in equity are not mandatory for non-corporate entities); ICAI Technical Guide on Financial Statements of Non-Corporate Entities (June 2022), Ch V and Appendix A Annexure 2 (AS 3 not applicable to Levels II–IV; the two places are worded differently). Statement of affairs: ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 9 §3, pp. 9.6–9.10 (a working statement for incomplete records, differing from a balance sheet). Ruled 20 Oct 2026 (O-20-5, O-20-8).

**Example.** Professional (BoS Ch. 9, the figures of Rakesh): cash ₹3,200, stock ₹34,800, debtors ₹31,000, machine ₹85,000 against creditors ₹12,000 and bank loan ₹10,000: `Dr Cash ₹3,200 · Dr Goods bought for sale ₹34,800 · Dr Debtors ₹31,000 · Dr Machine ₹85,000 · Cr Creditors ₹12,000 · Cr Bank loan ₹10,000 · Cr Capital ₹1,32,000`. The textbook's Stock goes to *Goods bought for sale*, and its Debtors and Creditors to one account per person (01, opening). Plain words: "On the first day I wrote down everything the shop owns and owes; the ₹1,32,000 between them is my own money in it."

## 6. Reports by kind of book

Every kind of book gives the reports of section 5. Each adds the following.

### 6.1 Household: Income & Expenses

**Rule.** The household's year is the report *Income & Expenses*. *Income* includes salary, interest, rent, *Income from {business}* and gifts received. It never includes loans taken, money returned, transfers between the household's own accounts, or a deposit coming back. Expenses are listed by head. The result is called **Saving** (income more than expenses) or **Spent more than came in** (expenses more than income). At year close the result goes to *Own money*.

```
Income & Expenses — {household} — 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027
Income                           Expenses
Salary           4,20,000        Groceries       1,10,000
Rent received      60,000        School fees       90,000
Total income     4,80,000        Other            2,40,000
                                 Total expenses   4,40,000
Saving                              40,000
```

**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Part I, Ch 1 §1.5.10 (profit as revenue less expenses; used here as the test of what is not income). The household's wording and statement: none settles it — ruled 20 Oct 2026 (O-13-5): no source covers a household; this follows the household's own words.

**Example.** Professional: `Dr Income and Expenses ₹40,000 · Cr Own money ₹40,000`. Plain words: "We earned ₹4,80,000 and spent ₹4,40,000; ₹40,000 is saved and added to our own money."

### 6.2 Business, farm, truck, landlord: Profit & Loss

**Rule.** A shop, contractor, farm, truck or landlord's business book has the year's report *Profit & Loss*: income heads (sales, work done, produce sold, freight, rent received) less expense heads, giving *Profit* or *Loss*. A household that lets property keeps *Income & Expenses* (rule 6.1; *03*, 6). When a book that buys goods for sale has not typed a closing stock, the note of rule 4.3 appears. The result goes at year close to *Own money* (a proprietor) or to the partners (a firm, rule 6.4). Drawings are not part of the result: they are closed to *Own money* and shown in the position's make-up of own money (rule 6.3).

**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Part II, Ch 8 §8.3 and Summary item 1 (Trading and Profit and Loss Account and Balance Sheet from the trial balance); ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 7 Unit 1 §1.1, pp. 7.2–7.3. NCERT Ch 8 §8.6.1 says there is no prescribed form of balance sheet for a proprietary or partnership firm, and gives no form of Profit & Loss either; ICAI's Technical Guide recommends one, and the layout here follows neither. Ruled 20 Oct 2026 (O-20-3).

**Example.** Professional (NCERT Ch 8, Ankit): income ₹1,30,000 less expenses ₹1,25,500 = profit ₹4,500: `Dr Profit and Loss ₹4,500 · Cr Own money ₹4,500`. Plain words: "A year's page of what came in and what went out: ₹4,500 left over, added to Ankit's own money."

### 6.3 Sole proprietor's own money

**Rule.** One *Own money* account and one *Drawings* account. The position shows *Own money* at opening, plus money put in during the year, plus profit (or less loss), less drawings, equal to closing. There is no separate current account.

**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Part II, Ch 8 §8.6.2 item (8) (drawings closed to capital). No current account: ruled 20 Oct 2026 (O-13-2) — the simplest form that gives the same result.

**Example.** Professional: opening `Cr ₹12,000`, profit `Cr ₹4,500`, drawings `Dr ₹36,000`; closing is `Dr ₹19,500`, that is own money of minus ₹19,500. Plain words: "Own money was ₹12,000; profit added ₹4,500; he took ₹36,000; he took out more than his own money, and the position says *Owes more than it owns.*"

### 6.4 Partnership: Profit & Loss, then P&L Appropriation

**Rule.** The firm's *Profit & Loss* gives the net profit. Interest on a partner's loan is an expense above the profit line. The profit is then carried to the credit of the **P&L Appropriation Account**, below the profit line, which is shown as:

```
P&L Appropriation — {firm} — year to 31 March 2027
Net profit brought from Profit & Loss                       3,00,000
Add: interest charged on partners' drawings                     1,750
Less: interest on capital (only if agreed)                     48,000
Less: partner's salary                                         60,000
Profit left to share                                        1,93,750
Shared in the ratio 3 : 2     Ramesh 1,16,250     Suresh 77,500
```

Interest on capital and partners' salary are allowed only out of profit. If the profit is less than their total, it is shared in proportion to the amounts due; in a loss none is allowed. Interest on capital is given only if the partners agreed it.

### 6.5 Partners' accounts

**Rule.** Each partner has a capital account and a current account, or a single account if capital is kept fluctuating. For each, the year shows: opening balance; capital put in; salary; interest on capital; withdrawals (with interest charged on them); share of profit or loss; closing balance. The closing balances of all partners together equal the firm's *Own money* in the position.

```
Ramesh's current account — year to 31 March 2027
Opening balance                              0
Interest on capital                     30,000
Share of profit                       1,16,250
Drawings                              (40,000)
Interest on drawings                   (1,000)
Closing balance — owed to Ramesh      1,05,250
```

**Source for rules 6.4 and 6.5:** NCERT Class 12 Accountancy Part I, Ch 1 §1.5, §1.5.1 (Journal Entries 1–6, Note and Fig. 1.3, pp. 8–9; Summary item 5, p. 35: the appropriation account is an extension of the profit and loss account; nothing is allowed in a loss); ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 10 Unit 1 §1.8, pp. 10.15–10.17 (salary and interest on capital are appropriations below the profit line; interest on a partner's loan is charged before it) and §1.9, p. 10.21 (interest on capital is paid only out of profit; if profit is less, profit is shared in the capital ratio); ICAI exposure draft of the Guidance Note on non-corporate entities, Notes 3a and 3b and General Instructions for the Balance Sheet note 6A (what each partner's account shows; PDF pp. 21, 52–53). Posted by the book at year close, below the profit line, in proportion if short: ruled 20 Oct 2026 (O-14-1, O-14-2, O-14-3). The exposure draft shows partners' remuneration above the profit line; NCERT and the BoS show it below it, and the BoS and NCERT are followed here. Interest on drawings placed in the withdrawals column: that mapping is a choice of this document, the draft does not say.

**Example.** Professional (NCERT-based figures, own amounts): Ramesh and Suresh share 3 : 2; net profit ₹3,00,000; `Dr Profit and Loss ₹3,00,000 · Cr P&L Appropriation ₹3,00,000`; then `Dr P&L Appropriation ₹1,93,750 · Cr Ramesh ₹1,16,250 · Cr Suresh ₹77,500`. Check: 48,000 + 60,000 + 1,93,750 − 1,750 = ₹3,00,000. Plain words: "The year's profit was ₹3,00,000. After interest on capital and Suresh's salary, ₹1,93,750 was left and was shared 3 to 2."

### 6.6 Joint family: the family summary

**Rule.** A joint family keeps its own account book and, beside it, the books of each member or business that belongs to it. The **family summary** is a report across them. It contains:

1. For each pair of related books, the **account between the two books**: both books' accounts side by side, each with its opening balance, every entry (date, number, note) and its closing balance. Two kinds of pair:
   - **Running-account pair** (the family and a sub-family; two people): the balances agree, on opposite sides. The last line is *Agrees*, or the entries found in only one book.
   - **Owner and the business it owns** (the family and its business): the entries agree one for one (money put in against the business's own money; *Income from {business}* against the business's drawings), but the balances do not. The family holds the business at the money put in, while the business's own money also carries its profit. The last line therefore prints the difference as *profit kept in the business*, or, where the business has paid out more than the profit, as *taken out beyond the profit* (*03*, 4.1).
2. The family book's own year statement, *Income & Expenses*, with the result *Saving* or *Spent more than came in* (rule 6.1), taken to the family's own money; it is printed with the family's book, and the summary does not repeat it. The family's income is only what comes home; a business's profit is worked out in the business's own book and **stated** in the summary, not counted again as the family's income.
3. Each business's result, stated beside the family's, for information.

```
Account between Family book and Sharma Textile — 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027
                            Family book's view               Sharma Textile's view
Opening                     Money in a business  1,00,000    Own money            1,00,000
Money put in                                       20,000                           20,000
Profit for the year                                     —                           31,000
Sent home                   Income from the business 6,000   Drawings              (6,000)
                            (income, not in the stake)
Entries agree one for one
Closing                     Money in a business  1,20,000    Own money, with profit 1,45,000
Profit kept in the business (1,45,000 − 1,20,000 = 31,000 − 6,000)                  25,000
```

A running-account pair is laid out the same way, with the closing line *Agrees*. Between two people the balances read *You will get* in one book and *You will give* in the other. Between the family and a sub-family the account is not a debt (*03*, 3.2): it sits within own money in both books and reads *against the household* or *in the household's favour* in the family's book, and *in the family's favour* or *against the family* in the sub-family's book.


The accounts of each pair carry the other book's name and are never called *Due to* or *Due from*.

**Source:** ICAI BoS Intermediate, Ch. 15 §6 (converse accounts of head office and branch — used here only by analogy; no source treats a joint family). Pair of accounts, statement and family income: none settles it — ruled 20 Oct 2026 (O-16-1 as amended, O-16-5, O-13-4, O-15-3, O-15-4, O-20-3): no source read describes a family summary, and counting a business's profit as the family's income as well as in its own book would count the same money twice.

**Example.** Professional: the family puts ₹20,000 into its shop: family book `Dr Sharma Textile (money in a business) ₹20,000 · Cr Cash ₹20,000`; business book `Dr Cash ₹20,000 · Cr Family book (own money) ₹20,000`. Plain words: "The family put ₹20,000 into the shop; it is the family's own money there, not a loan. Both pages show the same ₹20,000 entry. The shop's own money is bigger than what the family put in, because it also holds the profit kept in the business." Between two sub-families a ₹20,000 loan would read *You will get ₹20,000* in one book and *You will give ₹20,000* in the other, and agree.

### 6.7 Trust or committee: the three statements and a statement for each fund

**Rule.** A trust or committee book prints all three of these from the same entries, always together:

**Receipts & Payments** — a summary of the Cash Book. Receipts on the left, payments on the right, each grouped by head; it opens with the opening cash and each bank balance and ends with the closing ones. It has no date column. It includes capital receipts and payments as well as those of the year, so it cannot show a surplus or deficit.

**Income & Expenditure** — the year's income and the year's expenditure only, with the balance called **Surplus** (more came than was spent) or **Deficit**. It has no opening balance; capital receipts and capital payments are left out. The surplus or deficit is closed to the **General Fund** at the year end, which is the trust's *Own money*.

**Balance Sheet** — what the trust has and what it owes at a date, with the General Fund and each other fund on the side of the fund. Each fund is shown at its own balance and the funds are not netted against each other.

**A statement for each fund** — for each fund (for example a Hall Fund): the opening balance; the money received for it; the amounts spent from it; the interest added if the donor's terms say so; and the closing balance. Spending from a fund is posted in two steps entered as one action ("paid from the Hall Fund"): the amount is charged to Income & Expenditure, and the same amount is transferred from the fund. So every rupee spent is visible in Income & Expenditure and the fund's own surplus is nil. Where the donor's terms are silent, the interest on a fund's investment goes to that fund. When a thing owned is bought from a fund, it is carried at cost and the fund's balance up to the cost is transferred to the General Fund, since its purpose is met; if depreciation is opted in, the fund is instead released to income in step with the depreciation.

```
Fund statement — Hall Fund — year to 31 March 2027
Opening balance                             50,000
Received for the fund                       20,000
Interest added (donor's terms silent)        3,000
Spent from the fund (see I&E)              (18,000)
Closing balance                             55,000
```

Receipts & Payments alone is not the report of any trust or committee here, however small.

**Source:** ICAI BoS Foundation Paper 1 Accounting (Aug 2026 ed.), Ch. 8 §1, p. 8.2 (non-profit bodies prepare Receipts and Payments, Income and Expenditure and a Balance Sheet); §2.1, p. 8.4 (Receipts and Payments alone "is not favoured" except for simple activities with no assets but cash and no liabilities); §2–§4, pp. 8.3–8.11 (what each statement is; surplus carried to the capital fund). ICAI Technical Guide on Accounting by Not-for-Profit Organisations, paras 100–103 (the Income and Expenditure Account and Balance Sheet; integrated balance sheet, no set-off), 106(a), 110, 111; 2009 ed. ¶27 (a cash-basis body prepares a Receipts and Payments statement) and ¶32 (accrual recommended for all). The ICAI BoS virtual-coaching deck "9. Financial Statements of Not-for-Profit Organisations", Concepts 1–7, gives the same set (a faculty slide deck). Ruled 20 Oct 2026 (O-20-4, O-17-9, O-17-1, O-17-2, O-17-3, O-17-4): all three statements always, because the three come from the same entries and a small committee loses nothing; the ICAI Technical Guide (paras 107, 110) is followed over the BoS for spending from a fund. A thing owned bought from a fund: the fund moves to the General Fund, not to the *Capital Reserve* of the Technical Guide's para 110 — a departure, so the trust keeps one own-money account (owner ruling, 22 Oct 2026; *03*, 5.7).

**Example.** Professional: membership fees ₹40,000 received in cash: `Dr Cash ₹40,000 · Cr Membership fees ₹40,000`; on the receipts side of Receipts & Payments in full, and in Income & Expenditure in full too, since the book is kept on money received; only a book that opted into year-end adjustments counts just the part that belongs to the year. Year's surplus ₹8,000: `Dr Income and Expenditure ₹8,000 · Cr General Fund ₹8,000`. Plain words: "The cash page shows every rupee that came in; the income page counts it as this year's income (a book that opted into adjustments counts only the part that belongs to the year). We came out ₹8,000 ahead and added it to the General Fund."

## 7. Where the notes and the sources leave a question

These points are stated so that a bookkeeper can check them:

- **Formats.** No source requires the report head here (basis line, open-or-closed status, date made); AS 1 asks for policies to be stated in one place, and the Technical Guide's forms show name, date, unit and comparatives. The head is a ruling (rule 4.1).
- **Day Book, account statement, ageing, family summary.** None is a report named in the textbooks or ICAI material read; each rests on a ruling (rules 5.3, 5.6, 5.7, 6.6).
- **ICAI Guidance Note.** The Guidance Note (Aug 2023) text could not be read; its FAQ says it applies from accounting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2024, prescribes formats, and leaves not-for-profit bodies to ICAI's own guide (Q1–Q4, Q6–Q9); Q3 says it supersedes the June 2022 Technical Guide on non-corporate entities, which this document cites only for what its forms show. Nothing in this document claims to follow either.
- **Cash flow.** The ICAI Technical Guide words the small-entity position in two ways (Ch V "may not include … encouraged" and Annexure 2 "Not Applicable"); the BoS says "not mandatory". No cash-flow statement is made (rule 5.10).
- **Trust statements.** The ICAI Technical Guide names the Receipts and Payments statement for cash-basis bodies and recommends accrual for all; the BoS names all three. All three are produced (rule 6.7).
