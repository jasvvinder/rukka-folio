Visual guidelines Version 1.1 India · 9 scripts
01

What we are

Real double-entry underneath, a surface anyone can read on top. Encrypted so the books stay open to the people sharing the money and closed to everyone else — including us.

Legibility

Competitors' output is correct and unreadable. Ours is correct and obvious. The person keeping the books and the person checking them see the same page.

Directional trust

Not a privacy product, not a transparency product. Transparent in one direction, sealed in the other. Preserve that asymmetry in every piece of messaging.

02

Personality

Trustworthy · plain-spoken · quietly precise · warm. The product should feel like a well-kept physical ledger — solid, orderly, a little handsome.

We are

  • Orderly, like a ruled page
  • Direct about numbers and problems
  • Warm without being chatty
  • Respectful of the reader's competence

We are not

  • Playful, disruptive, "fintech"
  • Minimal to the point of cold
  • Gamified — no streaks, no confetti
  • Reassuring when we should be specific
03

The mark — the sealed ledger

A bound book, strap fastened by a wax seal, entries beside it. Two states with meaning: sealed is the brand mark everywhere — the promise that nobody has opened this. Open exists only in-product, after a successful unlock: the closure contracts and tucks left, a dog-ear folds in, the entries extend. Never decorative.

Sealed — the logo. Icon, favicon, lockups, print

Open — in-product, post-unlock. Dog-ear appears only here

Inverse — paper book on indigo, never white knockout

Circular crops and adaptive icons — inset at ~72%, never full-bleed

Size behaviour — one geometry, every size

Identical construction at every size; margins are 6 units on both sides in both states. Below 24px, entry strokes fall under one device pixel — optical thickening at asset-cutting time is a permitted production decision, not a redesign.

The open animation

Total ≈ 860ms at natural speed; use 0.6× (~520ms) for the biometric-unlock moment. Sealing is the exact reverse, the seal returning to full size last. Respect prefers-reduced-motion with a jump cut. Strap, seal and entries are CSS transforms; the fold is a path morph — build production in Rive or Lottie as one asset.

Always

  • Sealed state for icon, favicon, print, lockups
  • Open state only after a real unlock — it is earned
  • Clear space equal to the strap width, all sides
  • Mark height = cap height × 1.6 in lockup
  • Commission Devanagari and Tamil lockups alongside Latin

Never

  • Rotate, stretch, outline or add a gradient to the flat mark
  • Recolour outside the palette
  • Full-bleed into a circular crop — inset instead
  • Play the open animation decoratively
  • White knockout on dark — use the paper-book inverse

Three rules above the hexes

The primary is never a verdict. Indigo must never read as "good" or "correct". Credit and debit are visually distinct from both indigo and bahi red.

Colour never carries meaning alone. Every amount is also signed and column-positioned. Roughly 1 in 12 Indian men is red-green colour deficient; a ledger that fails them is a broken ledger.

The app is near-monochrome. Let the numbers carry the colour. Marketing may lean into indigo; the product should not.

Neutrals and dark mode

Neutrals are warm greys derived from paper — never blue-grey. Mixing a cool grey into this palette is the fastest way to make it look generic.

Dark mode is required, not optional. Most users open this app at night after closing shop. Invert to ink background with paper text; keep the credit and debit hues but lift their lightness for contrast.

Numerals

₹1,24,500.00

Tabular figures, always. Columns that don't align are the fastest way to look amateur in accounting software.

Indian grouping, always. ₹1,24,500.00 — not ₹124,500.00. Getting this wrong tells an Indian accountant in half a second that the software wasn't built for them.

Layout under translation

Assume Tamil and Malayalam run 30–40% longer than English. Design containers to that, not to English.

Never concatenate strings to build a sentence — word order differs across the nine languages.

Financial terms — GST, TDS, ledger, journal — stay in English in every locale. Users search for them in English and their CA uses them in English.

06

The ruled column

Every accounting artifact in India is defined by ruled lines and paired columns: the cloth bahi-khata, the columnar pad, the audit sheet. That is the whole shape language. If an element isn't a rule, a column, or a fold — cut it.

07

Voice

Short sentences. Verbs first. Sentence case everywhere. State the number — "₹500 unaccounted" beats "the entry is unbalanced" every time.

Banned: utilize, leverage, seamless, empower, simply, just, easy, effortless, revolutionise. No exclamation marks in system copy. No apology theatre — someone auditing a society wants to know what's wrong and where, not to be soothed.

Build order

1. Name clearance — IP India, classes 9/35/36/42
2. Wordmark, then Devanagari and Tamil lockups
3. Tokens as code, one source for site and both apps
4. The ledger table component
5. Mobile app — entry flow first
6. Web app — reports and reconciliation
7. Website
8. Pitch deck, from real screens

Don't skip step four

The ledger table is the hardest and most-used screen. Design it first and let it constrain everything else. Test it in Tamil at 14px on a five-inch phone before you approve it.

If the table works in nine languages at small sizes with correct tabular alignment, every other screen is downhill. If it doesn't, no amount of brand polish will save the product.
