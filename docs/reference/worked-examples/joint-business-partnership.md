**Settlement capacity:** the business can settle every partner balance today — bank ₹9,29,000 against ₹8,79,000 owed.

### How to read this
- **Profit is equal** (₹1,98,000 each) even though contributions were very unequal. Paying more costs earns a larger *claim for repayment*, never a larger share of profit.
- **The difference is already visible** in the last column: Amrit funded ₹68,332 more than her third; Harjit ₹51,666 less. Nothing further needs computing — the current-account balances are the answer.
- **Settlement options (02 §7.1):** the business pays out balances · Harjit pays Amrit directly and the claim transfers (`Dr Amrit · Cr Harjit`) · or the balances **carry forward** to FY 2027-28 as certified opening balances (the default).
- **If a partner only ever funds and never draws**, her balance simply grows across years, dated and certified at each year close. The optional **interest on capital** setting (02 §7.1, Phase 1, off by default, all-partner consent) is the classical remedy. On this example at 8% for the 122-day season it would credit Amrit ₹4,296 · Sukhdev ₹2,311 · Harjit ₹1,355 first (all rounded half-up to the paisa, 02 §7.1), and the remaining ₹5,86,038 would then split three ways — compensating funding without touching the sharing ratio.
- **Money the family takes "as needed" is not shown here.** Business surplus remitted to the common pool is an inter-book transfer; what each sub-family then takes from the pool is tracked in the pool's own book. Two separate fairness ledgers.

### Profit computation shown in full
Income ₹9,70,000 (crop ₹8,50,000 + milk ₹1,20,000) − expenses ₹3,76,000 (seed ₹1,80,000 + diesel ₹95,000 + labour ₹60,000 + repair ₹41,000) = **net profit ₹5,94,000** ÷ 3 = **₹1,98,000 each**, posted 31 Jul 2026 as one multi-line entry (voucher **G-008**): `Dr Profit Distributed ₹5,94,000 · Cr each Partner Current A/c ₹1,98,000`.
